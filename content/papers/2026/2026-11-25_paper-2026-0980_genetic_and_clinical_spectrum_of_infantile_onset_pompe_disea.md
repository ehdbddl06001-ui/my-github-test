## Title
Genetic and clinical spectrum of infantile-onset Pompe disease: A Vietnamese cohort study

## Authors
Dang Giang Thi Kim, Nguyen Khanh Ngoc, Do Mai Thi Thanh, Pham Duy Quang, Can Ngoc Thi Bich, Bui Thao Phuong, Bui Xuan Thi, Nguyen Huong Thi Mai, et al.

## Journal / DOI
PloS one · DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0363248 · PMID: 43077436
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077436/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Infantile-onset Pompe disease (IOPD) is the most severe form of Pompe disease, resulting from pathogenic GAA variants that cause lysosomal acid α-glucosidase deficiency. Although molecular diagnosis has improved substantially, comprehensive genotype-phenotype data from genetically confirmed IOPD cohorts in Southeast Asia remain limited. We aimed to characterize the clinical, biochemical, cardiac, and molecular spectrum of Vietnamese patients with genetically characterized IOPD and to explore genotype-phenotype associations according to predicted cross-reactive immunologic material (CRIM) status.

**METHODS:** This retrospective study included 81 Vietnamese patients with genetically characterized IOPD diagnosed between 2015 and 2025. We analyzed clinical, biochemical, cardiac, enzymatic, and molecular data at diagnosis. Genotype-phenotype associations were explored according to predicted CRIM status.

**RESULTS:** The median age at diagnosis was 4.53 months (range, 0.03-71.67 months). The most common clinical manifestations were muscle weakness or hypotonia (71.6%), pneumonia (59.3%), macroglossia (49.4%), and heart failure (38.3%). Genetic analysis identified 28 distinct GAA variants comprising 160 disease-associated alleles, including five previously unreported variants. The predominant variant was c.1933G > C (p.Asp645His), accounting for 46.3% of disease-associated alleles, followed by c.2040 + 1G > T (11.9%). Seven patients (8.6%) were predicted to be CRIM-negative. Among patients with available echocardiographic measurements, predicted CRIM-negative patients had a higher left ventricular mass index (LVMI) z-score than predicted CRIM-positive patients (median SDS, 9.90 vs. 6.05; p = 0.031).

**CONCLUSIONS:** This study reports the largest genetically characterized IOPD cohort from Vietnam to date and expands the clinical and molecular spectrum of IOPD by identifying five previously unreported variants in the GAA gene. The predominant variants were c.1933G > C and c.2040 + 1G > T. Predicted CRIM-negative status may be associated with more severe cardiac hypertrophy, as reflected by a higher LVMI z-score.

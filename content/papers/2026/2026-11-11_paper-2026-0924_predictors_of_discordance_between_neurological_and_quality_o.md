## Title
Predictors of Discordance Between Neurological and Quality-of-Life Recovery After Surgery for Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy: A Retrospective Cohort Study

## Authors
Bougadis Joshua, Alvi Mohammed Ali, Pedro Karlo M, Bidari Ava, Fehlings Michael G

## Journal / DOI
Spine · DOI: 10.1097/BRS.0000000000005924 · PMID: 43008133
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43008133/

## Abstract
**STUDY DESIGN:** Retrospective analysis of prospectively collected multicentre cohort data.

**OBJECTIVE:** Quantify discordance between neurological recovery (modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association [mJOA]) and quality-of-life (QoL) recovery (SF-36 PCS) after surgery for degenerative cervical myelopathy (DCM) and identify predictors of each profile.

**SUMMARY OF BACKGROUND DATA:** Neurological and patient-reported outcomes capture different dimensions of recovery after DCM surgery, but the frequency, structure, and baseline correlates of discordance between these outcome domains remain incompletely characterized.

**METHODS:** Retrospective analysis of prospective multicentre AO Spine CSM-NA and CSM-I cohorts. Recovery used published minimum clinically important differences (MCID) for mJOA and SF-36 PCS at 12 months, with 6-month values carried forward when unavailable. Patients were classified as concordant recovery, neurological-only recovery, QoL-only recovery, or no recovery. Discordant recovery was defined as achieving one MCID without the other. Baseline predictors were estimated by multinomial logistic regression using multiple imputation (m=20) for missing covariates.

**RESULTS:** Of 353 patients, 310 were classifiable: 88 (28.4%) concordant, 50 (16.1%) neurological-only, 82 (26.5%) QoL-only, 90 (29.0%) no recovery; 132 (42.6%) discordant. Higher baseline PCS predicted neurological-only versus concordant recovery (OR 4.42 per SD, 95% CI 2.34-8.35), whereas higher baseline mJOA predicted QoL-only (OR 5.67, 2.95-10.90) and no recovery (OR 4.23, 2.25-7.95; all P <0.001). Older age, higher Nurick grade, lower mental component score and greater frailty also predicted non-concordant profiles. In exploratory models, ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament predicted no recovery (OR 5.71, 2.17-15.0) and number of compressed levels predicted neurological-only recovery (OR 1.85, 1.17-2.92). Neurological-only patients improved less in pain-related disability (NDI Q1) than concordant or QoL-only patients (P <0.02).

**CONCLUSIONS:** Neurological and QoL recovery were discordant in over 40% of patients and governed by different baseline variables. Neurological-only recovery reflects limited room for measurable QoL gain, compounded by persistent pain, rather than surgical failure.

**LEVEL OF EVIDENCE:** III.

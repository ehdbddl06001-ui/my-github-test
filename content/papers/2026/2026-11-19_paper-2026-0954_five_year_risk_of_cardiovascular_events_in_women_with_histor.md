## Title
Five-year risk of cardiovascular events in women with history of pregnancy complicated by fetal growth restriction

## Authors
Anand K, Bucci T, Sudell M, Lip G Y H, Merriel A

## Journal / DOI
Ultrasound in obstetrics & gynecology : the official journal of the International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology · DOI: 10.1002/uog.70387 · PMID: 43050674
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43050674/

## Abstract
**OBJECTIVE:** To evaluate the association between fetal growth restriction (FGR) and adverse maternal cardiovascular outcomes within 5 years postpartum.

**METHODS:** This was a retrospective study that used a global federated health research network (TriNetX) in which diagnoses are coded using the International Classification of Diseases, tenth revision, Clinical Modification system (ICD-10-CM). Nulliparous women aged ≥ 18 years, without prior cardiovascular disease and with a singleton pregnancy delivered between April 2006 and December 2020, were categorized by the presence or absence of FGR (ICD-10-CM: O36.5). 1:1 propensity-score matching (PSM) was applied using two models: Model 1, matching for pre-existing cardiovascular risk factors; and Model 2, matching for both pre-existing and other pregnancy-related cardiovascular risk factors. Cox regression proportional hazard models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CI for a maternal composite cardiovascular outcome including heart failure, acute myocardial infarction and cerebral infarction/ischemic stroke within 5 years postpartum, and for each of these components individually.

**RESULTS:** After PSM, 84 863 women per group were analyzed in Model 1 and 84 861 per group in Model 2. FGR was associated with an increased risk of the composite cardiovascular outcome (Model 1: HR, 1.33 (95% CI, 1.16-1.52); Model 2: HR, 1.31 (95% CI, 1.15-1.50)), as well as with an increased risk of each of the components of the composite outcome individually: heart failure (Model 1: HR, 1.29 (95% CI, 1.08-1.54); Model 2: HR, 1.30 (95% CI, 1.09-1.56)), acute myocardial infarction (Model 1: HR, 1.55 (95% CI, 1.16-2.08); Model 2: HR, 1.53 (95% CI, 1.15-2.05)) and cerebral infarction/ischemic stroke (Model 1: HR, 1.38 (95% CI, 1.09-1.74); Model 2: HR, 1.33 (95% CI, 1.06-1.68)) within 5 years postpartum.

**CONCLUSIONS:** FGR in a first pregnancy is associated with increased maternal composite cardiovascular risk, including heart failure, acute myocardial infarction and cerebral infarction/ischemic stroke, within 5 years postpartum. These findings suggest that FGR may be a marker of early cardiovascular vulnerability. However, residual confounding, particularly by unmeasured timing or severity of pre-eclampsia, cannot be excluded. Also, FGR was identified using coding constructs based on electronic health records, which may include overlap with broader 'suspected poor growth' phenotypes such as small-for-gestational age, hence potentially diluting the observed associations. Earlier risk assessment and preventive strategies may help to reduce the risk of long-term morbidity and mortality in this population. © 2026 The Author(s).

## Title
Externally Validated Explainable Machine Learning for Identifying High Death Anxiety in Chronic Heart Failure: Multicentre Cross-Sectional Study

## Authors
Ma Yifan, Zhang Xuanbo, Shi Jing, Wan ZhiTong, Li Xinxin, Guo Chao, Zhang Longge, Tian Chengguang, et al.

## Journal / DOI
Journal of advanced nursing · DOI: 10.1111/jan.70843 · PMID: 43102904
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43102904/

## Abstract
**AIM:** To develop and externally validate explainable machine-learning models for identifying high death anxiety among patients with chronic heart failure.

**DESIGN:** A multicentre cross-sectional study with external validation.

**METHODS:** Hospitalised patients with chronic heart failure were recruited from four hospitals in China. The development cohort included 450 inpatients from three hospitals in Henan, Jilin, and Beijing, and the independent external validation cohort included 200 inpatients from a different tertiary hospital in Henan. Twenty-seven predictors were used to develop three machine-learning models. Discrimination, calibration, exploratory net benefit, and model explanations were assessed.

**RESULTS:** The primary extreme gradient boosting model showed strong external discrimination but imperfect calibration. Random forest had higher discrimination, and machine learning showed no clear discrimination advantage over logistic regression. Model explanations identified fear of disease progression, activities of daily living, hope, sleep duration, and self-esteem as the most influential predictors.

**CONCLUSION:** Explainable machine-learning models may support the identification of patients with chronic heart failure who have high death anxiety and may assist targeted psychological assessment.

**IMPLICATIONS FOR THE PROFESSION AND/OR PATIENT CARE:** Prospective evaluation should compare model-assisted assessment with direct measurement of death anxiety.

**IMPACT:** This study shows that multidomain information collected during patient assessment can be integrated using explainable machine learning to identify concurrent high Death Anxiety Scale scores. Such an approach may have clinical value when relevant information is already available, but it requires prospective comparison with direct Death Anxiety Scale assessment.

**REPORTING METHOD:** Reporting followed the Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis Or Diagnosis plus Artificial Intelligence guideline.

**PATIENT OR PUBLIC CONTRIBUTION:** This study did not include patient or public involvement in its design, conduct, analysis, interpretation, or reporting.

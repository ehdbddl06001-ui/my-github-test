## Title
Artificial intelligence for breast arterial calcification detection on mammography: A systematic review of diagnostic accuracy

## Authors
Campbell A Price, Petfield Leticia, Nemec Ii Eric C, Rose Suzanne J

## Journal / DOI
PLOS digital health · DOI: 10.1371/journal.pdig.0001829 · PMID: 43077485
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077485/

## Abstract
Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death globally and disproportionately affects women. Breast arterial calcifications (BAC) have emerged as a sex-specific predictor of cardiovascular disease. However, reporting is inconsistent due to the lack of standardized guidelines. Artificial intelligence (AI) has therefore gained attention as a tool to automate BAC detection. This systematic review examines the diagnostic performance of AI-based BAC detection compared with manual radiologist assessment in women undergoing mammography. A systematic review was conducted using MEDLINE, PubMed Central, ScienceDirect, Scopus, Academic Search Premier, IEEE Xplore, DOAJ, NCBI Bookshelf, MedRxiv, BioRxiv, and ClinicalTrials.gov (2015-2025), following PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) 2020 guidelines. Screening and data extraction were performed in Covidence by two independent reviewers. Risk of bias was assessed using the QUADAS-2 tool. The search yielded 84 articles, of which five met the inclusion criteria, representing 12,924 mammography cases. All studies compared AI-based BAC detection with radiologist-generated ground truth. AI systems demonstrated strong performance, with Area Under the Curve (AUC) values of 0.84-0.96, sensitivities of 0.696-0.87, specificities of 0.92-0.98, accuracies of 0.81-0.998, precisions of 0.683-0.89, and F1-scores of 0.665-0.87. Despite minor variability, outcomes were consistently high, showing that AI can augment the workflow of BAC detection during manual radiologist interpretation. Although larger, more diverse studies are needed, current evidence indicates that AI can accurately detect BAC on routine mammography and has the potential to enhance cardiovascular risk identification in women, thereby addressing a critical unmet need in preventive care.

## Title
[Atrial myxoma with endocarditis-like clinical presentation and neurological event]

## Authors

## Abstract
Cardiac myxomas are benign tumors that typically arise in the left atrium and account for 50% of all primary cardiac tumors. They can induce symptoms by obstructing blood flow, causing embolism, or producing general systemic symptoms. Cardiac myxomas derive from multipotent mesenchymal cells, which develop into a gelatinous mass. These tumors are usually solitary and pedunculated, often attached to the atrial septum via a stalk. Because cardiac myxomas pose a significant risk of life-threatening complications, such as embolism, heart failure, or sudden death, timely and effective care and treatment are essential. We present the case of a 16-year-old patient diagnosed with cardiac myxoma by histopathology. She presented clinically with cutaneous involvement consistent with systemic embolism secondary to suspected infective endocarditis. She was referred to a tertiary care center for diagnostic evaluation and to rule out rheumatological conditions such as lupus.

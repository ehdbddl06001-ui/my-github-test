## Title
Telerehabilitation-Based Group and Individual Exercise in People With Cystic Fibrosis: A Randomized Controlled Study

## Authors
Emirza Cilbir Cigdem, Kilinc Sakalli Ayse Ayzit, Tomlinson Owen William, Williams Craig Anthony, Kuran Aslan Goksen

## Journal / DOI
Pediatric pulmonology · DOI: 10.1002/ppul.71925 · PMID: 43042826
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43042826/

## Abstract
**AIM:** To compare the effects of telerehabilitation-based individual and group exercise, against routine treatment on exercise capacity, lung function, muscle strength, anaerobic power, balance, quality of life (QoL), and change in clinical status in people with cystic fibrosis (pwCF).

**METHODS:** Forty-five pwCF (8-18 years) were randomly allocated into three groups: (1) telerehabilitation-based group or (2) telerehabilitation-based individual exercise, and (3) control arm. Telerehabilitation-based groups underwent supervised exercise programmes 3 days/week via an online platform, and the control arm continued their routine care during an 8-week period. Exercise capacity of PwCF was assessed with 6-min walk test and incremental shuttle walk test. Spirometry measurements, muscle, and handgrip strength were recorded and evaluated. Anaerobic power via vertical jump test, balance via TimedUp and GoTest, and quality of life via Pediatric QoL Inventory were assessed as well. Adherence rates to exercise programmes were calculated. Change in clinical status for all groups, and motivation and enjoyment in the intervention groups were assessed.

**RESULTS:** Forty-one participants completed the study. Both exercise groups showed greater improvements than the control group in the 6-min walk distance, incremental shuttle walk test distance, the Timed Up and Go test, vertical jump performance, participant-reported QoL, and most upper- and lower-extremity muscle strength outcomes (all p < 0.05), with no significant differences between the two exercise groups. Significant group × time interactions were also observed for FEV1 and FEV1/FVC. Handgrip strength and parent-reported QoL did not differ between groups.

**CONCLUSION:** The current study demonstrates similar effects between tele-exercise sessions, with better outcomes than control group. Clinical teams can integrate telerehabilitation-based group exercise into CF centres.

**CLINICAL TRIAL NUMBER:** NCT05590169.

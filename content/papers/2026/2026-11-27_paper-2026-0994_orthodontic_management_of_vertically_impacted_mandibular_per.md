## Title
Orthodontic Management of Vertically Impacted Mandibular Permanent Second Molar in Adults: Case Report

## Authors
Mrisho Mashaka Hassan, Lingling Cui, Pallangyo Mary Paul, Li Jiaao, Galikano Deogratius David, Guo Xinwei, Zhang Yue, Xiao Si Long, et al.

## Journal / DOI
Interactive journal of medical research · DOI: 10.2196/101851 · PMID: 43097717

## Abstract
Impacted mandibular permanent second molars (MM2) are uncommon, with a reported prevalence of 1.8% to 3% among orthodontic populations, and vertically impacted MM2 are particularly infrequently reported. This case describes the orthodontic preservation of a vertically impacted tooth 47 in a 20-year-old Chinese male who presented with recurrent right-sided mandibular pain, gingival swelling, and bleeding associated with pericoronitis around tooth 48. Clinical examination revealed an Angle Class I molar relationship on the left and a Class II relationship on the right, a rightward mandibular midline deviation, 3 mm of anterior mandibular crowding, overeruption of teeth 17 and 27, and an unerupted tooth 47. Panoramic imaging showed vertically impacted teeth 18, 28, and 47, a horizontally impacted tooth 48, and mesioangular impaction of tooth 38. Cone beam computed tomography demonstrated the relationship between tooth 47 and adjacent structures and minimal resorption of the distal root of tooth 46, without obvious root dilaceration or clear ankylosis-related changes. Cephalometric findings included a skeletal Class I relationship, bimaxillary protrusion, proclined incisors, and a hypodivergent facial pattern. Following a discussion of extraction and implant replacement versus orthodontic retrieval, the patient selected preservation for psychosocial and economic reasons, with extraction planned if no movement occurred within 2 to 3 months. Treatment included fixed appliances, extraction of tooth 48, and surgical exposure of tooth 47. At month 6, with 0.017×0.025-inch stainless steel archwires in place, a customized 0.7-mm stainless steel loop with an approximately 10-mm distal extension was bonded to tooth 46. Elastic traction between this loop and the attachment on tooth 47 applied approximately 50 to 60 gf of extrusive force. Tooth 47 became visible at month 8, approximately half its crown was visible at month 11, and a buccal tube was then bonded for continued alignment. The tooth reached the occlusal level at month 13, and treatment was completed at month 14. Teeth 46 and 47 retained vital pulp responses, and no symptoms or apical pathologies were reported. Immediately after treatment, external root resorption of approximately 1 mm was observed on tooth 46; at 10 months, the corresponding radiolucent area was approximately 0.5 mm, with stable clinical findings and good oral hygiene. The outcome suggests that surgical exposure combined with tooth-borne orthodontic extrusion may be a feasible tooth-preserving option for carefully selected adults with vertically impacted mandibular second molars. Longer-term reports with standardized clinical and radiographic assessments are needed to define prognosis and broader applicability.

## Title
Antibiotic prescription patterns and determinants for influenza-like illness in selected tertiary hospitals in Bangladesh: A WHO AWaRe classification-based analysis

## Authors
Bhuiya Saju, Aleem Mohammad Abdul, Shuvo Tanzir Ahmed, Hassan Md Zakiul, Mamun Gazi Md Salahuddin, Khan Samin Yasar, Asadullah, Islam Md Aminul, et al.

## Journal / DOI
PLOS global public health · DOI: 10.1371/journal.pgph.0007648 · PMID: 43063190
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43063190/

## Abstract
Antibiotics are frequently prescribed for patients with influenza-like illness (ILI), despite their ineffectiveness against viral infections. This study aimed to assess the patterns, trends and factors associated with antibiotic prescriptions for patients with ILI based on the World Health Organization (WHO) AWaRe classification in Bangladesh. This cross-sectional study conducted secondary analysis of data collected through hospital-based influenza surveillance between January 2011 and December 2017, from outpatients who met the WHO ILI case definition. Descriptive statistics assessed prescribing patterns, the Cochran-Armitage test assessed temporal trends, and multivariable logistic regression identified determinants of antibiotic prescriptions. Among 7,041 enrolled patients with ILI [median age: 4 years (IQR: 1.17-20 years), 55.6% male], 84.3% (5,939) received at least one antibiotic. Of them (5,939), 98.3% received a single antibiotic agent, 1.7% received two agents, and 0.03% received more than three agents. The most prescribed antibiotics (N = 6,045) were azithromycin (42.2%), amoxicillin (26.2%), and cefixime (14.9%). Azithromycin (p = 0.015) and cefixime (p = 0.044) increased significantly, while amoxicillin (p = 0.019) and ciprofloxacin (p = 0.039) declined significantly. According to the AWaRe classification, 69% of prescribed antibiotics were from the Watch group, while 31% were from the Access group, and none were from the Reserve group. The prescriptions of Access group declined (p = 0.029), whereas the Watch group increased significantly (p = 0.029). Antibiotic prescriptions were more common among patients aged <5 years (aOR: 1.45; 95% CI: 1.25-1.68), those treated at government hospitals (aOR: 3.76; 95% CI: 3.26-4.35), and those reported history of runny nose (aOR: 1.91; 95% CI: 1.62-2.56) or sore throat (aOR: 1.93; 95% CI: 1.48-2.51). A substantial proportion of patients with ILI received antibiotics, with a notable increase in Watch group antibiotics. These findings underscore the need to strengthen hospital-based antimicrobial stewardship programmes in Bangladesh to promote appropriate antibiotic prescribing and address antimicrobial resistance.

## Title
Effect of a dietary behaviour change education intervention on iron status and linear growth in children aged 6 to 59 months: a cluster-randomised controlled trial in rural Ethiopia

## Authors
Abebe Ferede, Abera Muluemebet, Belachew Tefera

## Journal / DOI
Primary health care research & development · DOI: 10.1017/S1463423626101960 · PMID: 42992423
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42992423/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Food insecurity and limited maternal nutrition knowledge contribute to childhood iron deficiency anaemia (IDA) and stunting. This study evaluated community-based Dietary Intake Behaviour Education (DIBE) for improving iron status and linear growth among children aged 6-59 months in rural central Ethiopia.

**METHODS:** A cluster-randomized controlled trial with 1:1 allocation was conducted across 16 community units from April 2021 to January 2023, enrolling 1,012 mother-child pairs. Structured assessments collected data on nutritional status, maternal nutrition knowledge, feeding practices, and related factors. IDA was defined as a haemoglobin concentration below 110 g/L, while stunting was defined as a height-for-age Z-score (HAZ) below -2 SD from the WHO child growth standards. Independent-samples t-tests and generalized estimating equations assessed intervention effects and associated factors.

**RESULTS:** Baseline IDA prevalence was 18.2%, higher in the intervention group (22.13%) than the control group (14.2%). At endline, IDA prevalence decreased to 6.5% in the intervention group but increased to 20.4% in controls. Baseline stunting prevalence was comparable between intervention (41.5%) and control (40.7%) groups. At endline, stunting decreased to 15.0% in the intervention group but increased to 51.8% in controls. DIBE was positively associated with HAZ (B = 0.744, p = 0.003). Maternal food-preparation practices (B = 0.242, p = 0.020) and IDA awareness (B = 0.237) were also positively associated with HAZ.

**CONCLUSIONS:** Community-based DIBE improved linear growth and coincided with reductions in IDA and stunting. Integrating practical nutrition education into routine child health services may improve nutritional outcomes in resource-limited settings.Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov:NCT04846062.Registered: 15 April 2021, View at: https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT04846062.

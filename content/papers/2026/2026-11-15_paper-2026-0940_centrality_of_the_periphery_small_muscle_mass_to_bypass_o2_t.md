## Title
Centrality of the periphery: Small muscle mass to bypass O2 transport limitations and maximize the benefits of exercise training in heart failure

## Authors
Iannetta Danilo, Laginestra Fabio Giuseppe, Broxterman Ryan M, Simonelli Carla, Amann Markus, Paneroni Mara

## Journal / DOI
Experimental physiology · DOI: 10.1113/EP093476 · PMID: 43035010
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43035010/

## Abstract
Heart failure (HF) is a complex, systemic cardiovascular condition characterized by a severe impairment in O2 transport to skeletal muscle that drives exercise intolerance and reduces quality of life. Although exercise training (ET) is a cornerstone of HF clinical management, patients often exhibit limited improvements in peak oxygen uptake (  V ̇ O 2 peak   ) following traditional whole-body, aerobic exercise recruiting a large muscle mass (e.g., cycling). In patients with HF, adaptations within skeletal muscle (e.g., capillarization) exert a proportionally greater influence on changes in  V ̇ O 2 peak   than adaptations in cardiac performance. In this review, we discuss the mechanisms by which traditional ET prescription may be suboptimal to elicit peripheral adaptations and argue that dynamic exercise recruiting only a small muscle mass (e.g., quadriceps) may be more effective in achieving this goal. Indeed, small muscle mass exercise reduces the cardiovascular restraints imposed by the failing heart, allowing the muscle to reach much greater metabolic rates and to be exposed to much larger degrees of homeostatic perturbations, a key factor triggering adaptations in O2 transport and utilization at the peripheral level. The intent of this review is to discuss the influence of muscle mass on exercise tolerance and adaptations to ET in HF, with the ultimate goal of promoting the integration of small muscle mass exercise into contemporary cardiac rehabilitation programmes.

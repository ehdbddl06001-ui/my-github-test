## Title
Safety, Signal Quality, and Patient Satisfaction With Insertable Loop Recorder Implantation by Specialized Electrophysiology Nurses: Prospective Observational Study

## Authors
Lillo Rodenas Isabel, Reig Gómez Helena, Ajo Ferrer Raquel, Carrillo Molina Ángela María, Ajo Ferrer María, Ghodbane Kiamouche Sofiane, Martínez Martínez Juan Gabriel

## Journal / DOI
JMIR nursing · DOI: 10.2196/106175 · PMID: 43063362
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43063362/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Insertable loop recorders (ILRs) are subcutaneous cardiac monitoring devices used in the diagnosis of arrhythmias and in the etiological investigation of syncope and cryptogenic stroke. Device miniaturization and procedural simplification have facilitated implantation in ambulatory settings. The involvement of specialized electrophysiology nurses may help meet rising clinical demand, although evidence in our setting remains limited.

**OBJECTIVE:** This study aimed to analyze implantation safety, detection signal quality, adverse events during follow-up, and patient satisfaction when the procedure was performed by specialized nurses.

**METHODS:** This prospective observational study (the CARDIONURSE study) was conducted in the Arrhythmia Unit of Hospital General Universitario Doctor Balmis between February 2022 and May 2023. Thirty-five patients with a clinical indication for ILR implantation were included. The procedure was performed by 2 specialized nurses under medical supervision. Sociodemographic, clinical, and technical implantation variables were collected, including detection signal amplitude (mV), together with adverse events and follow-up outcomes at 1 and 6 months. Patient satisfaction was assessed using a study-specific questionnaire at 4 time points. Descriptive statistical analysis was performed.

**RESULTS:** Mean age was 71.1 (SD 11.3) years, and 62.9% (22/35) were women. The most frequent indication was syncope (27/35, 77.1%), followed by suspected atrial fibrillation (AF) in cryptogenic stroke (6/35, 17.1%). No objective procedural complications occurred (35/35, 100% of procedures); mild, transient procedural pain was reported in 5.7% (2/35) of patients, and the procedure lasted less than 5 minutes in 74.3% (26/35) of procedures. Mean signal amplitude was 0.45 (SD 0.29) mV, with a visible P wave in 94.3% (33/35) of patients. No wound-related adverse events were recorded among the 85.7% (30/35) of patients with available follow-up data at 6 months; data were unavailable for the remaining 5 patients. A diagnosis was obtained in 20% (6/30) of patients with an available diagnostic outcome at 6 months. Overall, 97.1% (34/35) of patients reported maximum or very high satisfaction with the intervention, and 100% (35/35) with the care received.

**CONCLUSIONS:** ILR implantation by specialized electrophysiology nurses in an ambulatory setting is a safe, fast, and technically adequate procedure, with adequate signal quality and high patient satisfaction. These findings support the feasibility of specialized nursing as ILR implanters in similar ambulatory care settings, although confirmation in larger, multicenter studies is warranted before broader implementation.

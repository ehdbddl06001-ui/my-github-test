## Title
Serum procalcitonin in the assessment of febrile neutropenia in hematological patients

## Authors
Juutilainen Auni, Jantunen Esa, Pulkki Kari, Hämäläinen Sari

## Journal / DOI
Clinical chemistry and laboratory medicine · DOI: 10.1515/cclm-2026-1502 · PMID: 43077626
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077626/

## Abstract
This review focuses on recent literature of the use of procalcitonin (PCT) to assess infection severity and prognosis in hematological patients with chemotherapy-induced febrile neutropenia (FN). A substantial proportion of patients with hematological malignancies receive intensive chemotherapy, which often leads to neutropenia, associated with infectious complications, and prolonged use of antibiotics. Antimicrobial resistance is a growing problem with far-reaching consequences, and hematological patients undergoing chemotherapy are at a particularly high risk. Observational studies indicate that PCT concentration 0.1-0.5 μg/L suggests localized infection; systemic infection is unlikely when PCT <0.1 μg/L, and likely when PCT ≥2 μg/L. In sepsis studies utilizing PCT-guided therapy, the threshold values used at the initiation of treatment range from 0.10 to 0.5 μg/L. The commonly used criteria for discontinuation include 50 % reduction from the previous value, 65 % decrease within three days, or ≥80-90 % decrease from the peak value. Of note, these cut-offs are based on early clinical studies and do not consider variable analysis methods available. When these thresholds are applied to sepsis patients, the duration of treatment is typically reduced by approximately two days. Some studies have reported reduction in antibiotic-related adverse effects and antimicrobial resistance, and even a decrease in mortality. Further experience and data are needed regarding PCT-guided management in hematological patients with chemotherapy-induced FN. Findings of observational studies largely support the utility of PCT measurements in chemotherapy-induced FN, but there is a paucity of interventional studies evaluating the use of PCT algorithms in FN patients and the results are inconsistent.

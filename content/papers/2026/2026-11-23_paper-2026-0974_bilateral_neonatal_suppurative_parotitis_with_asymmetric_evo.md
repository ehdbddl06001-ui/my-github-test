## Title
Bilateral Neonatal Suppurative Parotitis With Asymmetric Evolution in an Extremely Preterm Infant: A Case Report

## Authors
Farhat Sarwat, Al Barazi Randa, Ghadieh Justin, Maalouf Faouzi I

## Journal / DOI
Cureus · DOI: 10.7759/cureus.118389 · PMID: 43068217
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43068217/

## Abstract
Neonatal suppurative parotitis (NSP) is a rare bacterial infection, usually unilateral and most often caused by Staphylococcus aureus. Bilateral involvement is distinctly uncommon, particularly in extremely preterm infants. A female infant born at 26 weeks and six days' gestation (birth weight 1040 g) developed culture-negative late-onset sepsis-like deterioration on day of life (DOL) 13. On DOL 16, bilateral preauricular and submandibular swelling with erythema was noted. Ultrasonography demonstrated bilateral salivary-gland enlargement and hypervascularity without a discrete abscess but could not confidently distinguish parotid from submandibular involvement. By DOL 18, the right preauricular region had spontaneously drained through the skin, while examination of the left side showed induration, warmth, fluctuance, and frank purulence expressed from Stensen's duct. Culture of the purulent drainage grew methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus (MSSA). The infant was treated with intravenous antibiotics, subsequently narrowed to cefazolin, together with warm compresses and gentle gland massage. Bilateral swelling resolved completely by DOL 24 without operative drainage. This case illustrates synchronous bilateral NSP with strikingly asymmetric evolution: active ductal suppuration on one side and spontaneous cutaneous fistulization with decompression on the other. In a deteriorating extremely preterm neonate, direct inspection of Stensen's duct can be decisive when ultrasonography does not clearly localize the involved salivary gland.

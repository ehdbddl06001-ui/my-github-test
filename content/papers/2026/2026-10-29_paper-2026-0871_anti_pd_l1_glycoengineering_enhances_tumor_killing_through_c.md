## Title
Anti-PD-L1 glycoengineering enhances tumor killing through coordinated interactions between PD-L1⁺ DCs and CD16A⁺ cytotoxic lymphocytes

## Authors
Blomberg Anne Louise, Tian Weihua, Agullet Judit Pina, Madsen Natasha Helleberg, Linde Katrine Grove, Psaradaki Christina, Skov Søren, Skovbakke Sarah Line, et al.

## Journal / DOI
Cancer research communications · DOI: 10.1158/2767-9764.CRC-26-0696 · PMID: 42956317
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42956317/

## Abstract
Blockade of the PD-1/PD-L1 axis has transformed cancer immunotherapy, yet many patients fail to achieve durable responses. Although Fc glycoengineering by defucosylation is well established to enhance NK cell-mediated antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity through increased FcγRIIIa binding, its effects on other FcγRIIIa-expressing immune cells remain poorly understood. Using complementary human in vitro dendritic cell-lymphocyte coculture models, we compared non-glycosylated, wild-type, and defucosylated anti-PD-L1 antibody variants. Defucosylation enhanced FcγRIIIa binding and increased the relative frequency and activation of cytotoxic lymphocytes, particularly CD3⁺CD56⁺ NKT-like cells. Our findings identify a previously unrecognized mechanism whereby coordinated engagement of PD-L1 on dendritic cells and FcγRIIIa on lymphocytes alters cytotoxic lymphocyte composition. The changes in cytotoxic lymphocyte composition and activation induced by defucosylated anti-PD-L1 were associated with enhanced tumor killing in both human 2D and 3D cancer models, supporting Fc glycoengineering as a potential strategy to improve cancer immunotherapy.

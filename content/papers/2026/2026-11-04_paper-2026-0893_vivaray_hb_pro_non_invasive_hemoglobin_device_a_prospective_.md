## Title
Vivaray Hb pro non-invasive hemoglobin device: A prospective diagnostic accuracy study measuring agreement with a calibrated blood cell counter

## Authors
Kumar Devesh, Kapoor Sunita, Gowda Ambanna, Gupta Dipti, Mittal Harsh, Sood Suneet

## Journal / DOI
PloS one · DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0353640 · PMID: 42976121
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42976121/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Non-invasive hemoglobin measurement offers a painless and rapid alternative to conventional blood-based testing. The Vivaray Hb pro is a handheld photoplethysmography-based device designed for point-of-care hemoglobin assessment without blood sampling. We evaluated the clinical performance of the Vivaray Hb pro by comparing device-generated hemoglobin values with those obtained from a calibrated laboratory blood cell counter.

**METHODS:** In this cross-sectional, non-randomized clinical performance study, participants aged ≥8 years were prospectively recruited. Hemoglobin was measured non-invasively using the Vivaray Hb pro and compared with venous blood samples analyzed on a calibrated Coulter counter. Agreement between methods was assessed using Bland-Altman analysis, including regression-based evaluation for proportional bias. Mean difference, mean absolute error (MAE) and proportions of measurements within tolerance limits were also calculated. Complete paired measurements were available for 763 individuals.

**RESULTS:** Bland-Altman analysis returned a mean difference of -0.40 g/dL. The bias was hemoglobin-dependent, being higher than the true value at low hemoglobin levels and underestimation at higher levels. Regression-based analysis showed proportional bias (β1 = -0.178), indicating decreasing difference with increasing hemoglobin concentration. The MAE was 1.5 g/dL but was lower (1.2) in the clinically predominant ranges of 8.1-13 g/dL.

**CONCLUSION:** The Vivaray Hb pro can function as a useful non-invsive screening and point-of-care assessment device for measuring hemoglobin, particularly in settings where rapid, painless and repeatable measurements are desirable.

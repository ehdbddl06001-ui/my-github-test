## Title
Interaction between sex, filaggrin, and early food and skin interventions in atopic dermatitis and allergic sensitization development; a PreventADALL study in children up to 3 years

## Authors
Tan Bowen, Rehbinder Eva Maria, Hoyer Angela, Lødrup Carlsen Karin C, Granum Berit, Haugen Guttorm, Hedlin Gunilla, Jonassen Christine Monceyron, et al.

## Journal / DOI
Pediatric allergy and immunology : official publication of the European Society of Pediatric Allergy and Immunology · DOI: 10.1111/pai.70549 · PMID: 43077225
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077225/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Early food introduction reduced food allergy at 3 years, whereas early use of skin emollients did not affect atopic dermatitis (AD) in the PreventADALL study.

**OBJECTIVE:** To determine if sex or Filaggrin (FLG) mutation modifies the effect of early skin or food intervention on AD or allergic sensitization by 3 years of age.

**METHODS:** From the PreventADALL trial we included 2394 mother-child pairs. Infants were randomized to one of four intervention groups (no intervention, food intervention by introducing peanut, milk, wheat and egg from 3 to 4 months, skin intervention with regular skin emollients from 0.5 to 9 months, or both). Clinical presence of AD, AD severity and allergic sensitization were assessed at 1 year and 3 years. Interaction terms and stratified estimates were analyzed.

**RESULTS:** At 1 year, sex-specific interaction effects were found for the presence of AD with combined intervention (Adjusted pint = 0.004); the stratified analysis showed that the combined intervention was protective in boys (OR 0.16; 95% CI 0.05-0.41) but not in girls. Sex-intervention interactions were also observed for AD severity at 1 year (Adjusted pint = 0.003 for combined intervention) and 3 years (Adjusted pint = 0.01 for the skin, food intervention; and adjusted pint = 0.03 for the combined intervention). No interaction between FLG mutation and interventions was observed. No sex- or FLG-related interaction was found for allergic sensitization.

**CONCLUSION:** Sex may be a modifier in the effect of early interventions on AD, while FLG mutation status seems not. These findings suggest that sex-specific biological differences may modulate the response to early-life AD prevention.

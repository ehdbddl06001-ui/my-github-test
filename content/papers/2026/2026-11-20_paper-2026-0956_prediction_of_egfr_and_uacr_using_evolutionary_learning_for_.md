## Title
Prediction of eGFR and uACR Using Evolutionary Learning for Identifying High-Risk Individuals in Renal Disease Prevention

## Authors
Hu Yao-Feng, Yen Chia-Heng, Lin Yen-Cheng, Chen Ting-Yu, Ho Shinn-Ying

## Journal / DOI
Journal of clinical laboratory analysis · DOI: 10.1002/jcla.70371 · PMID: 43056505
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43056505/

## Abstract
**OBJECTIVES:** Many individuals at high risk for renal disease remain undetected in clinical practice due to insufficient testing of estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR). This study aims to develop models to predict reduced eGFR and elevated uACR, thereby facilitating early identification of individuals at risk.

**METHODS:** From 72,346 analyzed samples, we derived a dataset containing 15 features (U15). We developed three evolutionary learning-based models: PeGFR, PuACR1, and PuACR2, to predict eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, uACR ≥ 30 mg/g, and uACR > 300 mg/g, respectively. These models were built using an intelligent bi-objective combinatorial genetic algorithm (IBCGA) and SVM parameter settings optimized simultaneously. External dataset without eGFR and uACR measurements to evaluate clinical applicability.

**RESULTS:** In the U15 training and test datasets for uACR prediction, gender, pH, and bilirubin were not statistically significant; whereas all features were significant in eGFR prediction (p < 0.05). The areas under the curve (AUC) for PeGFR, PuACR1, and PuACR2 were 0.846, 0.827, and 0.949, respectively. In the external test, 589 individuals (11.36%) were identified as high- or very-high-risk, defined as having eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 and uACR ≥ 30 mg/g.

**CONCLUSIONS:** The evolutionary learning framework proposed accurately predicts renal risk using clinical data as an early screening tool. It also demonstrates scalability in assisting healthcare providers in determining whether further renal-related examinations are warranted, thereby facilitating the identification of high-risk individuals for secondary prevention. An online tool has also been developed for clinical and research use: https://nycuiclab.ngrok.io.

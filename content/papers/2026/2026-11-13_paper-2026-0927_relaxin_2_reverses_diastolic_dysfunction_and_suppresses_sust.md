## Title
Relaxin-2 reverses diastolic dysfunction and suppresses sustained arrhythmias in a rat model of Heart Failure with preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF)

## Authors
Palma Joshua B, Gabris-Weber Bethann, McMahon Brenda, Mauro Anthony J, St Hilaire Cynthia, Cuevas Rolando A, Dschietzig Thomas B, Romero Guillermo, et al.

## Journal / DOI
PloS one · DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0350373 · PMID: 43019361
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43019361/

## Abstract
**AIMS:** Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) afflicts millions annually and current treatments provide symptomatic relief. The goal is to test the effects of synthetic human Relaxin-2 (RLX) on diastolic dysfunction (DD) and arrhythmia vulnerability.

**METHODS AND RESULTS:** Male ZSF1 rats were placed on a normal diet (ND, N = 8 controls) or a high-fat diet (HFD, N = 11), resulting in the development of DD in 11-weeks, based on serial echocardiograms (enlarged left atrium (LA), wall thickness, doppler flow: E/e'). Once HFpEF was confirmed, ND and HFpEF rats were randomly treated with Relaxin (400 µg/kg/day RLX, N = 6) or the vehicle (N = 5) for 2-weeks using implanted minipumps. Echocardiograms were repeated for 2 more weeks, then hearts were isolated, optically mapped, subjected to programmed electrical stimulation (PES) and tissues dissected for immuno-fluorescence (IF), and qPCR analysis. Circulating levels of glucose, RLX and NT-pro-ANP were measured, pre- and post-treatment. Echocardiograms indicated that RLX reversed DD by reducing LA dimensions and E/e'. Optical mapping showed that vehicle-treated, ND rats (N = 3) had non-sustained atrial (1/3), and ventricular (3/3) arrhythmias and RLX-treatment reduced them to 0/3 and 1/3, respectively. Vehicle-treated, HFD rats had non-sustained (2/5) and sustained (2/5) atrial and ventricular arrhythmias (N = 5). RLX-treatment (N = 6) suppressed sustained atrial and ventricular arrhythmias (0/6) but not non-sustained atrial (2/6) and ventricular (3/6) arrhythmias. RLX tended to increase conduction velocity (CV), particularly at short cycle lengths consistent with increased Nav1.5, Connexin-43, β-catenin and Wnt1 expression, based on IF. There were no significant changes in fibrosis in this HFpEF model. NT-pro-ANP was elevated in HFpEF and reduced towards control values by RLX. qPCR analysis showed that RLX decreased Dickkopf (DKK1) and Matrix Metallopeptidase 1a (MMP1A) genes and increased SCN5A expression compared to vehicle (N = 6 and 5, respectively).

**CONCLUSIONS:** The ZSF1 model showed indications of HFpEF, including DD, enlargement of the LA, hemodynamic stress, increased vulnerability to sustained AF and VF, and elevated glucose and blood pressure. RLX treatment largely reversed DD, hemodynamic stress, and suppressed sustained arrhythmias. RLX elicited cardiac genomic changes, most likely through Wnt/canonical signaling, demonstrating RLX's potential as a therapy for HFpEF.

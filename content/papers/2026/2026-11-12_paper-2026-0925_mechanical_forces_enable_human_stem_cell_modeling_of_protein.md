## Title
Mechanical forces enable human stem cell modeling of protein aggregation cardiomyopathy

## Authors
Kooh Yasaman Kargar Gaz, Bahmani Bahareh, Zhao Chen, Malayath Ganesh, Khokhlova Anastasia, Hayem Leah, Umuragwa Hertier, Jin Hanxun, et al.

## Journal / DOI
Science advances · DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aee9464 · PMID: 43013638
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43013638/

## Abstract
Inherited cardiomyopathies are the leading cause of heart failure in young adults, with limited therapeutic options. Desmin-related cardiomyopathy (DRM), caused by mutations to cytoskeletal proteins and their chaperones, exemplifies this challenge: Here, protein quality control is compromised, leading to toxic protein aggregates that destroy cardiomyocyte contractile machinery. One of the deadliest DRM mutations in the chaperone protein αB crystallin (CRYAB-R120G) lacks a genetically matched preclinical model: Mouse models require massive overexpression of mutant CRYAB, raising questions about physiological relevance. Here, we establish a human model of CRYAB-linked DRM, using induced pluripotent stem cells expressing CRYAB-R120G at near-endogenous levels. We show that three-dimensional mechanical loading, but not fatty acid-based metabolic maturation media, is essential for expressing Desmin, whose aggregation defines DRM. Using microengineered heart tissues, we found that contractile deficits are associated with impaired calcium handling, pointing to electromechanical uncoupling in DRM. Treatment with the Janus kinase 1 inhibitor itacitinib partially rescued contractility, providing a functional validation of this emerging therapeutic strategy.

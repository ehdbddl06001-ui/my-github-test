## Title
Artificial intelligence EEG biomarkers for schizophrenia diagnosis: Systematic review with PROBAST+AI assessment

## Authors
Łoś Kacper, Waszkiewicz Napoleon

## Journal / DOI
European psychiatry : the journal of the Association of European Psychiatrists · DOI: 10.1192/j.eurpsy.2026.12262 · PMID: 42878218
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42878218/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** The application of artificial intelligence (AI) to resting-state electroencephalography (EEG) data represents a burgeoning frontier in schizophrenia diagnostics. However, the inferential validity of findings is frequently compromised by methodological heterogeneity. This systematic review rigorously evaluates the diagnostic efficacy and methodological robustness of AI-generated EEG biomarkers by implementing the Prediction model Risk Of Bias Assessment Tool for Artificial Intelligence (PROBAST+AI).

**METHODS:** Following PRISMA guidelines, a systematic search of PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science identified 43 primary studies encompassing 3,420 patients with schizophrenia or first-episode psychosis and 3,164 healthy controls (total N = 6,584). Methodological quality and validation strategies were assessed using the 34 signaling questions of the PROBAST+AI framework.

**RESULTS:** The aggregate mean diagnostic accuracy across the literature was 93.38%, with deep learning architectures demonstrating superior metrics relative to classical machine learning (96.83% vs. 87.68%). Critically, external validation was restricted to 4.7% of the included studies. A pronounced performance discrepancy was observed between subject-wise (88.22%) and segment-wise (98.14%) validation protocols, indicating pervasive data leakage. Overall, 76.7% of the analyzed studies were characterized by a high risk of bias, predominantly stemming from suboptimal data partitioning and inadequate independent verification.

**CONCLUSIONS:** Contemporary diagnostic accuracies appear to be artifactually elevated due to structural methodological deficiencies and data leakage. Consequently, current evidence does not support the immediate clinical implementation of AI-EEG biomarkers in schizophrenia. To facilitate translational progress, we propose a standardized methodological framework optimizing validation strategies.

## Title
Dermoscopic Features of Lichen Sclerosus ET Atrophicus, Morphea, and Overlap: A Study of 50 Patients in Skin of Colour

## Authors
Chauhan Payal, Goyal Pratika, Meena Dilip, Daroach Manju, Jindal Rashmi

## Journal / DOI
Dermatology practical & conceptual · DOI: 10.5826/dpc.1604a7221 · PMID: 43063413
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43063413/

## Abstract
**INTRODUCTION:** Morphea and extragenital lichen sclerosus (LSA) are chronic inflammatory dermatoses with overlapping clinical features, requiring invasive biopsy for confirmation. Dermoscopy, a noninvasive tool, may aid clinical assessment by identifying characteristic cutaneous patterns.

**OBJECTIVES:** To study the dermoscopic features of LSA, morphea, and their overlap in histopathologically confirmed cases and describe characteristic dermoscopic patterns.

**METHODS:** This retrospective observational study was conducted in 2020-2024 in four tertiary care centres across North India. Histopathologically confirmed cases of morphea, LSA, or overlap were included. Dermoscopic evaluation was performed using hand-held dermoscope (10x magnification, polarized non-contact mode), and findings were analysed descriptively at the lesion level.

**RESULTS:** Among 90 lesions in 50 patients, 13 were genital LSA, 52 extragenital LSA, 15 morphea and 10 LSA-morphea overlap. Common dermoscopic findings in LSA included white structureless areas (96.15%; shiny in 92.31%), pigment change (84.62%, predominantly brown dots), reddish hue (71.15%), violaceous hue (59.62%), scaling (53.85%) and mainly dotted vessels (36.54%). Morphea showed white structureless areas (100%; predominantly dull and broad, 80%), brown structureless areas (73.33%), pigmentary change (80%, predominantly network type), reduced eccrine openings (80%) and relative absence of vessels with occasional occurrence of linear curved vessels (13.33%). Follicular plugging was comparable in both. Overlap cases had a mix of shiny to dull white structureless areas, pigment change (80%, variable pattern), preserved eccrine openings (60%), follicular plugs (50%) and vessels (dotted 50% and linear curved 30%). Genital LSA lesions showed shiny white structureless areas (92.31%), reddish hue (76.92%) and violaceous hue (61.54%).

**CONCLUSION:** Recurring dermoscopic patterns were observed: shiny white structureless areas with reddish/violaceous hue and polymorphous vessels in extragenital LSA; dull white and brown structureless areas, pigment network and reduced eccrine openings in morphea; and hybrid features in overlap lesions. Dermoscopy may therefore complement clinical assessment of these entities; however, prospective studies evaluating its diagnostic accuracy are needed before it can inform decisions regarding biopsy.

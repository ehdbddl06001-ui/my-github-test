## Title
A mHealth-Based Vestibular Rehabilitation Program for Postoperative Vestibular Dysfunction in Patients With Vestibular Schwannoma: Randomized Controlled Trial

## Authors
Zhang Dongmei, Zhu Furong, Wu Peixia, Cheng Guiqing, Ye Xiaonan, Liu Ying, Gu Xuyan, Ma Ziling

## Journal / DOI
Journal of medical Internet research · DOI: 10.2196/98941 · PMID: 43056702
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43056702/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Patients with vestibular schwannoma often experience postoperative vestibular dysfunction, including vertigo, dizziness, and imbalance, which severely impair daily functioning and quality of life. Effective and accessible rehabilitation strategies are needed to improve recovery after surgery.

**OBJECTIVE:** This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of a mobile health-based vestibular rehabilitation program in improving postoperative vestibular outcomes after unilateral vestibular schwannoma surgery.

**METHODS:** A prospective randomized controlled trial comparing a customized mobile app-based vestibular rehabilitation program with standard face-to-face vestibular rehabilitation was conducted at the Otology Center, Eye & ENT Hospital, Fudan University, from October 2023 to May 2025. A total of 60 patients undergoing unilateral vestibular schwannoma surgery were enrolled and randomly assigned to either an intervention group (n=30) or a control group (n=30). Both groups received a 90-day vestibular rehabilitation program. The intervention group received guidance, symptom recording, and adherence monitoring through a customized mobile app, whereas the control group received face-to-face guidance. The primary outcome was the between-group difference in the change in Dizziness Handicap Inventory (DHI) score from baseline (preoperative) to postoperative day 90. Secondary outcomes included visual analog scale scores for vestibular symptoms and the incidence of vertigo, dizziness, vestibulo-visual symptoms, and postural symptoms.

**RESULTS:** Of the 60 randomized participants, 52 (86.7%) had DHI scores available at postoperative day 90, and 49 (81.7%) had paired baseline and day-90 DHI scores available to calculate the primary outcome. The adjusted between-group difference in DHI change from baseline to postoperative day 90 was not significant (adjusted mean difference -0.83, 95% CI -8.79 to 7.13; P=.84). No significant differences were observed in visual analog scale scores at postoperative days 7, 30, or 90 (all P>.05). Among the assessed vestibular symptoms, postural symptoms on postoperative day 7 were reported by 53.3% (16/30) of the intervention group and 83.3% (25/30) of the control group; the difference was not significant after Holm adjustment (unadjusted P=.03; Holm-adjusted P=.30).

**CONCLUSIONS:** In conclusion, mobile health-based rehabilitation did not demonstrate greater improvement in postoperative vestibular outcomes than face-to-face rehabilitation following unilateral vestibular schwannoma surgery. The lower proportion of patients reporting postural symptoms on postoperative day 7 remains an exploratory finding requiring confirmation in future studies.

## Title
Genome-wide mapping shows coding ITGAM variants drive a reversible interferon-suPAR axis in lupus nephritis

## Authors
Li Xiaobo, Villanueva Veronica, Balza Pineda Santiago, Jimenez Viviana, Nguyen Billy, Khan Samia Q, Chauhan Nishant Ranjan, Gurusamy Kamraj Sowmya, et al.

## Journal / DOI
The Journal of clinical investigation · DOI: 10.1172/JCI211731 · PMID: 43097437
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43097437/

## Abstract
Lupus nephritis (LN) is a leading cause of kidney failure in systemic lupus erythematosus. Coding variants in ITGAM, encoding the myeloid integrin CD11b, are among the strongest genetic risk factors for LN, yet leave its abundance unchanged. Mapping the locus's trans-regulatory effects in blood from 31,684 individuals, the risk haplotype drove a type I interferon (IFN-I) program in myeloid cells. The locus resolved into two haplotypes, with an R77H-linked coding haplotype driving the trans program without affecting cis-abundance and a regulatory haplotype modulating abundance without any trans regulation. In paired blood and kidney from LN patients, the IFN-I and NF-κB arms were engaged and amplified in R77H carriers, and CD11b+ myeloid cells accumulated at the glomerulus and upregulated PLAUR, coupling the program to suPAR, an effector of podocyte injury. Re-engaging CD11b pharmacologically or genetically suppressed both arms and reversed established disease in LN models, and protected mice engrafted with cells from R77H-carrier patients. A Phase Ib trial is evaluating this mechanism in LN. A genome-wide scan found that this architecture, trans regulation without cis effect on variant's own gene, recurs at 80 disease-associated loci, defining a class of checkpoint variants. Together, these findings suggest that a common coding variant can drive kidney disease without changing expression and is therapeutically targetable.

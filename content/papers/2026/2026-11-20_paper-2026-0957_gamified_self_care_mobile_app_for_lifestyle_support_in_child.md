## Title
Gamified Self-Care Mobile App for Lifestyle Support in Children With Leukemia: Development and Randomized Controlled Trial

## Authors
Karimi Fatemeh, Behnam Vashani Hamidreza, Aghebati Nahid, Pourghaznein Tayebeh, Rajabpour Mohammad

## Journal / DOI
Journal of participatory medicine · DOI: 10.2196/93106 · PMID: 43056709
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43056709/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Children with leukemia undergoing chemotherapy may experience difficulties maintaining a healthy lifestyle and self-care behaviors. Mobile health interventions incorporating interactive and gamified educational features may provide an accessible complement to conventional education.

**OBJECTIVE:** This study aimed to develop and preliminarily evaluate a gamified self-care mobile app and evaluate the effect of the resulting intervention package on lifestyle scores among children aged 7 to 12 years with leukemia undergoing chemotherapy.

**METHODS:** This 2-phase study was conducted in Mashhad, Iran. In phase 1, the app was developed using evidence from pediatric oncology guidelines and literature and underwent multidisciplinary expert review, user-based quality assessment using the Mobile App Rating Scale, and technical testing. In phase 2, a total of 60 children undergoing chemotherapy were randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to an intervention or control group. Both groups received routine face-to-face education and a pamphlet; the intervention group additionally received the Lukemo app and weekly telephone follow-up for 8 weeks. The prespecified primary outcome was the total score of the Persian version of the Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory 4.0 (PedsQL 4.0), assessed at baseline and after 8 weeks.

**RESULTS:** Thirty children were allocated to each group in phase 2, and 59 completed the study (intervention: n=29; control: n=30). At 8 weeks, mean lifestyle scores were 89.79 (SD 10.88) in the intervention group and 81.10 (SD 10.57) in the control group (P=.003). The mean change from baseline was 9.10 (SD 10.36) vs 1.00 (SD 2.39) in each group, respectively, yielding a between-group difference in change of 8.10 points (95% CI 4.08-12.12; P<.001). Within-group improvement was significant in the intervention group (P<.001) but not in the control group (P=.05).

**CONCLUSIONS:** The intervention package incorporating the Lukemo app and weekly telephone support was associated with greater improvement in lifestyle scores over 8 weeks among children with leukemia undergoing chemotherapy. The independent contributions of the app, gamification, and telephone support could not be determined. Longer-term and multicenter studies are needed to assess sustainability and generalizability.

**TRIAL REGISTRATION:** Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials IRCT20211007052692N2; https://en.irct.ir/trial/66986.

## Title
Bow hunter's syndrome presenting with embolism in an older patient treated with C1-2 posterior fixation using intraoperative digital subtraction angiography: illustrative case

## Authors
Kakimoto Takayuki, Ogihara Kotaro, Okamoto Yusei, Kotani Hayato, Nishi Kazuhiko, Kawauchi Satoshi, Itami Hisakazu, Otsuka Shinji, et al.

## Journal / DOI
Journal of neurosurgery. Case lessons · DOI: 10.3171/CASE26750 · PMID: 43002770
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43002770/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Bow hunter's syndrome (BHS) is a rare cause of vertebrobasilar insufficiency (VBI) or artery-to-artery embolism resulting from vertebral artery (VA) occlusion during head rotation due to cervical instability. VBI predominates, whereas embolic BHS is rare.

**OBSERVATIONS:** A 77-year-old man presented with recurrent posterior circulation ischemic strokes. A cardiac embolism was initially suspected, and a brief episode of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (AF) was detected via an insertable cardiac monitor. However, wall irregularity was noted in the left VA at the C1-2 level, together with atlantoaxial subluxation associated with os odontoideum. Dynamic digital subtraction angiography (DSA) with head rotation demonstrated left VA occlusion at C1-2 in the right rotational flexion position, with distal flow maintained via collateral circulation. The patient was diagnosed with a cerebral embolism due to BHS. Given the recurrent strokes, atlantoaxial fixation was performed, and intraoperative DSA confirmed a cervical alignment preserving VA flow. No recurrence occurred during postoperative follow-up.

**LESSONS:** BHS should be considered in the differential diagnosis of infarction confined to the posterior circulation, even in older patients with AF. C1-2 posterior fixation effectively prevents recurrent infarction, and intraoperative DSA is useful for determining a cervical alignment that preserves VA blood flow. https://thejns.org/doi/10.3171/CASE26750.

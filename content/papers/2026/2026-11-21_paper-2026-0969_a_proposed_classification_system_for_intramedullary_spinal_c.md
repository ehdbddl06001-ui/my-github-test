## Title
A proposed classification system for intramedullary spinal cord arteriovenous malformations that informs surgical strategy

## Authors
Taylor Davis G, Srinivasan Visish M, Scherschinski Lea, Rhodenhiser Emmajane G, Gulsuyu Belda, Lawton Michael T

## Journal / DOI
Journal of neurosurgery · DOI: 10.3171/2026.6.JNS26183 · PMID: 43063481
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43063481/

## Abstract
**OBJECTIVE:** An anatomical classification system is proposed to characterize spinal arteriovenous malformations (SpAVMs) using preoperative digital subtraction angiography (DSA) and MRI to guide surgical strategy for each SpAVM type.

**METHODS:** A consecutive, single-surgeon series of 52 intra- and perimedullary SpAVMs resected over 17 years was reviewed. DSA, MRI, intraoperative videography, and digital photography findings were analyzed to classify SpAVMs into 5 types on the basis of arterial supply (anterior spinal artery, posterior spinal artery, or both), depth within the cord parenchyma (pial or intramedullary), and anatomical location (anterior, posterior, concentric [entirely intramedullary], or eccentric [intramedullary and presenting to or through the pial surface]).

**RESULTS:** SpAVMs were classified into 5 types as follows: type 1, posterior pial (11/52, 21%); type 2, anterior pial (11/52, 21%); type 3, anterior intramedullary (7/52, 13%); type 4, concentric intramedullary (4/52, 8%); and type 5, eccentric extra- and intramedullary (17/52, 33%). Two SpAVMs were unclassifiable. Extramedullary resection was used with types 1 and 2 pial SpAVMs (20/21, 95%) and all 17 eccentric type 5 SpAVMs. Cord rotation was applied for types 2 (8/11, 73%) and 5 (13/17, 76%). Midline myelotomy was used with all type 3 (7/7) and type 4 (4/4) SpAVMs, whereas perinidal myelotomy was used with type 5 SpAVMs (14/17, 82%). Intramedullary resection was used with all type 3 (n = 7) and type 4 (n = 4) SpAVMs and most type 5 SpAVMs (14/17, 82%). In situ occlusion was used for holomedullary SpAVMs. Algorithms recommended by the classification system were applied in 86% of cases, resulting in complete SpAVM resection or angiographic obliteration in 83% of patients (43/52). At the last follow-up, 33 (65%) surviving patients were intact or had mild motor or sensory deficits (McCormick grade 1 or 2); 18 (35%) patients had moderate or severe deficits or quadriplegia/paraplegia (McCormick grade ≥ 3). Overall, 3 of 51 (6%) patients experienced deterioration relative to preoperative baseline to dependent status (McCormick grade ≥ 3).

**CONCLUSIONS:** The proposed classification system clarifies the SpAVM pathology and guides the application of surgical techniques, thereby defining surgical strategies for each SpAVM type. This classification delineates intra- and perimedullary SpAVMs by expansion from 2 to 5 categories. The proposed classification system differentiates the spectrum of SpAVM pathoanatomy, guiding resection technique by type-specific algorithms. The classification does not predict surgical risk, patient outcome, or natural history.

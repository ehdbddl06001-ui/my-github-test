## Title
Childhood insulinoma after years of recurrent hypoglycemia: the role of GLP-1 receptor imaging

## Authors
Singh Parminder, Gupta Divya, Asima Bushra, Shukla Rajat, Kaur Hempreet

## Journal / DOI
Journal of pediatric endocrinology & metabolism : JPEM · DOI: 10.1515/jpem-2026-0696 · PMID: 43034894
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43034894/

## Abstract
**OBJECTIVES:** To highlight the diagnostic challenges of childhood insulinoma and the complementary role of GLP-1 receptor-targeted imaging in localizing a small pancreatic lesion.

**CASE PRESENTATION:** A 12-year-old boy had a history of recurrent episodes suggestive of hypoglycemia reportedly beginning at approximately 4 years of age, with reported glucose concentrations as low as 15-20 mg/dL and associated night sweats. Diagnostic evaluation was fragmented over several years due to frequent family relocations. During a critical sample, plasma glucose was 37 mg/dL, with insulin 105.2 µIU/mL and C-peptide 5.48 ng/mL, supporting endogenous hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia. MRI demonstrated a small lesion in the pancreatic body. 68Ga-Exendin-4 PET/CT demonstrated focal GLP-1 receptor-targeted uptake corresponding to the pancreatic lesion, with a delayed SUVmax of 9.1. The patient underwent open surgical enucleation. Histopathology demonstrated a 1.7-cm well-differentiated pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor, WHO Grade 1, with a Ki-67 index of 1 %, negative margins, and no necrosis, lymphovascular, or perineural invasion. Postoperatively, glucose concentrations remained normal.

**CONCLUSIONS:** Persistent or unexplained hypoglycemia in children warrants consideration of insulinoma when endogenous hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia is demonstrated. In this case, GLP-1 receptor-targeted imaging provided complementary functional localization alongside MRI and supported focused curative surgery.

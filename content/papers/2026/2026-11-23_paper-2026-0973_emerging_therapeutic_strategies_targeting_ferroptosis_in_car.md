## Title
Emerging Therapeutic Strategies Targeting Ferroptosis in Cardiomyocyte Injury: Molecular Mechanisms and Future Perspectives

## Authors
Thangavel Logapriya, Maida Engels S E

## Journal / DOI
Cardiovascular toxicology · DOI: 10.1007/s12012-026-10227-3 · PMID: 43068421
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43068421/

## Abstract
Ferroptosis is a regulated, iron-dependent form of cell death characterized by excessive lipid peroxidation resulting from disrupted iron homeostasis and impaired cellular antioxidant defenses. Increasing evidence identifies ferroptosis as a central contributor to cardiomyocyte injury in ischemia-reperfusion injury, anthracycline-induced cardiotoxicity, metabolic cardiac disorders, diabetic cardiomyopathy, and heart failure. Unlike apoptosis, necroptosis, autophagic cell death, and pyroptosis, ferroptosis is driven by glutathione depletion, glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) inactivation, and uncontrolled lipid peroxide accumulation, making it a distinct and potentially reversible therapeutic target. This review provides a comprehensive overview of the molecular mechanisms regulating cardiac ferroptosis, emphasizing the roles of GPX4, Solute Carrier Family 7 Member 11 (SLC7A11), Nuclear factor Erythroid 2-related Factor 2 (NRF2), Acyl-CoA Synthetase Long-Chain Family Member 4 (ACSL4), Ferroptosis Suppressor Protein 1 (FSP1), Dihydroorotate Dehydrogenase (DHODH), and ferritinophagy, together with their interactions with other regulated cell death pathways. We further discuss emerging therapeutic approaches, including natural phytochemicals, small-molecule ferroptosis inhibitors, and gene- and molecular target-based strategies, together with the cardio-oncology implications of inhibiting ferroptosis and the translational prospects offered by nanoparticle-based delivery, multi-omics integration, and artificial intelligence-assisted predictive modeling. Current translational challenges, including the absence of validated ferroptosis-specific biomarkers, limited human-relevant disease models, and interpatient heterogeneity, are critically evaluated. By integrating molecular mechanisms, therapeutic advances, and translational perspectives, this review provides an updated framework for understanding ferroptosis in cardiovascular toxicity and identifies key opportunities for developing clinically translatable ferroptosis-targeted cardioprotective strategies.

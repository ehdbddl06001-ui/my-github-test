## Title
Image-Based AI for the Diagnosis of Retinopathy of Prematurity: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Authors
Bai Haiyao, Zhou Yuran, Bai Yang, Wang Yaolu, Yang Ying

## Journal / DOI
JMIR medical informatics · DOI: 10.2196/109141 · PMID: 43056714
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43056714/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) is one of the major causes of preventable blindness in premature infants. Early screening, accurate diagnosis, and severity classification are crucial for reducing irreversible visual impairment. Recently, image-based AI models have been widely applied to ROP screening, diagnosis, and severity classification. However, the model architecture, data source, validation method, and diagnostic task vary across studies. Consequently, the overall diagnostic efficiency and clinical utility of deep learning (DL) remain to be systematically assessed.

**OBJECTIVE:** This study aims to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of image-based AI for ROP by disease type, validation method, model type, and use of transfer learning (TL).

**METHODS:** We performed a systematic search across Web of Science, Embase, IEEE Xplore, PubMed, and Cochrane Library as of October 12, 2026, to identify studies investigating image-based AI for ROP detection or clinical subtype classification. The risk of bias (RoB) of included studies was assessed using the Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies 2 (QUADAS-2) tool. Sensitivity and specificity were pooled using bivariate mixed-effects models, with analyses stratified by diagnostic task and validation method.

**RESULTS:** Sixty-two studies contributed 185 contingency tables. For normal vs ROP, pooled sensitivity was 0.94 (95% CI 0.92-0.96) and specificity was 0.96 (95% CI 0.94-0.97). For normal vs treatment-requiring ROP (TR-ROP), sensitivity was 0.97 (95% CI 0.91-0.99) and specificity was 0.74 (95% CI 0.66-0.80). For mild vs severe ROP, specificity was 0.78 (95% CI 0.59-0.90). In external validation, pooled sensitivity and specificity were 0.95 (95% CI 0.89-0.97) and 0.83 (95% CI 0.72-0.90), respectively, while the corresponding estimates were 0.95 (95% CI 0.93-0.96) and 0.97 (95% CI 0.96-0.98) in internal validation. For DL and conventional machine learning (ML) models, pooled sensitivity was 0.95 (95% CI 0.94-0.96) and 0.96 (95% CI 0.90-0.98), respectively, while specificity was 0.96 (95% CI 0.95-0.97) and 0.91 (95% CI 0.84-0.94), respectively. With and without TL, pooled sensitivity was 0.95 (95% CI 0.94-0.96) and 0.95 (95% CI 0.92-0.97), respectively, while specificity was 0.97 (95% CI 0.96-0.98) and 0.92 (95% CI 0.86-0.95), respectively.

**CONCLUSIONS:** Image-based AI may have high sensitivity for ROP assessment, but its specificity is lower for identifying TR-ROP and in the external-validation subgroup. Given the predominance of retrospective studies, heterogeneity, and limited external validation, current evidence supports AI as an adjunct to screening and referral, with clinicians retaining responsibility for diagnostic and treatment decisions. Prospective, multicenter external validation is needed.

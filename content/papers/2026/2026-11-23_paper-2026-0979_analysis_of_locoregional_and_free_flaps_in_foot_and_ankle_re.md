## Title
Analysis of Locoregional and Free Flaps in Foot and Ankle Reconstruction

## Authors
Kaur Jaswinder, Bashir Sheikh A, Rasool Altaf, Baba P Umar Farooq, Yasir Mir, Zargar Haroon R, Mohsin Mir, Singh Dharminder

## Journal / DOI
Cureus · DOI: 10.7759/cureus.118365 · PMID: 43068343
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43068343/

## Abstract
Introduction Reconstruction of defects in the foot and ankle region is particularly challenging because of the limited availability of local soft tissue, the superficial location of vital structures, and the need to restore both form and function while preserving ambulation. Aim and objective This study aimed to analyze the indications for locoregional and free flaps, mean operative time, immediate postoperative complications, and secondary procedures performed. Materials and methods The study was conducted in the Department of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery, Sher-i-Kashmir Institute of Medical Sciences (SKIMS), Srinagar, retrospectively from January 2022 to December 2023 and prospectively from January 2024 to December 2025. Eighty-seven patients with foot and ankle defects, irrespective of the cause (trauma, diabetes, tumor, or infections), were enrolled in the study. Of these, 53 underwent free-flap reconstruction, and 34 underwent locoregional-flap reconstruction. Results Operative time was significantly longer for free-flap reconstruction (mean, 5.81 hours) than for locoregional flap reconstruction (2.71 hours). The flap survival rate in our study was excellent in both groups, at 96.23% for free flaps and 94.12% for locoregional flaps. Flap failure occurred in two patients in each group, resulting in failure rates of 3.77% for free flaps and 5.88% for locoregional flaps. The complication rates in our study were 7.54% for free flaps and 23.50% for locoregional flaps. Conclusion Both locoregional and free flaps are reliable options for the reconstruction of foot and ankle defects, with high flap survival rates. Locoregional flaps are particularly useful for smaller defects of the heel and ankle region and require shorter operative times. Free flaps were more commonly required in younger patients with large traumatic defects involving the dorsum and distal foot, where local tissue was inadequate for coverage.

## Title
Management of Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma

## Authors
Chauhan Ayushi, Chihara Dai, Westin Jason

## Journal / DOI
JCO oncology practice · DOI: 10.1200/OP-26-00830 · PMID: 43077648

## Abstract
Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most common subtype of non-Hodgkin lymphoma, accounting for approximately 30%-40% of cases worldwide. It is an aggressive, clinically heterogeneous disease, typically presenting with rapidly enlarging lymphadenopathy that may be accompanied by systemic B symptoms. Although many patients achieve durable remissions with frontline chemoimmunotherapy, a significant subset will experience relapse. Rather than a single disease, DLBCL comprises a spectrum of biologically distinct entities, characterized by differences in cell of origin, genomic alterations, and tumor microenvironment. Advances in molecular profiling have refined our understanding of DLBCL biology, identifying subgroups with distinct prognostic and therapeutic implications. However, these biological insights have not yet been translated into meaningful improvements in frontline treatment outcomes. Over the past decade, the treatment landscape of DLBCL has undergone significant evolution, particularly in the relapsed/refractory setting with the emergence of T-cell-directed therapies, including chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy and bispecific antibodies. These advances have reshaped treatment paradigms and offer new opportunities to improve outcomes. In this review, we provide a comprehensive overview of the current management of DLBCL, with a focus on frontline therapy, evolving strategies in relapsed or refractory disease, and emerging approaches aimed at personalized treatment.

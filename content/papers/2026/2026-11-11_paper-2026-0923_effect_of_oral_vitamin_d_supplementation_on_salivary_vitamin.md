## Title
Effect of oral vitamin D₃₃ supplementation on salivary vitamin D levels and orthodontic treatment duration: a randomized controlled trial

## Authors
Sagar Sandra, Ramani Pratibha, Moses Sagar, Jeyaraj Genickson

## Journal / DOI
Clinical oral investigations · DOI: 10.1007/s00784-026-07254-3 · PMID: 43007448
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43007448/

## Abstract
**OBJECTIVES:** To evaluate the effect of systemic oral vitamin D₃ supplementation on salivary vitamin D₃ levels during different phases of orthodontic treatment and on the overall orthodontic treatment duration in adolescents undergoing fixed orthodontic treatment.

**MATERIALS AND METHODS:** A randomized controlled trial was conducted among 52 adolescents (13-18 years) with Angle's Class I malocclusion requiring extraction of all four first premolars. Participants were randomly allocated into two groups: Group 1 (control; n = 26) received no vitamin D₃ supplementation, while Group 2 (supplementation; n = 26) received oral vitamin D₃ (60,000 IU every 20 days). Allocation concealment was achieved using the Sequentially Numbered Opaque Sealed Envelope (SNOSE) method. Unstimulated saliva samples were collected at three treatment phases: T1 (archwire insertion), T2 (extraction space closure), and T3 (debonding). Salivary vitamin D₃ concentrations were quantified using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Overall orthodontic treatment duration was recorded from initial archwire placement to appliance debonding. Statistical analysis was performed using independent samples t-tests, with statistical significance set at p ≤ 0.05.

**RESULTS:** Baseline salivary vitamin D₃ levels were comparable between the two groups. Participants receiving vitamin D₃ supplementation demonstrated significantly higher salivary vitamin D₃ concentrations during extraction space closure (44.39 ± 5.22 ng/mL vs. 38.19 ± 8.65 ng/mL; p = 0.003) and at debonding (48.31 ± 4.86 ng/mL vs. 39.91 ± 8.04 ng/mL; p < 0.001) compared with the control group. The supplementation group also demonstrated a significantly shorter overall orthodontic treatment duration (17.81 ± 1.12 months) than the control group (19.50 ± 1.35 months), corresponding to a mean reduction of 1.69 months (approximately 51 days; p < 0.001). No significant gender-related differences in salivary vitamin D₃ levels were observed.

**CONCLUSION:** Oral vitamin D₃ supplementation was associated with higher salivary vitamin D₃ levels during key phases of orthodontic treatment and a significant reduction in overall treatment duration. Salivary monitoring of vitamin D₃ may have potential as a useful non-invasive biomarker to guide targeted supplementation strategies aimed at improving orthodontic treatment efficiency, however, further studies are required to establish its clinical utility in guiding supplementation.

**CLINICAL RELEVANCE:** Vitamin D₃ supplementation may represent a simple, minimally invasive adjunctive strategy for improving orthodontic treatment efficiency. Monitoring salivary vitamin D₃ levels offers a practical, non-invasive method for assessing vitamin D status, particularly in children and adolescents undergoing prolonged orthodontic treatment. However, supplementation should be individualized and guided by clinical assessment, and further studies are required before routine clinical implementation.

## Title
Association between clinical examination and CT angiography findings in patients with vascular injury following extremity trauma: a cross-sectional study

## Authors
Adrogue Santiago Rafael Olivera, Gama Lucas Gonçalves da, Reichardt Gabriel Sebben, Sarquis Lucas Mansano, Pimentel Silvania Klug

## Journal / DOI
Revista do Colegio Brasileiro de Cirurgioes · DOI: 10.1590/0100-6991e-2026006726-en · PMID: 43097561
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43097561/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Computed tomography angiography (CTA) is the imaging modality of choice for diagnosing traumatic vascular injuries in hemodynamically stable patients. However, clinical examination may allow early detection of vascular injury based on clinical predictors, without the need for CTA. Studies in the literature remain inconsistent regarding the diagnostic accuracy of clinical examination. This study aimed to assess the diagnostic accuracy of clinical examination in trauma patients with suspected vascular injury to the extremities.

**METHODS:** Retrospective cross-sectional study of patients who underwent CT angiography of the extremities. The epidemiological characteristics of the study population, including mechanisms of injury, were described, and predictors of vascular injury were evaluated.

**RESULTS:** The study included 447 patients with suspected vascular injury to the extremities, of whom 17.2% had vascular injury detected on CTA. The hard signs of vascular injury evaluated were highly statistically significant, with pulsatile bleeding occurring in 29.8% of patients with vascular injury and absent distal pulses in 63.3%, consistent with findings from previous studies. The strongest predictors of vascular injury were capillary refill time >3 s (OR = 9.03), active bleeding (OR = 2.52), pulsatile hematoma (OR = 8.6), and absent pulse (OR = 27.9).

**CONCLUSION:** Major and minor predictors of vascular injury showed agreement with physical examination findings and can be safely used to identify vascular injury. Capillary refill time (CRT) > 3 s showed statistical relevance comparable to that of the major signs of vascular injury and may represent a new parameter for identifying vascular injury.

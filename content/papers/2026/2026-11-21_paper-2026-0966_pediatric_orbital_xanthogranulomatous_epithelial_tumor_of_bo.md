## Title
Pediatric Orbital Xanthogranulomatous Epithelial Tumor of Bone

## Authors
Sanfelippo William A, Spader Heather L, Sun Wei, McCord Matthew R, Fanburg-Smith Julie C, Druzgal Colleen H, Gigantelli James W

## Journal / DOI
Ophthalmic plastic and reconstructive surgery · DOI: 10.1097/IOP.0000000000003307 · PMID: 43063483
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43063483/

## Abstract
Xanthogranulomatous epithelial tumor (XGET) is a recently recognized mesenchymal tumor that originates from soft tissue or bone. While XGETs may occur throughout the skeleton, there has been only 1 previously reported case of cranial involvement and no cited cases of orbital involvement. We report a 4-year-old boy presenting with subacute unilateral proptosis and hypoglobus of the OD secondary to an orbital roof soft tissue tumor. Following cranio-orbital resection of the tumor, its histopathology demonstrated overlapping fibrohistiocytic, myofibroblastic, and giant cell-rich morphologies and an immunophenotypic profile demonstrating an HMGA2-NCOR2 gene fusion characteristic of XGET. Twenty-three months post-resection, the patient is doing well with resolution of symptoms and no evidence of recurrence or metastasis. To our knowledge, this is the second youngest reported patient with XGET, only the second case involving the skull, and the first involving the orbit. Although rare, XGET should be considered in the workup of giant cell-rich lesions in young patients.

## Title
Endogenous vitamin C interference in routine clinical chemistry assays: evaluation of ascorbate oxidase pretreatment across 19 analytes

## Authors
Yoo Dong-Won, Kim Hyung-Hoi

## Journal / DOI
Laboratory medicine · DOI: 10.1093/labmed/lmag058 · PMID: 42993860
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42993860/

## Abstract
**INTRODUCTION:** Ascorbic acid (vitamin C) interferes with oxidase-peroxidase-based clinical chemistry assays. We evaluated endogenous ascorbic acid interference across 19 routine analytes and ascorbate oxidase (AOx) pretreatment efficacy.

**METHODS:** Plasma from 100 patients with ascorbate-containing bowel preparation exposure was split into untreated and AOx-treated aliquots. The untreated aliquot received an equal-volume 0.9% sodium chloride vehicle under identical incubation (37 °C, 15 minutes), controlling for dilution and handling. Both aliquots were analyzed on a Roche cobas c702 Chemistry Analyzer. Lipid profiles were compared before and after colonoscopy in 176 patients. Bias was assessed against European Federation of Clinical Chemistry and Laboratory Medicine-allowable bias; reference change values used local imprecision. Concentration dependence was assessed by correlation, regression, and tertile analysis.

**RESULTS:** Lipid changes stayed within reference change values; only triglyceride (TG) reduction with the high-ascorbate solution exceeded the Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments 2025 criterion (-27.21% vs ±15%). After AOx, TG levels changed markedly (+28.48%), with alanine aminotransferase (-45.15%) and aspartate aminotransferase (+46.43%) showing larger opposite changes. Concentration did not predict individual TG bias (R² = 0.056); no analyte survived Bonferroni correction.

**DISCUSSION:** Ascorbate oxidase altered several analytes, TG most clearly, with an opposite aspartate aminotransferase/alanine aminotransferase pattern. Because concentration did not predict interference, concentration-based screening is an ineffective gatekeeper; exposure-triggered selective AOx pretreatment appears to be more practical, pending assay-specific local validation.

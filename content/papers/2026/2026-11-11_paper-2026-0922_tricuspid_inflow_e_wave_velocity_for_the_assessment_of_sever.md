## Title
Tricuspid inflow E-wave velocity for the assessment of severe or greater tricuspid regurgitation: derivation and external validation of threshold values

## Authors
Fisicaro Samantha, Moldovan Madalina-Patricia, Kawada Yuka, Penso Marco, Simonato Matheus, Buta Alexandra Simona, Negru Andra, Clement Alexandra, et al.

## Journal / DOI
European heart journal. Cardiovascular Imaging · DOI: 10.1093/ehjci/jeag304 · PMID: 43008201
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43008201/

## Abstract
**AIMS:** Current guidelines include tricuspid inflow E-wave (>100 cm/s by pulsed-wave Doppler) as a semiquantitative criterion for diagnosing severe tricuspid regurgitation (TR), despite limited supporting evidence. We assessed its diagnostic performance for identifying severe or greater TR, deriving and externally validating practical thresholds.

**METHODS AND RESULTS:** Tricuspid inflow E-wave velocity was measured by pulsed-wave Doppler in consecutive patients with at least mild TR between December 2024 and February 2026. Among 178 patients (77±13 years, 65% women), TR was mild in 44, moderate in 61, severe in 44, massive in 15, and torrential in 14. Mean tricuspid E-wave velocity was 56±16 cm/s and increased with TR severity: 47±8, 50±10, 62±16, 67±13, and 77±21 cm/s for mild, moderate, severe, massive, and torrential TR. Only three patients had E-wave velocity >100 cm/s (one with severe and two with torrential TR). Using multiparametric echocardiographic grading as the reference standard, the >100 cm/s threshold showed 5.5% sensitivity, 100% specificity, and kappa 0.25 for severe or greater TR. As a continuous variable, E-wave velocity discriminated severe or greater TR with an AUC of 0.81. A lower threshold of 52 cm/s yielded 81% sensitivity and 67% specificity, whereas 47 cm/s provided 90% sensitivity and 43% specificity. External validation resulted in 94% sensitivity and 47% specificity.

**CONCLUSION:** The guideline-recommended threshold of >100 cm/s is highly insensitive for detecting severe or greater TR. Lower cut-offs, particularly 47 cm/s, may be useful for screening to trigger a comprehensive multiparametric assessment.

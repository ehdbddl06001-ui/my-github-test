## Title
Diagnostic accuracy of Cxbladder Monitor compared with surveillance cystoscopy in low- and intermediate-risk non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer: an exploratory prospective pilot study at two hospitals

## Authors
Yang Jeffery, Bushati Ymer, Stanowski Matthew

## Journal / DOI
International urology and nephrology · DOI: 10.1007/s11255-026-05503-8 · PMID: 43102739
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43102739/

## Abstract
**PURPOSE:** Cxbladder Monitor (CxM) is a urinary biomarker with a high negative predictive value (NPV) for detecting bladder cancer recurrence. In this exploratory pilot, we evaluated the diagnostic performance of CxM against flexible cystoscopy in surveillance of low-risk and intermediate-risk non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) and assessed patient-reported outcomes and procedure-level cost.

**METHODS:** In this prospective study at two hospitals, 50 patients on annual surveillance for previously low-grade Ta NMIBC provided CxM urine tests immediately before flexible cystoscopy; the sample size was set by kit availability. Cystoscopy with histopathological confirmation was the reference standard. Patient experience was assessed with a self-completed, non-validated Likert questionnaire, and procedure-level costs were compared.

**RESULTS:** Forty-six patients had valid paired CxM and cystoscopy results. Five recurrences were identified, all low-grade Ta, with no high-grade or muscle-invasive recurrences. CxM detected three recurrences, giving a sensitivity of 60% (95% CI 23-88) and specificity of 97.6% (95% CI 87-99.6). The positive predictive value was 75% (95% CI 30-95) and NPV 95.2% (95% CI 84-99). Accuracy for clinically significant disease could not be assessed. Patient-reported outcomes strongly favoured urine testing. The procedure-level saving of approximately AUD 610 per patient fell to AUD 253 when a results consultation was costed.

**CONCLUSION:** In a low-risk and intermediate-risk cohort, CxM showed high specificity and NPV alongside cystoscopy, but the small number of recurrences precludes conclusions about its clinical use. These data can inform an adequately powered confirmatory study enriched for high-grade events.

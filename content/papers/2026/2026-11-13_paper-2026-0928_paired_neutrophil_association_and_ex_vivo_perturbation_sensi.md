## Title
Paired neutrophil association and ex vivo perturbation sensitivity define a bounded myeloid activation model in ARDS

## Authors
Wei Wei, Liu Jing, Zhou Ying, Zhao Rui, Zhang LinGang

## Journal / DOI
PloS one · DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0362747 · PMID: 43019329
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43019329/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) is biologically heterogeneous, but how myeloid transcriptional programmes relate across blood and lung compartments remains uncertain. We examined whether a pre-specified neutrophil effector/degranulation programme and a separately derived perturbation-response module provide convergent, boundary-aware evidence across public cohorts.

**METHODS:** Patient-level analyses descriptively localized programmes in a seven-patient peripheral blood mononuclear cell cohort and assessed paired bronchoalveolar lavage fluid and longitudinal alveolar-macrophage profiles. PBMC disease groups were severely confounded with expression-inferred sex. In purified neutrophils, paired ARDS-healthy-volunteer comparisons and healthy-donor granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF)/broad phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) perturbations were analysed separately. A donor-adjusted whole-transcriptome weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) derived a module under frozen retention rules, which was externally projected using fixed loadings and a pre-specified coverage gate.

**RESULTS:** The neutrophil-degranulation programme was higher in all 12 ARDS-healthy-volunteer pairs (mean paired difference, 0.0321; Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery rate [BH-FDR] = 0.000977), increased after GM-CSF in 9 of 10 donors (BH-FDR = 0.0276) and decreased after broad pan-PI3K inhibition in all 10 donors (BH-FDR = 0.00469). No gene passed the genome-wide three-direction conjunction threshold; SERPINB1, PNP and TLR2 were retained only as pathway-restricted directional response anchors. A separate 627-gene green module met the perturbation-response rule (conjunction BH-FDR = 3.50 × 10⁻⁴) and showed functional convergence with limited gene-level overlap. PBMC localization was descriptive, without an interpretable ARDS-sepsis contrast. External green projection showed post hoc monocyte/macrophage-biased ordering rather than neutrophil specificity. In eight paired BALF patients, the pre-specified contrast was directionally opposite but inconclusive (P = 0.571); longitudinal outcome evidence was inconsistent.

**CONCLUSIONS:** These data support an ARDS-associated myeloid/neutrophil effector programme with paired disease association and ex vivo GM-CSF/pan-PI3K sensitivity. They support mechanistic prioritization of compartment-dependent myeloid biology, but not a prognostic signature, a causal programme-to-outcome mechanism, PI3K isoform specificity or PI3K-directed therapeutic efficacy.

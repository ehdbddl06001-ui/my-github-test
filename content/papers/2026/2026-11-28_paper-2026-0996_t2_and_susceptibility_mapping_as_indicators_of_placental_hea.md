## Title
T2* and susceptibility mapping as indicators of placental health

## Authors
Turnbull A, Hutchinson G, Dewick L, Li R, Bradley C, Leach L, Amantis D, Chen X, et al.

## Journal / DOI
Ultrasound in obstetrics & gynecology : the official journal of the International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology · DOI: 10.1002/uog.70381 · PMID: 43102962
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43102962/

## Abstract
**OBJECTIVES:** Transverse relaxation time (T2*) and magnetic susceptibility (χ) mapping from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) provide complementary measures of the hemodynamic environment in the placenta. We aimed to use these simultaneously obtained measurements to investigate the role of oxygen distribution on the well-established reduction of T2* with gestational age (GA) observed in healthy pregnancies and how oxygenation varies in different regions of the placenta. We also aimed to explore differences in these measurements in placentae of pregnancies with a range of complications, including pre-eclampsia, fetal growth restriction and abnormal placental histopathology.

**METHODS:** Participants with a singleton pregnancy were recruited from Nottingham University Hospitals National Health Service Trust (Nottingham, UK) antenatal care between July 2023 and April 2025. Participants attended 1-5 visits at the Sir Peter Mansfield Imaging Centre for research MRI scans between 29 and 41 weeks' gestation. Clinical measures of placental health were assessed, including pregnancy outcomes, and were used to categorize the participants by degree of placental compromise (healthy/no compromise, mild compromise or severe compromise). T2* and χ were measured simultaneously via a double-echo, echo-planar scan of the whole placenta, across a range of GAs and pregnancy complications. Regional variations were investigated by dividing the placental mask into maternal, fetal and central regions. Measurements were compared between outcome groups using linear mixed-effects models to account for GA trends, with comparisons performed separately for visits < 34 and ≥ 34 weeks' gestation.

**RESULTS:** Overall, 78 participants completed the study, with 77 included in the analyses (51 healthy controls, 13 participants with mild placental compromise and 13 with severe placental compromise). Whole placental mean T2* was more strongly associated with the SD of χ than with mean χ, indicating that it may be driven more by increasing local inhomogeneities than bulk deoxygenation with healthy gestation. Compared with healthy controls, compromised placentae showed increased SD of χ (P < 0.001 and P = 0.011 at < 34 weeks and ≥ 34 weeks, respectively) as well as lower mean T2* (P < 0.001 in both GA ranges), suggesting flow/uptake mismatch and reduced oxygenation. Considering cross-sectional variation from the maternal to the fetal side, in healthy controls, χ was lowest (indicating most oxygenation) and least variable in the central region of the placenta (P = 0.009 and P < 0.001 for mean χ compared with the maternal and fetal regions, respectively, and P < 0.001 for SD of χ for both comparisons), indicating good mixing and refreshment of blood in this area. χ was highest (indicating most deoxygenation) and most variable in the fetal region (P < 0.001 for mean χ compared with both maternal and central regions, and P = 0.003 and P < 0.001 for SD of χ compared with the maternal and central regions, respectively) in healthy controls, suggesting less effective perfusion in this region. Compromised cases showed the greatest difference compared with healthy controls in the fetal region for both the mean and the SD of χ. Mean T2* was lowest in the fetal region for healthy controls (P < 0.001 compared with both maternal and central regions) and a similar but non-significant trend was seen in compromised cases (P = 0.090 and P = 0.059 for the fetal region compared with maternal and central regions, respectively), but there was a greater difference in mean T2* between healthy and compromised cases in the maternal and central regions.

**CONCLUSIONS:** T2* and χ can be mapped simultaneously from a single MRI scan, and provide complementary information about the function of the placenta across healthy gestational development, and they could potentially act as indicators of placental compromise. © 2026 International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology.

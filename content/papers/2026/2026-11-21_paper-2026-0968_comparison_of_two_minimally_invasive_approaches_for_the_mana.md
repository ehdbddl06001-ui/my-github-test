## Title
Comparison of two minimally invasive approaches for the management of molar-incisor hypomineralization opacities: effects on esthetic perceptions of children and their parents

## Authors
Demiralay Ceren, Bolaca Arif

## Journal / DOI
BMC oral health · DOI: 10.1186/s12903-026-09742-0 · PMID: 43058640
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43058640/

## Abstract
**BACKGROUND:** This study aimed to compare the short-term esthetic outcomes of two minimally invasive approaches for the management of molar-incisor hypomineralization (MIH) opacities on the esthetic perceptions of children and their parents, and to assess color changes in affected incisors.

**METHODS:** Fifty children aged 8-10 years presenting with well-demarcated white-cream and/or yellow-brown opacities on their permanent incisors due to MIH were included. Participants were randomly allocated into two groups: Group I (resin infiltration, Icon®) and Group II (etch-bleach-seal (EBS) technique). The ''Child's and Parent's Questionnaire about Teeth Appearance'' was administered to assess esthetic perception. Color changes were measured using a spectrophotometer, and the color differences (ΔE) were calculated. The data were statistically analyzed.

**RESULTS:** Although the overall questionnaire results at the 3-month follow-up showed no statistically significant difference between the two groups (p > 0.05), improvements were observed in the physical and psychological domains of esthetic perceptions for both children and parents. Moreover, increased satisfaction among children regarding tooth appearance and color (p < 0.05) suggested that the minimally invasive approaches positively influenced esthetic perception. The color change at 1 month (ΔE1) observed in Group I (Icon®) was statistically greater than that in Group II (EBS) (p < 0.05), while no significant difference was observed between the groups at 3 months.

**CONCLUSION:** Both treatment approaches improved esthetic perceptions of children and their parents, although the difference between groups was not significant. Minimally invasive approaches produced clinically perceptible color changes; however, complete masking of MIH-related anterior enamel opacities was not consistently achieved.

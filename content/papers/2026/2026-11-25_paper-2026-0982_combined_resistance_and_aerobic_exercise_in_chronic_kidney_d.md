## Title
Combined resistance and aerobic exercise in chronic kidney disease: An exploratory study of physical activity as a moderator of adaptations

## Authors
Gollie Jared M, Ryan Alice S, Harris-Love Michael O, Kokkinos Peter, Scholten Joel D, Pugh R Jamil, Hazel Cooper G, Blackman Marc R

## Journal / DOI
Experimental physiology · DOI: 10.1113/EP093682 · PMID: 43077359
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077359/

## Abstract
Physical inactivity is common in chronic kidney disease (CKD) and might contribute to impaired skeletal muscle and physical function. Whether habitual physical activity (PA) influences adaptation to structured exercise in CKD remains unclear. We examined the effects of 6 weeks of combined flywheel resistance and aerobic exercise (FRE+AE) on neuromuscular and physical function and explored whether baseline self-reported PA moderated treatment response in older male Veterans with stages 3-4 CKD. Twenty-four participants were randomized to FRE+AE (n = 12) or health education (EDU; n = 12). Outcomes included vastus lateralis (VL MT) and rectus femoris (RF MT) muscle thickness, maximal voluntary isometric torque (MVIT), isokinetic knee-extensor power at 60° s- 1 and 180° s- 1, gait speed (GS), five-repetition sit-to-stand (STS) and timed up and go (TUG). Intervention effects were evaluated using baseline-adjusted linear regression, and baseline PA was examined as a continuous moderator. At 6 weeks, FRE+AE resulted in greater VL MT than EDU (adjusted difference, 3.31 mm; 95% confidence interval, 0.91-5.71; P = 0.010) and greater GS (0.17 m s- 1; 95% confidence interval, 0.07-0.26; P = 0.002). Treatment effects for RF MT (2.09 mm; P = 0.051) and isokinetic power at 180° s- 1 (28 W; P = 0.060) favoured FRE+AE but were not statistically significant after controlling for multiple comparisons. No clear treatment effects were observed for MVIT, power at 60° s- 1, STS or TUG. Baseline PA did not moderate treatment effects across outcomes. These findings demonstrate that short-term FRE+AE can improve VL MT and GS in older male Veterans with moderate-to-advanced CKD, whereas baseline habitual self-reported PA did not explain variation in treatment responsiveness.

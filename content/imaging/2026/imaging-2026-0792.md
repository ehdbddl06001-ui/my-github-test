## 문제
A 34-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department because of agitation and confusion for 12 hours. Three days ago, she developed fever and a productive cough. Her husband says she has lost 6 kg over the past 3 months and has had palpitations and heat intolerance, but she has not seen a physician. Temperature is 40.1°C (104.2°F), pulse is 148/min and irregularly irregular, respirations are 26/min, and blood pressure is 152/58 mm Hg. She is diaphoretic and disoriented. There is a diffuse, nontender goiter with a bruit and bilateral proptosis. Crackles are heard over the right lower lung field. Serum TSH concentration is undetectable, and free T4 concentration is markedly elevated. She is given intravenous fluids, acetaminophen, antibiotics for pneumonia, and propranolol. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in pharmacotherapy?

- A. Potassium iodide
- B. Radioactive iodine
- C. Levothyroxine
- D. Aspirin
- E. Propylthiouracil

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: Fever over 40°C, <b>delirium</b>, tachycardia with atrial fibrillation, and a precipitating infection in a patient with untreated Graves disease (goiter with bruit, proptosis) is <b>thyroid storm</b>. After beta-blockade, the next step is a <b>thionamide to block new hormone synthesis</b>; propylthiouracil is preferred in storm because it also inhibits peripheral T4-to-T3 conversion. Iodine is given only <b>at least 1 hour after</b> the thionamide.
- **원리**: <b>Four targets, in order</b>: (1) block β-adrenergic effects (propranolol), (2) block synthesis of new hormone (thionamide), (3) block release of stored hormone (iodine), (4) block peripheral T4→T3 conversion (propylthiouracil, glucocorticoids, propranolol). Treat the precipitant and support temperature and volume.<br> <b>Why the thionamide comes before iodine</b>: Iodine acutely inhibits release of preformed hormone (Wolff-Chaikoff effect). But if the gland's organification enzyme, thyroid peroxidase, is not yet blocked, the large iodine load becomes substrate for even more hormone synthesis and can worsen thyrotoxicosis (Jod-Basedow effect). Giving the thionamide first closes the synthesis pathway so iodine only blocks release.<br> <b>Why propylthiouracil here</b>: Both propylthiouracil and methimazole block thyroid peroxidase; only propylthiouracil also inhibits type 1 deiodinase, lowering T3 faster. Outside storm and the first trimester, methimazole is preferred because propylthiouracil can cause fulminant hepatotoxicity.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Propylthiouracil (answer)</th><th>Potassium iodide (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Action</td><td>Blocks thyroid peroxidase (synthesis) and peripheral T4→T3</td><td>Blocks release of stored hormone</td></tr> <tr><td>Timing in storm</td><td><b>First, after beta-blocker</b></td><td><b>At least 1 hour after thionamide</b></td></tr> <tr><td>If given alone first</td><td>Safe</td><td>Iodine fuels new hormone synthesis</td></tr> </tbody></table> "Synthesis before release": block the factory before you shut the warehouse door, otherwise the iodine becomes raw material.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Potassium iodide would be the correct next drug at least 1 hour after a thionamide has already been given.
  - (B) Radioactive iodine would be appropriate for definitive treatment of Graves disease once she is euthyroid and stable, not during storm.
  - (C) Levothyroxine would be correct if TSH were elevated and free T4 low, as in myxedema coma, the opposite emergency.
  - (D) Aspirin should be avoided because it displaces T4 from binding proteins and raises free hormone; it would suit fever in a patient without thyrotoxicosis.
- **함정**: Giving iodine first because it acts fastest — without prior thionamide blockade, the iodine load becomes substrate for more hormone.
- **학습목표**: Treat thyroid storm by giving a thionamide (propylthiouracil) before iodine, together with a beta-blocker and glucocorticoid, and explain why iodine must follow thionamide blockade

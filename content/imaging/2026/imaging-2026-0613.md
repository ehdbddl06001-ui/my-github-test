## 문제
A 29-year-old woman who works as a nurse is evaluated after a routine employee screening interferon-gamma release assay is positive; her test 1 year ago was negative. She has no cough, fever, night sweats, or weight loss. She has no history of tuberculosis or HIV infection and takes no medications. She does not drink alcohol. Physical examination shows no abnormalities. Serum aminotransferase levels are within the reference ranges, and an HIV test is negative. A chest x-ray shows no abnormalities. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Bacille Calmette-Guérin revaccination
- B. Daily rifampin for 4 months
- C. Isoniazid, rifampin, pyrazinamide, and ethambutol for 2 months
- D. No treatment and repeat testing in 1 year
- E. Sputum acid-fast bacilli smear and culture before any decision

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: A newly positive interferon-gamma release assay (conversion within 1 year) in a health care worker indicates recent infection with Mycobacterium tuberculosis. She has no symptoms, a normal examination, and a normal chest radiograph, so active disease is excluded clinically and she has latent tuberculosis infection. Recent converters have the highest risk of progression, so treatment is indicated. A rifamycin-based short regimen — daily rifampin for 4 months — is preferred because it is as effective as 9 months of isoniazid, with better completion and less hepatotoxicity.
- **원리**: <b>Why latent infection needs treatment</b>: In latent infection, a small number of bacilli are contained in granulomas by T-cell immunity. They divide slowly or not at all, so a single drug is enough to sterilize them without selecting resistance — unlike active disease, where the large, replicating population contains spontaneous resistant mutants and needs multiple drugs.<br> <b>Why recent conversion matters</b>: About half of the lifetime risk of progression (5-10% overall) occurs in the first 2 years after infection. A documented conversion therefore identifies someone at the peak of risk.<br> <b>Why rifampin is preferred</b>: Rifampin kills semi-dormant bacilli quickly, so 4 months suffices; shorter courses are completed more often, and hepatotoxicity is lower than with isoniazid. Its main drawback is CYP induction, so drug interactions (for example, hormonal contraception, anticoagulants) must be checked.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Latent infection — rifampin 4 months (answer)</th><th>Active disease — four-drug regimen (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Symptoms</td><td>None</td><td>Cough, fever, night sweats, weight loss</td></tr> <tr><td>Chest radiograph</td><td><b>Normal</b></td><td>Infiltrate, cavity, effusion, or lymphadenopathy</td></tr> <tr><td>Bacillary burden</td><td>Small, semi-dormant</td><td>Large, replicating — resistant mutants present</td></tr> <tr><td>Regimen</td><td>Single rifamycin (or rifapentine-isoniazid)</td><td>Four drugs for 2 months, then two drugs for 4 months</td></tr> </tbody></table> The decision hinges on excluding active disease; once symptoms and chest imaging are normal, multidrug therapy is unnecessary and single-drug preventive therapy is enough.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) BCG revaccination is not used to treat or prevent progression of established infection in adults and would never be the answer here.
  - (C) A four-drug regimen would be correct if she had symptoms or an abnormal chest radiograph suggesting active tuberculosis.
  - (D) Observation without treatment would be reasonable only for a low-risk person with a likely false-positive result, not for a documented recent converter.
  - (E) Sputum smear and culture would be needed if she had respiratory symptoms or an abnormal radiograph; with both normal they add little.
- **함정**: Starting four-drug therapy for any positive test — with no symptoms and a normal chest radiograph the diagnosis is latent infection, treated with a single rifamycin.

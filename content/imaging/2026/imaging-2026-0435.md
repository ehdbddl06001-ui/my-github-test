## 문제
A 34-year-old woman comes to the physician because of an intensely itchy rash for 3 weeks. The itching is worst at night. Her 6-year-old son, who sleeps in her bed, has developed similar itching during the past week. She has no history of atopic dermatitis and takes no medications. Vital signs are within normal limits. Examination shows excoriated papules and several short, wavy, gray-white linear burrows in the finger web spaces and on the flexor surfaces of the wrists, with scattered papules around the umbilicus. The face and scalp are spared. Microscopy of a mineral oil scraping from a burrow shows an eight-legged organism and oval eggs. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

- A. Permethrin cream for her and all household contacts at once
- B. Permethrin cream for the patient only
- C. High-potency topical corticosteroid alone
- D. Oral cephalexin for the patient and her son
- E. Oral hydroxyzine alone for symptom relief

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: Nocturnal pruritus, burrows in the finger webs and wrists, periumbilical papules, a symptomatic household member, and mites with eggs on a burrow scraping establish scabies. Treatment is topical permethrin 5% for the patient and all close and household contacts at the same time — even those without symptoms — repeated in 7–14 days, with hot-water laundering of bedding and clothing.
- **원리**: <b>Why everyone at once</b>: <i>Sarcoptes scabiei</i> spreads by prolonged skin-to-skin contact, such as sharing a bed. The itch is a delayed (type IV) hypersensitivity reaction to the mite, its eggs, and feces that takes 3–6 weeks to develop in a first infestation — so infested contacts can carry mites for weeks without symptoms. Treating only the patient leaves an untreated reservoir, and reinfestation follows.<br> <b>Why permethrin, and why repeat</b>: permethrin blocks sodium channels in the mite's nerve membranes, causing paralysis and death. It is less reliably ovicidal, so a second application after 7–14 days kills mites that hatch from surviving eggs.<br> <b>Why itching persists</b>: the hypersensitivity reaction lasts for 2–4 weeks after successful treatment; persistent itch alone is not treatment failure, and new burrows are.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Treat patient and contacts (answer)</th><th>Treat patient only (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Asymptomatic contacts</td><td><b>Treated — may be infested without itch for weeks</b></td><td>Left as a reservoir</td></tr> <tr><td>Timing</td><td>Same day for all</td><td>Contacts treated only when itching starts</td></tr> <tr><td>Outcome</td><td>Cure of the household</td><td>Ping-pong reinfestation</td></tr> </tbody></table> The symptomatic son proves transmission; the delayed itch means others may already be infested.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Treating only the patient would be adequate only if she had no close contacts, which is not the case here; untreated bedmates reinfest her.
  - (C) A topical corticosteroid alone fits atopic or contact dermatitis; it may be added for post-treatment itch but does not kill mites.
  - (D) Oral cephalexin is needed when excoriations become impetiginized with honey-colored crusts; it does not treat the infestation itself.
  - (E) Hydroxyzine helps nocturnal itch as an adjunct after scabicide treatment, but used alone it leaves the mites and the reservoir untouched.
- **함정**: Treating only people who itch — the type IV reaction takes weeks, so infested contacts are often still asymptomatic.
- **학습목표**: 밤에 심한 가려움·손가락 사이 굴·가족 내 발생으로 옴을 진단하고, 환자와 가까운 접촉자 모두를 동시에 퍼메트린으로 치료한다

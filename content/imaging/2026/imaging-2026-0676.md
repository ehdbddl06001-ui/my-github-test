## 문제
A 74-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of bleeding from his gums and several bruises on his forearms for 2 days. He has taken warfarin for 3 years for atrial fibrillation, and his INR has been stable between 2.0 and 3.0 at monthly checks. Eight days ago he began taking trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for a urinary tract infection. He has not changed his diet and drinks no alcohol. His pulse is 84/min and irregularly irregular, and blood pressure is 132/80 mm Hg. Hemoglobin concentration is 12.8 g/dL, platelet count is 230,000/mm3, and INR is 6.9. Liver function tests are within the reference ranges. Which of the following is the most likely mechanism of the increase in INR?

- A. Displacement of warfarin from plasma albumin
- B. Induction of cytochrome P450 3A4
- C. Inhibition of vitamin K epoxide reductase gene expression
- D. Decreased renal excretion of unchanged warfarin
- E. Inhibition of cytochrome P450 2C9

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: Warfarin is a racemic mixture; the S-enantiomer is 3–5 times more potent and is cleared almost entirely by hepatic CYP2C9. Sulfamethoxazole is a CYP2C9 inhibitor, so S-warfarin clearance falls and its concentration rises over several days until a new, higher steady state is reached — the INR rises from a stable 2–3 to 6.9 about a week after the antibiotic was started. Liver function, diet, and alcohol are unchanged, so the interaction explains the change.
- **원리**: <b>Why the effect takes days</b>: Inhibiting the clearance of a drug raises its steady-state concentration in proportion to the fall in clearance, but the new steady state is reached only after about four to five half-lives. Warfarin's half-life is about 36–42 hours, and the INR also lags because existing clotting factors (especially prothrombin, half-life about 60 hours) must decay. Hence the bleeding appears a week after the antibiotic was added.<br> <b>Why stereochemistry matters</b>: The S-enantiomer drives most of the anticoagulant effect and depends on CYP2C9; the R-enantiomer is cleared by CYP1A2 and CYP3A4. Inhibitors of CYP2C9 (sulfamethoxazole, metronidazole, fluconazole, amiodarone) therefore cause large INR rises, whereas inhibitors affecting only R-warfarin have smaller effects. Patients with reduced-function CYP2C9 variants need lower warfarin doses for the same reason.<br> <b>Why protein displacement is not the main mechanism</b>: Displacement from albumin briefly raises the free fraction, but the extra free drug is also available for metabolism and distribution, so free concentration returns to baseline within a few half-lives. A sustained rise requires a fall in clearance.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Displacement from plasma albumin raises free warfarin only transiently and would matter mainly if clearance were also reduced, such as with severe liver disease.
  - (B) Induction of CYP3A4, as by rifampin or carbamazepine, would increase warfarin clearance and lower the INR, so it would be correct for a falling INR.
  - (C) Inhibition of vitamin K epoxide reductase is warfarin's own mechanism; a gene variant (VKORC1) changes dose requirement from the start, not after a new antibiotic.
  - (D) Decreased renal excretion would matter for drugs eliminated unchanged by the kidney, such as dabigatran; warfarin is cleared almost entirely by hepatic metabolism.
- **함정**: Choosing albumin displacement because both drugs are highly protein bound — only reduced clearance produces a sustained INR rise.
- **학습목표**: Explain the sustained rise in INR after adding trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole to warfarin by sulfamethoxazole inhibition of CYP2C9, which metabolizes the more potent S-enantiomer of warfarin, rather than by plasma protein displacement

## 문제
A 2-year-old boy is brought to the physician because of fever for 6 days that has not responded to acetaminophen. His mother reports that his eyes have been red without discharge for 4 days and that his hands and feet are swollen. His temperature is 39.6°C, and pulse is 150/min. He is irritable. Examination shows bilateral nonexudative conjunctival injection, red cracked lips, and a strawberry tongue. There is a polymorphous maculopapular rash on the trunk. The hands and feet are diffusely red and swollen. There is no lymphadenopathy. Leukocyte count is 17,500/mm3, platelet count is 450,000/mm3, C-reactive protein is 12 mg/dL, and serum albumin is 2.9 g/dL. A rapid streptococcal antigen test is negative. Echocardiography shows no coronary artery dilation. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Supportive care and reevaluation in 5 days
- B. Intravenous immunoglobulin and aspirin
- C. Aspirin alone and repeat echocardiography in 2 weeks
- D. Oral amoxicillin
- E. Intravenous methylprednisolone alone

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Fever for 6 days with four of the five principal features — bilateral nonexudative conjunctival injection, oral changes (cracked lips, strawberry tongue), polymorphous rash, and swollen red hands and feet — fulfills the criteria for Kawasaki disease even without lymphadenopathy. A normal echocardiogram does not remove the need for treatment: intravenous immunoglobulin 2 g/kg with aspirin within the first 10 days of fever lowers the risk of coronary artery aneurysms from about 25% to about 5%.
- **원리**: <b>What happens to the coronaries</b>: Kawasaki disease is a medium-vessel vasculitis of young children. Neutrophils, then lymphocytes and plasma cells, infiltrate the coronary artery wall and destroy the internal elastic lamina and media, so the artery dilates into an aneurysm. Aneurysms usually begin to form in the second week, which is why the treatment window is the first 10 days.<br> <b>Why IVIG works</b>: High-dose IVIG modulates the immune response (Fc-receptor blockade, neutralization of toxins and cytokines, regulatory T-cell effects), quickly ending the fever and the arterial inflammation. Aspirin is given at moderate–high dose for its anti-inflammatory effect and then at low dose for its antiplatelet effect until echocardiography is normal at 6–8 weeks.<br> <b>Why a normal first echocardiogram does not change treatment</b>: The diagnosis is clinical, and the purpose of treatment is to <b>prevent</b> aneurysms that have not formed yet. A strawberry tongue and rash also occur in scarlet fever, but the negative streptococcal test, nonexudative conjunctivitis, and extremity changes point away from it.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">IVIG + aspirin (answer)</th><th>Aspirin alone, recheck later (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Effect on aneurysm risk</td><td><b>~25% → ~5%</b></td><td>No proven reduction in aneurysms</td></tr> <tr><td>Why</td><td>Stops arterial wall inflammation before the elastic lamina is destroyed</td><td>Controls fever and platelets but not the vasculitis</td></tr> <tr><td>Timing</td><td>Within 10 days of fever onset (ideally 5–7)</td><td>Waiting lets the window close</td></tr> <tr><td>This patient</td><td>Day 6, criteria met — treat now</td><td>Normal echo is not a reason to delay</td></tr> </tbody></table> Diagnose clinically, treat early, and use echocardiography to follow — not to decide whether to treat.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Supportive care and reevaluation would suit a self-limited viral exanthem, but delaying here lets coronary aneurysms form.
  - (C) Aspirin alone with later echocardiography would be inadequate even after day 10; IVIG is still given beyond 10 days if fever or inflammation persists.
  - (D) Oral amoxicillin would be correct if the rapid streptococcal test were positive for scarlet fever, with sandpaper rash and exudative pharyngitis.
  - (E) Corticosteroids are added to IVIG for high-risk patients or for IVIG-resistant disease, not given alone as first-line therapy.
- **함정**: Withholding treatment because the first echocardiogram is normal — IVIG is given to prevent aneurysms, not to treat existing ones.
- **학습목표**: Diagnose Kawasaki disease from ≥5 days of fever with four principal features and treat with intravenous immunoglobulin plus aspirin within 10 days to prevent coronary aneurysms

## 문제
A 71-year-old man comes to the physician because of severe pain and swelling of the right first metatarsophalangeal joint that began suddenly overnight. He has hypertension, atrial fibrillation, and chronic kidney disease. Current medications are apixaban, metoprolol, and amlodipine; 3 days ago, he started clarithromycin for community-acquired pneumonia. Temperature is 37.6°C, blood pressure is 138/82 mm Hg, and pulse is 76/min. The right first metatarsophalangeal joint is red, warm, swollen, and exquisitely tender. Serum creatinine is 2.3 mg/dL (eGFR 27 mL/min/1.73 m2), and serum uric acid is 8.9 mg/dL. Arthrocentesis yields cloudy fluid with 18,000 leukocytes/mm3, negative Gram stain, and needle-shaped, negatively birefringent crystals. Which of the following is the most appropriate pharmacotherapy?

- A. Oral colchicine
- B. Oral indomethacin
- C. Oral allopurinol
- D. Intravenous pegloticase
- E. Oral prednisone

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: Negatively birefringent needle-shaped crystals confirm acute gout. NSAIDs are inappropriate because of eGFR 27 and apixaban (bleeding and further renal injury). Colchicine is contraindicated because he has renal impairment and is taking clarithromycin, a strong CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein inhibitor that can raise colchicine levels to fatal toxicity. A short course of oral glucocorticoid (prednisone) is the safest effective treatment; intra-articular injection is an alternative for a single joint.
- **원리**: <b>Why the flare happens</b>: monosodium urate crystals are phagocytosed by macrophages and activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, releasing interleukin-1β, which recruits neutrophils — the intense, self-limited neutrophilic synovitis of gout. All three first-line flare drugs (NSAIDs, colchicine, glucocorticoids) blunt this inflammation; none lowers urate.<br> <b>Why colchicine is dangerous here</b>: colchicine binds tubulin and stops neutrophil migration, but it has a narrow therapeutic index. It is cleared by CYP3A4 metabolism and P-glycoprotein efflux in the gut, liver, and kidney. Clarithromycin inhibits both, and reduced renal function removes the other exit; together they cause colchicine accumulation with diarrhea, myopathy, bone marrow suppression, and multiorgan failure. Labels contraindicate colchicine with strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors in patients with renal or hepatic impairment.<br> <b>Why NSAIDs are also out</b>: NSAIDs block renal prostaglandins that maintain afferent arteriolar dilation in chronic kidney disease, risking acute kidney injury, and they add to the bleeding risk of anticoagulation. Glucocorticoids have neither problem; their short-term risks (hyperglycemia, fluid retention) are manageable.<br> <b>Urate lowering</b> (allopurinol) is indicated later because of recurrent risk, but is begun with prophylaxis rather than as treatment of the inflamed joint.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Oral prednisone (correct)</th><th>Oral colchicine (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Clearance</td><td>Hepatic; no dose change in CKD</td><td><b>CYP3A4 + P-gp</b> and renal excretion</td></tr> <tr><td>Clarithromycin</td><td>No dangerous interaction</td><td><b>Contraindicated</b> with renal impairment — fatal toxicity reported</td></tr> <tr><td>eGFR 27</td><td>Usable</td><td>Dose-limit; avoid repeat courses within 14 days</td></tr> <tr><td>Main short-term risk</td><td>Hyperglycemia, fluid retention</td><td>Diarrhea, myopathy, marrow suppression</td></tr> </tbody></table> Colchicine alone in stage 4 CKD can be used at reduced dose; it is the added strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitor that makes it contraindicated.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Low-dose colchicine would be correct for an early flare in a patient with normal renal function who is not taking strong CYP3A4 or P-glycoprotein inhibitors such as clarithromycin or cyclosporine.
  - (B) Indomethacin would be correct for a flare in a younger patient with normal kidney function, no anticoagulant, and no peptic ulcer disease or heart failure.
  - (C) Allopurinol lowers serum urate for long-term prevention and is started with flare prophylaxis; it does not relieve an acute flare, so it would be correct only as the long-term management question.
  - (D) Pegloticase is reserved for severe chronic tophaceous gout refractory to oral urate-lowering drugs and is not a treatment for an acute flare.
- **함정**: Choosing colchicine because it is gout-specific — with renal impairment and a strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitor such as clarithromycin, colchicine toxicity can be fatal.
- **학습목표**: 만성 콩팥병·항응고제·강한 CYP3A4/P-gp 억제제 복용 중인 급성 통풍에서 NSAID 와 콜히친을 피하고 경구 글루코코르티코이드로 치료한다

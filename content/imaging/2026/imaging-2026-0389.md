## 문제
A 19-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department by her roommate 6 hours after she ingested thirty 500-mg tablets of acetaminophen after an argument with her boyfriend. She has had nausea and vomited once. She takes no other medications and did not drink alcohol. Her temperature is 36.8°C (98.2°F), pulse is 96/min, respirations are 16/min, and blood pressure is 118/72 mm Hg. She is alert and oriented. Abdominal examination shows mild epigastric tenderness. Serum studies show an acetaminophen concentration of 180 µg/mL, aspartate aminotransferase 28 U/L, alanine aminotransferase 31 U/L, and prothrombin time 12 seconds. Serum salicylate and ethanol are undetectable. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Oral activated charcoal
- B. Intravenous fomepizole
- C. Urgent hemodialysis
- D. Repeat level in 4 hours
- E. Intravenous N-acetylcysteine

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: A single ingestion of 15 g (about 15 g in a young woman of average weight, well over 150 mg/kg) with a 6-hour concentration of 180 µg/mL lies far above the treatment line of the Rumack-Matthew nomogram (about 100 µg/mL at 6 hours). Normal aminotransferases do not exclude toxicity because liver injury appears after 24 hours, so intravenous N-acetylcysteine is started now.
- **원리**: <b>Why acetaminophen injures the liver</b>: most of the drug is conjugated (glucuronide, sulfate) and excreted, and a small fraction is oxidized by CYP2E1 to the reactive metabolite NAPQI, which is detoxified by glutathione. In overdose the conjugation pathways saturate, more NAPQI is formed, glutathione is used up, and NAPQI binds hepatocyte proteins, causing centrilobular (zone 3) necrosis.<br> <b>How N-acetylcysteine works</b>: it replenishes glutathione (as a cysteine donor) and directly conjugates NAPQI; started within 8 hours of ingestion it nearly abolishes serious liver injury, and it still helps later.<br> <b>Why the 4-hour rule</b>: absorption is not complete before 4 hours, so the nomogram applies only to a single acute ingestion with a known time, from 4 hours onward. Aminotransferases rise only after 24–36 hours, which is why a normal ALT at 6 hours is reassuring for nothing.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">N-acetylcysteine (answer)</th><th>Activated charcoal (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Purpose</td><td>Restores glutathione, neutralizes NAPQI</td><td>Binds drug still in the gut</td></tr> <tr><td>Time window</td><td>Best within <b>8 hours</b>, still useful later</td><td>Within about <b>1–2 hours</b> of ingestion</td></tr> <tr><td>Decided by</td><td>4-hour or later level above the nomogram line, or unknown time with detectable level</td><td>Recent ingestion in a patient who protects the airway</td></tr> </tbody></table> At 6 hours, absorption is complete, so binding the drug in the gut has little to offer; the level above the line decides antidote therapy.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Activated charcoal would be correct within about 1 to 2 hours of the ingestion in an alert patient, before most of the drug has been absorbed.
  - (B) Fomepizole would be correct for methanol or ethylene glycol poisoning with an anion-gap metabolic acidosis and an increased osmolal gap.
  - (C) Hemodialysis would be considered for massive ingestions with very high levels, coma, and lactic acidosis, or for salicylate or toxic alcohol poisoning.
  - (D) Repeating the level in 4 hours would be reasonable only if the first level were drawn before 4 hours, whereas this 6-hour level is already above the line.
- **함정**: Being reassured by normal aminotransferases and prothrombin time — hepatotoxicity appears after 24 hours, and the 6-hour level above the nomogram line requires N-acetylcysteine now.
- **학습목표**: 복용 시각이 분명한 아세트아미노펜 급성 과량 복용에서 4시간 이후 혈중 농도를 노모그램 치료선과 비교해 N-아세틸시스테인을 시작하고, 시간이 지난 활성탄과 구별한다

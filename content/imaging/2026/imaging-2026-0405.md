## 문제
A 78-year-old man comes to the physician because of a 5-day history of nausea, loss of appetite, and seeing yellow halos around lights. He has heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and atrial fibrillation treated with digoxin 0.125 mg daily, metoprolol, sacubitril-valsartan, and apixaban; his dose of digoxin has been unchanged for 2 years. Three weeks ago, amiodarone was added for recurrent symptomatic atrial fibrillation. His pulse is 48/min and irregular, and blood pressure is 112/68 mm Hg. Serum potassium is 4.3 mEq/L, magnesium is 2.0 mg/dL, and creatinine is 1.1 mg/dL, unchanged from 3 months ago. Serum digoxin concentration is 2.9 ng/mL. Which of the following best explains the increase in the digoxin concentration?

- A. Decreased glomerular filtration rate
- B. Increased oral bioavailability from enhanced gastric absorption
- C. Inhibition of P-glycoprotein-mediated renal tubular secretion
- D. Inhibition of hepatic CYP3A4-mediated oxidation
- E. Displacement of digoxin from plasma albumin binding sites

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: Digoxin is eliminated mostly unchanged by the kidney, largely through P-glycoprotein efflux in the proximal tubule, and in part by intestinal P-glycoprotein. Amiodarone inhibits P-glycoprotein, so digoxin clearance falls and its concentration roughly doubles within weeks, producing nausea, visual disturbance, and bradycardia. The creatinine is unchanged, so glomerular filtration has not fallen.
- **원리**: <b>How digoxin leaves the body</b>: about 60–70% is excreted unchanged in urine. Glomerular filtration contributes, but a large share is active secretion by P-glycoprotein (ABCB1) on the apical membrane of proximal tubular cells. P-glycoprotein in enterocytes also pumps absorbed digoxin back into the gut lumen. Hepatic CYP metabolism plays almost no role.<br> <b>What amiodarone does</b>: amiodarone (and verapamil, quinidine, dronedarone, clarithromycin, cyclosporine) inhibits P-glycoprotein. Renal secretion falls and intestinal efflux falls, so clearance drops and concentration rises — typically by 70–100%. Because amiodarone has a very long half-life, the rise develops over 1–3 weeks, the timing in this case. Guidelines advise halving the digoxin dose when amiodarone is started.<br> <b>Why the symptoms</b>: excess Na+/K+-ATPase inhibition raises vagal tone and slows AV conduction (bradycardia, AV block), increases intracellular calcium and automaticity (ectopy, bidirectional VT), and acts on the retina and chemoreceptor trigger zone (xanthopsia, nausea).
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">P-glycoprotein inhibition (answer)</th><th>CYP3A4 inhibition (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Main route of digoxin elimination</td><td><b>Renal and intestinal P-gp efflux</b></td><td>Negligible hepatic oxidation</td></tr> <tr><td>Amiodarone's effect</td><td>Inhibits P-gp → digoxin level up</td><td>Also inhibits CYP3A4 and CYP2C9 → warfarin, statins up</td></tr> <tr><td>Key clue</td><td>Creatinine unchanged, level doubled</td><td>Would matter for a CYP3A4 substrate such as simvastatin</td></tr> </tbody></table> Amiodarone inhibits both systems, so the question is which one digoxin depends on: digoxin is a P-gp substrate, not a CYP substrate.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) A decreased glomerular filtration rate would be correct if the creatinine had risen, as in dehydration or acute kidney injury; here it is unchanged.
  - (B) Increased bioavailability via the stomach would be correct only if a drug changed gastric emptying or gut flora that inactivates digoxin, such as some macrolides.
  - (D) CYP3A4 inhibition would explain amiodarone raising the level of a hepatically oxidized drug such as simvastatin; digoxin is hardly metabolized by CYP enzymes.
  - (E) Albumin displacement would matter for a highly protein-bound drug such as phenytoin or warfarin; digoxin is only about 25% bound and displacement changes only the free fraction transiently.
- **함정**: Attributing every amiodarone interaction to CYP inhibition — digoxin is a P-glycoprotein substrate, and that transporter is what amiodarone blocks here.
- **학습목표**: 디곡신은 대부분 콩팥 세관의 P-glycoprotein 으로 분비되므로, 아미오다론·verapamil·quinidine 같은 P-gp 억제제를 더하면 혈중 농도가 오른다는 것을 독성 증상과 연결한다

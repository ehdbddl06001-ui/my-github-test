## 문제
A 71-year-old man is brought to the emergency department because of generalized weakness for 2 days. He has type 2 diabetes mellitus, heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, and chronic kidney disease. Two weeks ago, spironolactone was added to his regimen of lisinopril, metoprolol, and furosemide. His pulse is 52/min and blood pressure is 108/64 mm Hg. Muscle strength is 4/5 in all extremities. Serum studies show a potassium concentration of 7.6 mEq/L, sodium 134 mEq/L, bicarbonate 19 mEq/L, glucose 142 mg/dL, and creatinine 2.9 mg/dL (baseline 1.8 mg/dL). The sample is not hemolyzed. An ECG shows peaked T waves, absent P waves, and a QRS duration of 160 msec. Which of the following is the most appropriate immediate treatment?

- A. Intravenous sodium bicarbonate
- B. Intravenous calcium gluconate
- C. Intravenous regular insulin with dextrose
- D. Nebulized albuterol
- E. Oral sodium zirconium cyclosilicate

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Potassium of 7.6 mEq/L in a nonhemolyzed sample after spironolactone was added to an ACE inhibitor in a patient with worsening chronic kidney disease, with peaked T waves, loss of P waves, and a QRS of 160 msec, is hyperkalemia with cardiotoxicity. The first step is intravenous calcium, which stabilizes the myocardial membrane within minutes. It does not lower potassium, so insulin with dextrose, albuterol, and potassium removal follow immediately.
- **원리**: <b>Why calcium comes first</b>: hyperkalemia raises the resting membrane potential (less negative), which inactivates fast sodium channels and slows conduction — hence the loss of P waves and the widening QRS that can progress to a sine wave, ventricular fibrillation, or asystole. Calcium raises the threshold potential, restoring the gap between resting and threshold potentials so the myocardium can conduct normally again. It acts in 1 to 3 minutes but lasts only 30 to 60 minutes.<br> <b>Why the others come second</b>: insulin (with dextrose) and β2 agonists drive potassium into cells through Na+/K+-ATPase, lowering serum potassium by about 0.5 to 1.2 mEq/L over 15 to 30 minutes. Binders and diuretics or dialysis remove potassium from the body over hours. When the ECG already shows conduction changes, minutes matter, so the fastest protector comes first.<br> <b>Cause here</b>: ACE inhibitor plus mineralocorticoid antagonist reduces aldosterone effect, and declining kidney function reduces excretion.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Sodium bicarbonate would be most useful if severe metabolic acidosis were driving the hyperkalemia; with bicarbonate of 19 mEq/L its potassium-lowering effect is small and slow.
  - (C) Insulin with dextrose would be the first treatment if the ECG showed no conduction changes; here it is given right after calcium because it takes 15 to 30 minutes to act.
  - (D) Nebulized albuterol is an adjunct that shifts potassium into cells and works best with insulin; it does not protect the heart within minutes and may cause tachycardia.
  - (E) Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate removes potassium in the gut over hours and would be correct for chronic or mild hyperkalemia without ECG changes.
- **함정**: Choosing insulin with dextrose because it lowers potassium fastest — with a widened QRS, calcium is needed first to protect the myocardium.
- **학습목표**: 심전도 변화(뾰족한 T·넓은 QRS)가 있는 심한 고칼륨혈증에서 첫 처치가 칼슘 정주로 심근막을 안정시키는 것임을 알고, 칼륨을 세포 안으로 옮기는 처치와 순서를 구별한다

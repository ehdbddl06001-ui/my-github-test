## 문제
A 58-year-old man asks his physician whether he should stop drinking coffee after reading about a cohort study of 6,000 adults followed for 10 years. In the study, the crude relative risk of lung cancer among daily coffee drinkers compared with non-drinkers was 2.1 (95% CI, 1.6-2.8). Daily coffee drinkers were three times more likely than non-drinkers to smoke cigarettes. When the analysis was stratified by smoking status, the relative risk for coffee drinking was 1.0 (95% CI, 0.7-1.4) among smokers and 1.0 (95% CI, 0.5-1.9) among nonsmokers. Losses to follow-up were less than 3% in both groups. He has smoked 1 pack of cigarettes daily for 30 years. Which of the following best explains the crude association between coffee drinking and lung cancer?

- A. Selection bias from loss to follow-up
- B. Reverse causation
- C. Confounding by smoking
- D. Effect modification by smoking
- E. Recall bias

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: Smoking is associated with coffee drinking and is an independent cause of lung cancer, and it is not on the causal path from coffee to cancer. After stratifying by smoking, the relative risks in both strata are the same (1.0) and differ from the crude estimate (2.1). Equal stratum-specific estimates that differ from the crude estimate are the signature of confounding; the apparent association of coffee with lung cancer is explained by smoking.
- **원리**: <b>Three conditions for confounding</b>: a confounder is (1) associated with the exposure, (2) an independent risk factor for the outcome, and (3) not an intermediate step between exposure and outcome. Smoking meets all three here: coffee drinkers smoke more, smoking causes lung cancer, and coffee does not cause smoking-induced cancer through smoking.<br> <b>How stratification shows it</b>: stratifying holds the third variable constant. If the stratum-specific relative risks are similar to each other but different from the crude relative risk, the crude estimate was distorted by the third variable — confounding. The appropriate summary is an adjusted (Mantel-Haenszel) estimate, here about 1.0.<br> <b>Contrast with effect modification</b>: if the stratum-specific estimates differ from each other (for example 3.0 in smokers and 1.0 in nonsmokers), the effect of the exposure truly depends on the third variable. That is a real biologic finding to report separately for each stratum, not a bias to remove.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Confounding (answer)</th><th>Effect modification (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Stratum-specific RRs</td><td><b>Similar to each other</b> (1.0 and 1.0)</td><td>Different from each other (e.g., 3.0 vs 1.0)</td></tr> <tr><td>Versus crude RR</td><td>Differ from crude (2.1)</td><td>Crude lies between them</td></tr> <tr><td>Nature</td><td>Bias — a distortion to be removed</td><td>Real interaction — to be described</td></tr> <tr><td>Reporting</td><td>Adjusted (Mantel-Haenszel) estimate</td><td>Separate estimate for each stratum</td></tr> </tbody></table> Compare the strata with each other first: same → confounding; different → effect modification.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Selection bias from loss to follow-up would be correct if many participants were lost and the losses differed by both exposure and outcome; losses here were under 3%.
  - (B) Reverse causation would be correct if early, undiagnosed disease changed the exposure, such as patients with preclinical cancer drinking less coffee.
  - (D) Effect modification would be correct if the relative risks differed between strata, for example 3.0 among smokers and 1.0 among nonsmokers.
  - (E) Recall bias would be correct in a case-control study in which cases remember past exposures differently from controls; this cohort measured exposure before the outcome.
- **함정**: Seeing results that change across smoking strata and calling it effect modification — the two strata agree with each other and differ only from the crude estimate.
- **학습목표**: 층화 분석에서 층별 상대위험도가 서로 같고 조(粗) 상대위험도와 다르면 교란, 층별 상대위험도가 서로 다르면 효과변경임을 구별한다

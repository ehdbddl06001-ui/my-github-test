## 문제
A 31-year-old woman is referred before a scheduled laparoscopic cholecystectomy because preoperative coagulation tests are abnormal. She has had two miscarriages at 9 and 11 weeks' gestation. She has never had abnormal bleeding, including after a dental extraction last year, and her menstrual periods are normal. She takes no medications. Platelet count is 210,000/mm³. Prothrombin time is 12 seconds (INR 1.0), and activated partial thromboplastin time is 52 seconds (reference 25–35). A 1:1 mixing study with normal plasma gives an aPTT of 49 seconds immediately and 50 seconds after 2 hours of incubation at 37°C. Factor VIII activity is 95%. Which of the following is the most likely cause of the prolonged aPTT?

- A. Lupus anticoagulant
- B. Acquired factor VIII inhibitor
- C. Factor XII deficiency
- D. von Willebrand disease
- E. Unfractionated heparin contamination

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: An isolated prolonged aPTT that does not correct immediately with 1:1 normal plasma indicates an inhibitor rather than a factor deficiency. The inhibitor is not time dependent (no further prolongation after incubation), factor VIII activity is normal, and she has no bleeding history but two pregnancy losses — features of a lupus anticoagulant, an antiphospholipid antibody that prolongs phospholipid-dependent clotting tests in vitro while predisposing to thrombosis and pregnancy morbidity in vivo. Confirmation uses a second phospholipid-dependent test such as the dilute Russell viper venom time with phospholipid neutralization, repeated after 12 weeks.
- **원리**: <b>Why mixing separates deficiency from inhibitor</b>: a clotting test stays normal as long as each factor is above about 40–50% of normal. Mixing patient plasma 1:1 with normal plasma supplies at least 50% of every factor, so a deficiency corrects. If the aPTT stays prolonged, something in the patient's plasma is blocking clotting — an inhibitor.<br> <b>Why a lupus anticoagulant prolongs the test but causes clotting</b>: these antibodies bind phospholipid–protein complexes (β2-glycoprotein I, prothrombin). In the test tube, they compete with clotting factors for the limited phospholipid in the aPTT reagent, slowing clot formation. In the body, the same antibodies activate endothelial cells, platelets, and monocytes and interfere with protein C, promoting thrombosis and placental injury.<br> <b>Why incubation matters</b>: factor VIII autoantibodies bind slowly and are temperature dependent, so the aPTT is often near-normal immediately but lengthens after 1–2 hours at 37°C; a lupus anticoagulant acts immediately.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) An acquired factor VIII inhibitor would be correct if factor VIII activity were very low, the aPTT lengthened after incubation, and she had sudden large bruises or muscle bleeds.
  - (C) Factor XII deficiency would be correct if the aPTT were prolonged without bleeding but corrected fully with 1:1 mixing, because it is a deficiency rather than an inhibitor.
  - (D) von Willebrand disease would be correct with mucocutaneous bleeding, heavy menses, and a low factor VIII; mixing would correct any aPTT prolongation.
  - (E) Heparin contamination would be correct if blood had been drawn from a heparinized line; the thrombin time would be prolonged and correct with heparinase.
- **함정**: Choosing factor XII deficiency because she does not bleed — a deficiency corrects with mixing, whereas this aPTT does not.
- **학습목표**: PT 는 정상이고 aPTT 만 연장되었는데 1:1 혼합 검사에서 즉시 교정되지 않으며 출혈이 없고 임신 손실 병력이 있으면 루푸스 항응고인자로 보고 응고인자 VIII 억제인자와 가른다

## 문제
A randomized controlled trial enrolls 4,000 adults with stable coronary artery disease and assigns half to a new antiplatelet drug and half to placebo. Over 3 years, a major cardiovascular event occurs in 160 of 2,000 patients in the placebo group and in 120 of 2,000 patients in the treatment group. Adherence and follow-up were similar in both groups. A 62-year-old man with stable coronary artery disease asks his physician how much the new drug would help him. Which of the following is the approximate number of patients who need to be treated for 3 years to prevent one major cardiovascular event?

- A. 50
- B. 25
- C. 100
- D. 4
- E. 8

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: The event rate is 160/2,000 = 8% with placebo and 120/2,000 = 6% with treatment. The absolute risk reduction is 8% − 6% = 2% (0.02). The number needed to treat is 1/ARR = 1/0.02 = 50 patients treated for 3 years to prevent one event. The relative risk reduction is 2%/8% = 25%, which describes the proportional effect but not how many patients must be treated.
- **원리**: <b>Why NNT uses the absolute difference</b>: the number needed to treat answers "if I treat N patients, how many events do I prevent?" Treating 100 patients prevents 8 − 6 = 2 events, so 50 patients prevent 1. That depends on the absolute difference between the groups, which in turn depends on the baseline risk.<br> <b>Why relative risk reduction can mislead</b>: the RRR (25%) stays roughly constant across populations, so it sounds the same whether the baseline risk is 8% or 0.8%. In a low-risk population with a 0.8% baseline event rate, the same 25% RRR gives an ARR of 0.2% and an NNT of 500. Reporting only the RRR makes small absolute benefits look large.<br> <b>Why the time frame must be stated</b>: an NNT is meaningful only with its duration — "50 over 3 years" — because event rates accumulate over time.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Measure</th><th style="width:38%">NNT = 1/ARR (answer)</th><th>Relative risk reduction (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Formula</td><td>1 / (8% − 6%) = <b>50</b></td><td>(8% − 6%) / 8% = 25%</td></tr> <tr><td>Depends on baseline risk</td><td><b>Yes</b></td><td>Largely no</td></tr> <tr><td>Answers</td><td>How many to treat to prevent one event</td><td>What fraction of events is prevented</td></tr> <tr><td>Common error</td><td>—</td><td>Reading 25% as "treat 25"</td></tr> </tbody></table> Subtract first (absolute), then invert — dividing first gives the relative effect.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) 25 is the relative risk reduction expressed as a percentage; it would be the NNT only if the absolute risk reduction were 4% (for example, 10% versus 6%).
  - (C) 100 would be the NNT if the absolute risk reduction were 1%, for example in a lower-risk population with 4% versus 3% event rates.
  - (D) 4 is the reciprocal of the relative risk reduction (1/0.25); it has no clinical meaning as a number needed to treat in this trial.
  - (E) 8 is the placebo event rate in percent; it would describe the baseline 3-year risk if the question asked about untreated patients.
- **함정**: Using the relative risk reduction (25%) as if it were the number to treat — NNT is the reciprocal of the absolute difference.
- **학습목표**: Calculate absolute risk reduction and number needed to treat from trial event rates, and distinguish them from relative risk reduction

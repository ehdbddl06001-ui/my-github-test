## 문제
A 45-year-old man comes to the physician for follow-up after treatment of Helicobacter pylori infection. Six weeks ago, upper endoscopy for epigastric pain showed a 6-mm duodenal ulcer, and biopsy of the gastric antrum was positive for H. pylori. He completed a 14-day course of bismuth quadruple therapy 5 weeks ago and has continued omeprazole 20 mg daily since then. His epigastric pain has resolved. He takes no other medications. His pulse is 72/min and blood pressure is 124/78 mm Hg. The abdomen is soft and nontender. Hemoglobin concentration is 14.2 g/dL. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step to confirm eradication?

- A. No testing is needed because symptoms have resolved
- B. Stop omeprazole for 2 weeks, then perform a urea breath test
- C. Perform a urea breath test today while continuing omeprazole
- D. Measure serum anti–H. pylori IgG antibodies
- E. Repeat upper endoscopy with antral biopsy now

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Every patient treated for H. pylori should have a test of cure with a urea breath test, stool antigen test, or biopsy at least 4 weeks after completing antibiotics. Proton pump inhibitors suppress the organism and cause false-negative results, so they should be held for 2 weeks before testing. He finished therapy 5 weeks ago but is still taking omeprazole; stopping it for 2 weeks and then performing a urea breath test is correct.
- **원리**: <b>How the urea breath test works</b>: H. pylori produces large amounts of <b>urease</b>. The patient swallows urea labeled with carbon-13; if the organism is present, urease splits it into ammonia and labeled CO2, which is absorbed and exhaled. The test detects <b>active infection</b>, so it depends on how many live bacteria are present at that moment.<br> <b>Why timing matters</b>: antibiotics, bismuth, and PPIs reduce the bacterial load without always killing every organism. Testing too soon — within 4 weeks of antibiotics or bismuth, or within 2 weeks of a PPI — can give a <b>false-negative</b> result from temporary suppression, and the surviving bacteria later regrow. PPIs also raise gastric pH, pushing organisms into the gastric body where they are sampled less efficiently.<br> <b>Why serology is useless here</b>: IgG antibodies persist for months to years after successful eradication, so a positive result cannot distinguish past from present infection.<br> <b>Why confirm at all</b>: eradication rates are only about 80 to 90% with rising resistance, and persistent infection keeps the ulcer recurrence and gastric cancer risk. If the patient needs acid suppression for symptoms during the washout, an H2-receptor antagonist can be used because it interferes less.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:26%">Feature</th><th style="width:37%">UBT after PPI washout (answer)</th><th>UBT today on PPI (closest rival)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Antibiotic/bismuth interval</td><td>≥ 4 weeks (5 weeks here)</td><td>≥ 4 weeks (5 weeks here)</td></tr> <tr><td>PPI interval</td><td><b>Held ≥ 2 weeks</b></td><td>Still taking</td></tr> <tr><td>Risk of false negative</td><td>Low</td><td><b>High — urease activity suppressed</b></td></tr> <tr><td>Result means</td><td>Reliable test of cure</td><td>A negative cannot be trusted</td></tr> </tbody></table> <b>The closest rival is testing today.</b> The antibiotic interval is already satisfied; the dividing line is <b>the ongoing PPI</b>. The same rule applies to the stool antigen test.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Resolution of symptoms does not prove eradication; treatment failure occurs in 10 to 20% and leaves the risk of ulcer recurrence and gastric cancer. Guidelines recommend a test of cure for every treated patient.
  - (C) Testing while he still takes omeprazole risks a false-negative result because PPIs suppress urease activity and bacterial load. It would be correct if he had stopped the PPI at least 2 weeks earlier.
  - (D) IgG antibodies remain positive for months to years after eradication, so serology cannot confirm cure. It is useful only for initial diagnosis in an untreated patient who has not recently taken PPIs.
  - (E) Repeat endoscopy is needed to confirm healing of gastric ulcers (to exclude cancer), not uncomplicated duodenal ulcers. It would be correct for a gastric ulcer, and biopsy would still require stopping the PPI.
- **함정**: The 4-week antibiotic interval is already met; the trap is the PPI he is still taking — hold it for 2 weeks first.
- **학습목표**: 헬리코박터 제균 치료 뒤 요소호기검사로 확인하되, 치료 종료 4주 뒤·양성자펌프억제제 2주 중단 뒤에 검사한다

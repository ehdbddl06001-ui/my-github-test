## 문제
A 31-year-old woman comes to the physician because she has not had a menstrual period for 7 months. Eight months ago, she underwent dilation and curettage for retained products of conception after a miscarriage at 10 weeks' gestation; the procedure was complicated by endometritis treated with antibiotics. Before that, her menses had occurred regularly every 28 days. She has no hot flashes, galactorrhea, headaches, or weight change, and she exercises moderately. Her BMI is 23 kg/m2. Pelvic examination shows no abnormalities. Urine pregnancy test is negative. Serum prolactin, TSH, and FSH concentrations are within the reference ranges. She has no withdrawal bleeding after a 10-day course of medroxyprogesterone or after a subsequent 21-day course of estrogen followed by progestin. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

- A. Polycystic ovary syndrome
- B. Sheehan syndrome
- C. Intrauterine adhesions
- D. Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea
- E. Primary ovarian insufficiency

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: Curettage of a recently pregnant uterus, especially with <b>endometritis</b>, can strip the basal layer of the endometrium and produce <b>intrauterine adhesions</b>. Her hormonal axis is intact (normal FSH, prolactin, TSH), yet <b>even estrogen followed by progestin produces no bleeding</b> — the defect is in the end organ, the endometrium, not the hormones. Hysteroscopy confirms and treats the adhesions.
- **원리**: <b>The logic of withdrawal tests</b>: Bleeding after a progestin course means the endometrium has been primed by enough estrogen and the outflow tract is open — the problem is anovulation (as in PCOS). No bleeding means either too little estrogen or a defective endometrium/outflow tract. Giving estrogen followed by progestin separates these: if bleeding then occurs, the problem was estrogen deficiency (hypothalamic, pituitary, or ovarian); if bleeding still does not occur, the endometrium itself cannot respond.<br> <b>Why curettage causes it</b>: The endometrium regenerates each month from the basal layer. Aggressive curettage of the soft, postpartum or postabortal uterus, particularly with infection, destroys the basal layer so that opposing raw surfaces fuse with fibrous bands. The cavity may be partly or wholly obliterated.<br> <b>Management</b>: Hysteroscopic adhesiolysis, often followed by estrogen to promote regrowth; fertility and recurrent miscarriage risk depend on the extent of scarring.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Intrauterine adhesions (answer)</th><th>Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>History</td><td>Curettage, postpartum or postabortal infection</td><td>Low weight, intense exercise, stress</td></tr> <tr><td>FSH / estrogen</td><td>Normal</td><td><b>Low-normal FSH, low estrogen</b></td></tr> <tr><td>Progestin challenge</td><td>No bleeding</td><td>No bleeding</td></tr> <tr><td>Estrogen-progestin challenge</td><td><b>No bleeding</b></td><td>Bleeding occurs</td></tr> </tbody></table> When the progestin challenge is negative, the estrogen-progestin challenge decides: bleeding means the hormones were missing; no bleeding means the uterus is the problem.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Polycystic ovary syndrome would be correct if she had hyperandrogenism, irregular cycles since menarche, and bleeding after the progestin challenge.
  - (B) Sheehan syndrome would be correct after massive postpartum hemorrhage with failure of lactation and low gonadotropins, TSH, and cortisol.
  - (D) Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea would be correct in a woman with low BMI or intense exercise who bleeds after the estrogen-progestin course.
  - (E) Primary ovarian insufficiency would be correct if FSH were repeatedly elevated above the menopausal range with hot flashes before age 40.
- **함정**: Assuming every negative progestin challenge means low estrogen — the estrogen-progestin challenge must be done before blaming the hormones.
- **학습목표**: Diagnose intrauterine adhesions (Asherman syndrome) when secondary amenorrhea follows uterine curettage and neither progestin nor estrogen-progestin withdrawal produces bleeding, and confirm with hysteroscopy

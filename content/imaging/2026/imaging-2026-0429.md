## 문제
A 58-year-old woman with schizophrenia is brought to the clinic by her sister because of involuntary mouth movements that have gradually worsened over 8 months. She has taken haloperidol 10 mg daily for 12 years, and her psychotic symptoms have been stable without hospitalization for 5 years. Six months ago, benztropine was added for the movements, and since then they have become more frequent. She is embarrassed to eat in public. She is alert and calm. There are repetitive lip smacking, puckering, and lateral jaw movements, and her tongue darts in and out of her mouth; the movements decrease when she is asked to tap her fingers. There is no rigidity, tremor, or bradykinesia, and her gait is normal. She does not report restlessness. Serum TSH, calcium, and copper concentrations are normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate pharmacotherapy?

- A. Increase the dose of haloperidol
- B. Intramuscular diphenhydramine
- C. Valbenazine
- D. Increase the dose of benztropine
- E. Propranolol

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: Choreoathetoid oro-buccal-lingual movements that began after years of dopamine-receptor blockade are tardive dyskinesia. Anticholinergic drugs such as benztropine worsen it, as happened here. For moderate to severe tardive dyskinesia that impairs function, a vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2) inhibitor — valbenazine or deutetrabenazine — is the recommended treatment, while the antipsychotic is continued or adjusted to keep psychosis stable.
- **원리**: <b>Why tardive dyskinesia appears late</b>: chronic D2-receptor blockade in the striatum leads to upregulated, supersensitive postsynaptic D2 receptors (and loss of striatal inhibitory interneurons). The indirect pathway is under-active and the direct pathway relatively over-active, producing hyperkinetic choreoathetoid movements — the opposite of acute drug-induced parkinsonism.<br> <b>Why anticholinergics make it worse</b>: in the striatum, acetylcholine and dopamine are in balance. Drugs like benztropine help dystonia and parkinsonism (relative cholinergic excess), but in tardive dyskinesia they further tilt the balance toward dopaminergic activity and increase the movements.<br> <b>Why VMAT2 inhibitors work</b>: VMAT2 loads dopamine into presynaptic vesicles. Inhibiting it depletes releasable dopamine, so the supersensitive receptors receive less stimulation. Valbenazine and deutetrabenazine reduce movement scores in randomized trials, with less depression and parkinsonism than tetrabenazine.<br> <b>Why not raise the antipsychotic</b>: a higher dose masks movements briefly by blocking more receptors but drives further supersensitivity.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Tardive dyskinesia — valbenazine (answer)</th><th>Drug-induced parkinsonism — benztropine (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Onset</td><td><b>Months to years</b> of D2 blockade</td><td>Days to weeks after starting or raising the dose</td></tr> <tr><td>Movements</td><td>Hyperkinetic oro-buccal-lingual chorea</td><td>Hypokinetic: rigidity, tremor, bradykinesia</td></tr> <tr><td>Anticholinergic</td><td><b>Worsens</b></td><td>Improves</td></tr> <tr><td>Striatal state</td><td>Relative dopaminergic excess</td><td>Dopaminergic deficit</td></tr> </tbody></table> Benztropine is the right drug for the wrong syndrome here — it relieves stiffness and tremor, not chorea.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Raising haloperidol temporarily suppresses movements by blocking more receptors but worsens the underlying supersensitivity; it would fit only acute psychotic relapse.
  - (B) Intramuscular diphenhydramine is the treatment for acute dystonia such as torticollis or oculogyric crisis within days of starting an antipsychotic.
  - (D) More benztropine would be correct for drug-induced parkinsonism or acute dystonia; in tardive dyskinesia it worsened the movements, as seen in this patient.
  - (E) Propranolol is the treatment for akathisia, an inner restlessness with an urge to move; she denies restlessness and has involuntary orofacial chorea instead.
- **함정**: Adding or increasing an anticholinergic because the problem is 'extrapyramidal' — tardive dyskinesia is worsened by anticholinergics and is treated with a VMAT2 inhibitor.
- **학습목표**: 항정신병약 장기 복용 뒤 생긴 입·혀의 무도무정위 운동을 지연이상운동증으로 판단하고, 항콜린제가 악화시킴을 알아 VMAT2 억제제를 고른다

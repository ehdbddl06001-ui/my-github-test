## 문제
A 29-year-old woman comes to the physician because of palpitations, heat intolerance, and a 3-kg weight loss for 3 weeks. She delivered a healthy infant 4 months ago and stopped breastfeeding 1 month ago. She has no neck pain. Pulse is 108/min and blood pressure is 126/74 mm Hg. The thyroid gland is slightly enlarged, nontender, and smooth. There is no proptosis. Serum TSH is less than 0.01 μU/mL, free T4 is 2.6 ng/dL (N = 0.9–1.7), and anti–thyroid peroxidase antibodies are positive. Thyrotropin receptor antibodies are negative. A 24-hour radioactive iodine uptake is 2% (N = 10–30). Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Prednisone
- B. Levothyroxine
- C. Propranolol
- D. Methimazole
- E. Radioactive iodine ablation

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: Painless thyrotoxicosis 4 months after delivery, a small nontender goiter, positive anti-TPO antibodies, negative TSH-receptor antibodies, and a very low radioactive iodine uptake indicate the thyrotoxic phase of postpartum thyroiditis. Excess hormone comes from release of stored hormone from an inflamed gland, not new synthesis, so antithyroid drugs do not help; a β-blocker controls symptoms until the phase resolves.
- **원리**: <b>Leak versus overproduction</b>: in Graves disease, TSH-receptor antibodies drive the gland to make new hormone, so it traps iodine avidly (high uptake). In thyroiditis, lymphocytic inflammation breaks follicles and preformed hormone leaks out; the stimulated TSH falls, the gland stops trapping iodine, and uptake is low.<br> <b>Why methimazole is useless here</b>: thionamides block thyroid peroxidase — the step that makes new hormone. When hormone is merely leaking from a damaged store, there is nothing for them to block, and the leak ends on its own over weeks.<br> <b>Why the postpartum timing matters</b>: immune tolerance in pregnancy rebounds after delivery; women with anti-TPO antibodies have a high risk of a thyrotoxic phase at 1–4 months and a hypothyroid phase at 4–8 months. TSH should be rechecked, and some develop permanent hypothyroidism.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Postpartum thyroiditis (answer: propranolol)</th><th>Postpartum Graves disease (methimazole)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Radioactive iodine uptake</td><td><b>Low (2%)</b> — hormone leaking</td><td>High and diffuse — hormone being made</td></tr> <tr><td>TSH-receptor antibodies</td><td>Negative</td><td>Positive</td></tr> <tr><td>Eye or skin signs</td><td>None</td><td>Ophthalmopathy, pretibial myxedema possible</td></tr> <tr><td>Course</td><td>Resolves in weeks; may turn hypothyroid</td><td>Persists without treatment</td></tr> </tbody></table> Both can appear months after delivery; uptake and TRAb decide which one it is. (Radioactive iodine uptake is avoided during breastfeeding — she has stopped.)
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Prednisone would be correct for subacute granulomatous thyroiditis with a painful, tender gland and high ESR when NSAIDs are not enough.
  - (B) Levothyroxine would be correct later if the hypothyroid phase is symptomatic or persistent, with an elevated TSH and low free T4.
  - (D) Methimazole would be correct if the uptake were high and diffuse with positive TSH-receptor antibodies, indicating postpartum Graves disease.
  - (E) Radioactive iodine ablation would be correct for Graves disease or a toxic nodule with high uptake; with 2% uptake the gland cannot take it up.
- **함정**: Treating every thyrotoxicosis with methimazole — a low uptake means hormone is leaking, not being made, so only symptom control is needed.
- **학습목표**: 출산 뒤 수개월 내 통증 없는 갑상샘중독증에서 방사성 요오드 섭취율이 낮으면 산후갑상샘염으로, 항갑상샘제 대신 베타차단제로 증상을 조절한다

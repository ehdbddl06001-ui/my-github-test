## 문제
A 9-month-old boy is brought to the emergency department because of episodes of inconsolable crying with drawing up of his legs for 12 hours. The episodes last a few minutes and recur every 15 to 20 minutes; between episodes, he is quiet and sleepy. He has vomited twice, and his last diaper contained stool mixed with blood and mucus. Temperature is 37.4°C (99.3°F), pulse is 140/min, and blood pressure is 88/54 mm Hg. Capillary refill time is 2 seconds. The abdomen is soft, with a sausage-shaped mass in the right upper quadrant; there is no guarding or rebound tenderness. Ultrasonography of the abdomen shows a target-shaped mass. Intravenous fluids are started. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Technetium-99m pertechnetate scan
- B. Air enema reduction under imaging guidance
- C. Emergency exploratory laparotomy
- D. CT scan of the abdomen with contrast
- E. Nasogastric decompression and observation

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Paroxysmal colicky pain with lethargy between episodes, currant-jelly-like stool, a right upper quadrant sausage-shaped mass, and an ultrasonographic target sign establish ileocolic intussusception. With a soft abdomen and stable circulation after fluids, the next step is nonoperative reduction by an imaging-guided air (or hydrostatic) enema, which is both diagnostic and therapeutic.
- **원리**: <b>How the bowel telescopes</b>: in infants 6–36 months, hypertrophied Peyer patches in the terminal ileum (often after a viral illness) act as a lead point; peristalsis drags the ileum into the colon. The trapped mesentery is compressed — veins first, so the bowel wall swells and oozes blood and mucus (<b>currant-jelly stool</b>), and later arterial supply fails.<br> <b>Why enema first</b>: pressure from air or contrast pushed retrograde from the rectum pushes the intussusceptum back; it succeeds in roughly 80–90% when done early, avoiding laparotomy. Air is preferred because perforation spills less material than barium.<br> <b>Why some need surgery</b>: once the bowel is necrotic or perforated, pressure reduction is dangerous — peritonitis, free air, or shock that does not respond to fluids are contraindications, and older children often have a pathologic lead point (Meckel diverticulum, polyp, lymphoma) that needs resection.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Air enema reduction (answer)</th><th>Emergency laparotomy (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Abdomen</td><td><b>Soft, no peritoneal signs</b></td><td>Guarding, rebound — peritonitis</td></tr> <tr><td>Circulation</td><td>Stable or responds to fluids</td><td>Shock despite resuscitation</td></tr> <tr><td>Imaging</td><td>Target sign, no free air</td><td>Free air (perforation)</td></tr> <tr><td>Other trigger</td><td>—</td><td>Failed or repeatedly recurrent enema reduction</td></tr> </tbody></table> Enema is the default; surgery is chosen by the presence of peritonitis, perforation, shock, or enema failure.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) A technetium-99m pertechnetate scan is correct for painless rectal bleeding in a toddler when a Meckel diverticulum with ectopic gastric mucosa is suspected.
  - (C) Emergency laparotomy becomes correct if there is peritonitis, free intraperitoneal air, shock unresponsive to fluids, or failed enema reduction.
  - (D) CT is not needed when ultrasonography already shows a target sign; it may help in older children when a pathologic lead point such as lymphoma is suspected.
  - (E) Nasogastric decompression with observation suits uncomplicated adhesive small-bowel obstruction, not an intussusception that will not reduce on its own.
- **함정**: Going straight to surgery because of bloody stool — blood alone does not mean necrosis; a soft abdomen and stable circulation allow enema reduction.
- **학습목표**: 영아의 발작성 복통·혈액 섞인 점액변·소시지 모양 종괴와 초음파 표적 징후로 장중첩증을 진단하고, 복막염·천공·쇼크가 없으면 영상 유도 공기 관장으로 정복한다

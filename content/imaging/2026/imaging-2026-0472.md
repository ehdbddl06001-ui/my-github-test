## 문제
A 24-year-old woman comes to the emergency department because of pain, redness, tearing, and sensitivity to light in her right eye for 1 day. She wears soft contact lenses and often sleeps in them. She has no history of eye surgery or cold sores. Visual acuity is 20/60 in the right eye and 20/20 in the left eye. Examination of the right eye shows conjunctival injection most prominent around the limbus and a 2-mm white corneal infiltrate with an overlying epithelial defect that stains with fluorescein. No dendritic pattern is seen. There are a few cells in the anterior chamber and no hypopyon. The contact lens is removed, and corneal scrapings are sent for Gram stain and culture. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Topical fluoroquinolone drops every hour
- B. Eye patching for 24 hours
- C. Topical corticosteroid drops
- D. Oral acyclovir
- E. Topical erythromycin ointment

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: A contact lens wearer who sleeps in lenses has a white corneal infiltrate with an overlying epithelial defect — bacterial keratitis until proven otherwise, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa is the organism to cover. After scrapings, start an intensive antipseudomonal topical antibiotic (a fluoroquinolone such as moxifloxacin, initially every hour) and arrange ophthalmology follow-up within 24 hours. Patching is contraindicated.
- **원리**: <b>Why contact lenses change the organism</b>: overnight wear starves the epithelium of oxygen, causes microscopic breaks, and lets biofilm on the lens and case seed the cornea. Pseudomonas aeruginosa — which can melt the cornea within a day or two — is the leading cause; Acanthamoeba is a rarer cause linked to tap water.<br> <b>Why "infiltrate" matters</b>: a simple abrasion is a clear epithelial defect. A white or gray opacity in the stroma beneath it means white cells have entered — an ulcer (keratitis), which threatens vision and needs culture and frequent antibiotic drops.<br> <b>Why no patch</b>: a patch creates a warm, closed, low-oxygen space where Pseudomonas grows faster and hides worsening. Corticosteroids are withheld at first because they slow epithelial healing and worsen fungal, herpetic, or uncontrolled bacterial infection.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Contact-lens keratitis (answer)</th><th>Non-lens corneal abrasion (patching / erythromycin)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Cornea</td><td><b>White stromal infiltrate + epithelial defect</b></td><td>Clear epithelial defect only</td></tr> <tr><td>Antibiotic</td><td>Antipseudomonal fluoroquinolone, hourly at first</td><td>Erythromycin ointment or similar, few times daily</td></tr> <tr><td>Patch</td><td>Contraindicated</td><td>Not needed, but not dangerous in small abrasions</td></tr> </tbody></table> Any contact-lens wearer with an abrasion gets antipseudomonal coverage and no patch — even before an infiltrate appears.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Patching was once used for small traumatic abrasions in non-lens wearers; in lens wearers or with an infiltrate it promotes Pseudomonas growth.
  - (C) Topical corticosteroids may be added by an ophthalmologist after cultures and clinical improvement on antibiotics, never as the first treatment.
  - (D) Oral acyclovir treats herpes simplex or zoster keratitis, suggested by a dendritic ulcer, vesicles, or prior cold sores — none are present.
  - (E) Topical erythromycin ointment is reasonable for a simple abrasion in a non-lens wearer, but it does not cover Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
- **함정**: Treating it like an ordinary abrasion (patch or erythromycin) — a contact-lens wearer with an infiltrate needs antipseudomonal drops and no patch.
- **학습목표**: 콘택트렌즈 착용자의 각막 침윤과 상피 결손을 세균 각막염(녹농균 포함)으로 보고, 가리개를 하지 않고 녹농균을 덮는 국소 항생제를 자주 점안한다

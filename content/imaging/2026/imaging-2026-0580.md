## 문제
A 17-year-old girl is brought to the physician because she has never had a menstrual period. She has no cyclic pelvic pain. She is on the school swim team and has had normal growth. Her height is 165 cm (5 ft 5 in), and BMI is 21 kg/m2. Breast and pubic hair development are Tanner stage 5. Pelvic examination shows normal external genitalia and a short vagina ending in a blind pouch; the cervix cannot be seen. Serum follicle-stimulating hormone and prolactin concentrations are within the reference ranges, and serum total testosterone concentration is within the reference range for adult women. Pelvic ultrasonography shows normal-appearing ovaries and no uterus. Further evaluation of this patient is most likely to show which of the following?

- A. Inguinal masses containing testicular tissue
- B. Elevated serum luteinizing hormone with low estradiol
- C. Hematocolpos on pelvic MRI
- D. Unilateral renal agenesis
- E. 46,XY karyotype

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: D

- **정답 핵심**: Normal breast development and normal FSH show functioning ovaries, and Tanner 5 pubic hair with a female-range testosterone shows normal androgen production and action. Absence of the uterus and cervix with a short, blind vagina therefore indicates Müllerian agenesis (Mayer-Rokitansky-Küster-Hauser syndrome) with a 46,XX karyotype. Because the Müllerian ducts develop in close association with the mesonephric (Wolffian) ducts that induce the kidney, renal anomalies, most often unilateral renal agenesis, occur in roughly a third of patients, so renal ultrasonography is part of the evaluation; skeletal anomalies are also common.
- **원리**: <b>Why the ovaries are normal but the uterus is absent</b>: The ovaries come from the gonadal ridge and germ cells, whereas the uterus, cervix, and upper vagina come from the paired paramesonephric (Müllerian) ducts. In Müllerian agenesis, the ducts fail to develop or fuse, so ovarian estrogen drives normal breast development and ovulation, but there is no uterus to menstruate.<br> <b>Why the kidneys are involved</b>: The Müllerian ducts form alongside and depend on the mesonephric ducts, which also give rise to the ureteric bud. A shared disturbance of intermediate mesoderm explains associated unilateral renal agenesis, pelvic kidney, and vertebral anomalies.<br> <b>Why androgen insensitivity looks similar but differs</b>: In 46,XY complete androgen insensitivity, testes secrete anti-Müllerian hormone, so the uterus is also absent and the vagina is short. But testosterone is in the male range, and because androgen receptors do not work, pubic and axillary hair are sparse.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Müllerian agenesis (answer)</th><th>Complete androgen insensitivity (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Karyotype, gonad</td><td>46,XX, normal ovaries</td><td>46,XY, testes (often inguinal)</td></tr> <tr><td>Pubic and axillary hair</td><td><b>Normal (Tanner 5)</b></td><td>Sparse or absent</td></tr> <tr><td>Testosterone</td><td>Female range</td><td>Male range or higher</td></tr> <tr><td>Associated finding</td><td>Renal and skeletal anomalies</td><td>Gonadal malignancy risk</td></tr> </tbody></table> Both lack a uterus with a blind vagina; pubic hair and the testosterone level separate them.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Inguinal masses containing testicular tissue would be correct in complete androgen insensitivity, where testes often lie in the inguinal canals or labia.
  - (B) Elevated LH with low estradiol would be correct for primary ovarian insufficiency such as Turner syndrome, which shows absent breast development and high FSH.
  - (C) Hematocolpos would be correct for imperforate hymen or transverse vaginal septum, which present with cyclic pelvic pain and a uterus present.
  - (E) A 46,XY karyotype would be correct if pubic and axillary hair were sparse and serum testosterone were in the male range, indicating androgen insensitivity.
- **함정**: Choosing a 46,XY karyotype because the uterus is absent — Tanner 5 pubic hair and female-range testosterone show normal androgen action, pointing to Müllerian agenesis.

## 문제
A 45-year-old woman asks her physician about a new blood test for a cancer that has a prevalence of 1% among women of her age in the general population. In a validation study, the test had a sensitivity of 90% and a specificity of 95%. She has no symptoms and no family history of cancer. Physical examination shows no abnormalities. She undergoes the test, and the result is positive. Which of the following is closest to the probability that she has the cancer?

- A. 99.9%
- B. 15%
- C. 47%
- D. 90%
- E. 95%

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Take 10,000 women: 100 have the cancer and 9,900 do not. The test detects 90 of the 100 (true positives) and is falsely positive in 5% of the 9,900, or 495 women. Of 585 positive results, 90 are true, so the positive predictive value is 90/585 ≈ 15%.
- **원리**: <b>Why a good test can give a weak positive</b>: sensitivity and specificity describe the test within people already known to have or not have the disease; predictive values describe what a result means for the person in front of you, and they depend on how common the disease is. When prevalence is 1%, the healthy group is 99 times larger than the diseased group, so even a 5% false-positive rate generates far more false positives (495) than the 90 true positives.<br> <b>Bayes' theorem in odds form</b>: pretest odds 1:99 × positive likelihood ratio (sensitivity/(1 − specificity) = 0.90/0.05 = 18) = posttest odds 18:99 ≈ 0.18, which is a probability of 0.18/1.18 ≈ 15%.<br> <b>What changes it</b>: raising prevalence (testing a high-risk group) or raising specificity raises the positive predictive value; sensitivity mainly governs the negative predictive value. That is why screening tests with modest PPV are followed by a more specific confirmatory test.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Measure</th><th style="width:39%">Positive predictive value (answer, ≈15%)</th><th>Sensitivity (closest distractor, 90%)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Question it answers</td><td>Given a positive test, how likely is disease?</td><td>Given disease, how likely is a positive test?</td></tr> <tr><td>Formula</td><td>TP / (TP + FP) = 90 / 585</td><td>TP / (TP + FN) = 90 / 100</td></tr> <tr><td>Depends on prevalence?</td><td><b>Yes</b> — falls as prevalence falls</td><td>No — a property of the test</td></tr> </tbody></table> A patient with a positive result is asking about PPV; the test brochure reports sensitivity.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) 99.9% is the negative predictive value (9,405 of 9,415 negatives are truly disease-free) and would be correct if her result had been negative.
  - (C) 47% would be the PPV if prevalence were about 5% (450 true vs 475 false positives per 10,000), as in a higher-risk population.
  - (D) 90% is the sensitivity — the probability of a positive test among women who have the cancer — and would be correct if the question asked that.
  - (E) 95% is the specificity — the probability of a negative test among women without the cancer — and would answer that question instead.
- **함정**: Equating the test's 90% sensitivity with the chance that a positive patient has the disease — at 1% prevalence false positives outnumber true positives.
- **학습목표**: 유병률·민감도·특이도로 양성예측도를 계산하고, 유병률이 낮으면 특이도가 높아도 양성예측도가 낮음을 이해한다

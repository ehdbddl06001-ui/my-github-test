## 문제
A 34-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of progressive weakness for 5 days. It began with tingling in his feet and then weakness of both legs that has ascended to his hips; since this morning he cannot walk without assistance. Two weeks ago, he had a self-limited episode of diarrhea. Temperature is 36.9°C, pulse is 92/min, and blood pressure is 128/76 mm Hg. Strength is 3/5 in the hip flexors and 4/5 in the hands. Deep tendon reflexes are absent throughout. Sensation to vibration is mildly decreased in the toes. Forced vital capacity is 2.8 L (65% of predicted), and he can swallow without difficulty. Cerebrospinal fluid shows 2 cells/μL and protein 120 mg/dL. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

- A. Intravenous immune globulin
- B. Intravenous methylprednisolone
- C. Pyridostigmine
- D. Plasma exchange followed by intravenous immune globulin
- E. Supportive care and observation only

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: Ascending symmetric weakness with areflexia after a diarrheal illness, with an elevated CSF protein and normal cell count (albuminocytologic dissociation), is Guillain-Barré syndrome. He can no longer walk unaided, which is the usual threshold for disease-modifying treatment. Intravenous immune globulin (2 g/kg over 5 days) or plasma exchange shortens the time to recovery when started within 2 weeks of onset; IVIG is easier to give. Glucocorticoids do not help, and giving plasma exchange followed by IVIG adds no benefit over either alone.
- **원리**: <b>What goes wrong</b>: In Guillain-Barré syndrome, an infection (often Campylobacter jejuni) induces antibodies that cross-react with gangliosides on peripheral nerve myelin or axons (molecular mimicry). Complement and macrophages strip myelin from nerve roots and proximal nerves, so conduction slows or blocks. Roots are affected first, which explains early areflexia and the rise in CSF protein without inflammatory cells.<br> <b>Why IVIG or plasma exchange</b>: Plasma exchange removes the pathogenic antibodies and complement; IVIG is thought to neutralize antibodies, block Fc receptors, and inhibit complement. Both, started within about 2 weeks, speed recovery to equal degrees. Because the inflammation is antibody- and complement-driven rather than T-cell steroid-responsive, glucocorticoids given alone have not improved outcome in trials.<br> <b>Why monitor breathing</b>: The phrenic nerves and bulbar nerves can be involved. Forced vital capacity below about 20 mL/kg, rapid progression, bulbar weakness, or autonomic instability predict the need for mechanical ventilation and admission to an intensive care unit.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Intravenous methylprednisolone would be correct for chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy, whose weakness keeps progressing for more than 8 weeks.
  - (C) Pyridostigmine would be correct for myasthenia gravis, with fatigable ptosis and diplopia, normal reflexes, and no sensory symptoms.
  - (D) Sequential plasma exchange followed by IVIG would be correct only if trials had shown added benefit; they did not, so either one alone is preferred.
  - (E) Supportive care alone would be correct for a mildly affected patient who can still walk unaided and is improving more than 2 weeks after onset.
- **함정**: Treating an inflammatory neuropathy with steroids — they help CIDP but not Guillain-Barré syndrome.

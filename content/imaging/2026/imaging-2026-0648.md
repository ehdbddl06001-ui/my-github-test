## 문제
A 72-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department because of a 2-day history of fever, chills, and right upper quadrant abdominal pain. She has had dark urine since yesterday. She has a history of gallstones. Her temperature is 39.1°C, pulse is 108/min, and blood pressure is 104/66 mm Hg. She is alert and oriented. The sclerae are icteric, and there is tenderness in the right upper quadrant without guarding. Leukocyte count is 16,800/mm3. Serum total bilirubin is 4.6 mg/dL, alkaline phosphatase is 420 U/L, ALT is 210 U/L, lipase is 40 U/L, and creatinine is 0.9 mg/dL. Ultrasonography shows gallstones and a common bile duct dilated to 11 mm. Intravenous fluids and piperacillin-tazobactam are begun. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Laparoscopic cholecystectomy within 24 hours
- B. Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography before any intervention
- C. Hepatobiliary iminodiacetic acid scan
- D. Continue antibiotics alone for 14 days
- E. Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography within 24 to 48 hours

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: Fever with chills, jaundice, and right upper quadrant pain (Charcot triad) with cholestatic liver tests and a dilated common bile duct in a patient with gallstones indicate acute cholangitis from an obstructing duct stone. Antibiotics do not penetrate an obstructed duct well, so the infected bile must be drained. With tachycardia and borderline blood pressure but normal mentation and creatinine, this is moderate cholangitis, for which ERCP drainage within 24–48 hours is recommended (urgently if she does not respond).
- **원리**: <b>Why drainage, not antibiotics alone</b>: Cholangitis needs two things — bacteria in bile and raised pressure in the duct. When pressure exceeds about 20–25 cm H2O, bacteria and endotoxin reflux from bile into hepatic veins and lymphatics (cholangiovenous reflux), causing bacteremia. Antibiotic secretion into bile also falls when the duct is obstructed. Only relieving the obstruction lowers the pressure and stops the reflux.<br> <b>Why ERCP</b>: ERCP both confirms the stone and treats it — sphincterotomy and stone extraction, or a stent if the patient is too unstable. Percutaneous transhepatic drainage is the alternative if ERCP fails or anatomy prevents it.<br> <b>Timing by severity (Tokyo guidelines)</b>: severe (organ dysfunction — hypotension needing vasopressors, confusion, creatinine > 2, INR > 1.5, platelets < 100,000) → drainage as soon as possible; moderate (e.g., WBC > 12,000, fever ≥ 39°C, age ≥ 75, bilirubin ≥ 5, hypoalbuminemia) → early drainage; mild → may start with antibiotics. Cholecystectomy follows later in the same admission to prevent recurrence.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Laparoscopic cholecystectomy within 24 hours would be correct for acute calculous cholecystitis without duct obstruction; here it comes after ERCP.
  - (B) Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography would be appropriate when duct stones are only of intermediate probability; this dilated duct with jaundice is high probability.
  - (C) A hepatobiliary iminodiacetic acid scan would confirm acute cholecystitis when ultrasonography is equivocal, but it does not drain an obstructed duct.
  - (D) Antibiotics alone would be acceptable initially only for mild cholangitis responding promptly; her fever 39.1°C and leukocytosis make this moderate disease.
- **함정**: Treating every gallstone emergency with cholecystectomy — jaundice with a dilated duct means the obstruction must be drained first.
- **학습목표**: Recognize acute cholangitis from fever, jaundice, and right upper quadrant pain with a dilated common bile duct, and choose biliary drainage by ERCP after antibiotics and fluids

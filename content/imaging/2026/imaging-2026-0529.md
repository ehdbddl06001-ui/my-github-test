## 문제
A 58-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of fever, productive cough, and right-sided pleuritic chest pain for 4 days. Temperature is 38.6°C, pulse is 104/min, and respirations are 22/min. Dullness to percussion and decreased breath sounds are present at the right lung base. Chest x-ray shows right lower lobe consolidation with a small to moderate free-flowing pleural effusion. Diagnostic thoracentesis yields slightly cloudy fluid. Pleural fluid/serum protein ratio is 0.7 and pleural fluid/serum LDH ratio is 0.8. Pleural fluid pH is 7.38, glucose is 92 mg/dL, and the white blood cell count is 6,500/mm3 with 85% neutrophils. Gram stain shows no organisms. Which of the following is the most likely cause of the effusion?

- A. Uncomplicated parapneumonic effusion
- B. Complicated parapneumonic effusion
- C. Malignant pleural effusion
- D. Tuberculous pleural effusion
- E. Heart failure-related transudate

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: The fluid is an exudate by Light's criteria (protein ratio > 0.5, LDH ratio > 0.6). With pneumonia, a neutrophil-predominant exudate, pH 7.38, normal glucose, and a negative Gram stain, the effusion is an uncomplicated parapneumonic effusion that usually resolves with antibiotics alone. Pus, a positive Gram stain, pH < 7.20, or low glucose would make it complicated or an empyema needing drainage.
- **원리**: <b>Why Light's criteria come first</b>: A transudate forms when hydrostatic or oncotic pressure changes push fluid across a normal pleura (heart failure, cirrhosis), so the fluid has little protein and LDH. An exudate means the pleura itself is inflamed or invaded and leaks protein and cells — infection, cancer, tuberculosis. One positive criterion (protein ratio > 0.5, LDH ratio > 0.6, or LDH > 2/3 of the upper normal serum value) makes an exudate.<br> <b>Why pH and glucose decide drainage</b>: When bacteria invade the pleural space, neutrophils and bacteria consume glucose and produce lactate and CO2 through anaerobic glycolysis. The pleural pH falls below 7.20 and glucose below 60 mg/dL — the fluid is now <b>complicated</b> and loculates unless drained. Normal pH and glucose with a sterile Gram stain mean the inflammation is reactive, and antibiotics for the pneumonia are enough.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Uncomplicated parapneumonic (answer)</th><th>Empyema / complicated (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Appearance</td><td>Clear to slightly cloudy</td><td><b>Frank pus</b> or loculated</td></tr> <tr><td>pH / glucose</td><td>≥ 7.20 / ≥ 60 mg/dL</td><td>&lt; 7.20 / &lt; 60 mg/dL (bacterial metabolism)</td></tr> <tr><td>Gram stain, culture</td><td>Negative</td><td>Often positive</td></tr> <tr><td>Management</td><td>Antibiotics alone</td><td>Chest tube drainage ± fibrinolytics or surgery</td></tr> </tbody></table> Both are neutrophilic exudates next to pneumonia — the pH, glucose, and Gram stain separate them.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) A complicated effusion or empyema would be correct with frank pus or a positive Gram stain, or pH below 7.20 and glucose below 60 mg/dL.
  - (C) Malignant effusion would be correct with a lymphocyte-predominant, often bloody exudate, weight loss, and malignant cells on cytology.
  - (D) Tuberculous pleuritis would be correct with a lymphocytic exudate (often > 80% lymphocytes), high adenosine deaminase, and a subacute course.
  - (E) Heart failure would be correct if the fluid were a transudate (protein ratio ≤ 0.5, LDH ratio ≤ 0.6), often bilateral with cardiomegaly.
- **함정**: Calling every effusion beside pneumonia an empyema — pH, glucose, and Gram stain decide whether drainage is needed.
- **학습목표**: Light 기준으로 삼출액을 확인하고 호중구 우세·정상 pH 와 포도당으로 단순 부폐렴삼출을 다른 삼출액과 가른다

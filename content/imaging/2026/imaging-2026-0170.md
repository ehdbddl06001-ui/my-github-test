## 문제
A 71-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of sudden, painless loss of vision in his right eye that began 90 minutes ago while he was reading. He has hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and a 40-pack-year smoking history. He has no headache, jaw claudication, or scalp tenderness. His pulse is 82/min and regular, and blood pressure is 156/90 mm Hg. Visual acuity is counting fingers in the right eye and 20/25 in the left eye. There is a right relative afferent pupillary defect. Fundoscopy of the right eye shows a pale, edematous retina with a cherry-red spot at the fovea. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate is 12 mm/h. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Outpatient carotid ultrasonography within 2 weeks
- B. Immediate referral to a stroke center for brain and vascular imaging
- C. Ocular massage and observation in the eye clinic
- D. Intravenous methylprednisolone
- E. Topical timolol and oral acetazolamide

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Sudden painless monocular vision loss with a relative afferent pupillary defect and a pale retina with a cherry-red spot is central retinal artery occlusion. It is a retinal stroke, usually embolic from the carotid artery or heart, and carries a high early risk of cerebral stroke. Guidelines recommend emergent transfer to a stroke center for brain MRI, vascular imaging, cardiac monitoring, and consideration of thrombolysis within the time window.
- **원리**: <b>The retina is brain tissue</b>: the central retinal artery is a branch of the ophthalmic artery, the first branch of the internal carotid. An embolus lodging there is an ischemic stroke of the retina, and the same sources — <b>carotid atherosclerosis, atrial fibrillation, cardiac emboli</b> — threaten the brain. Silent brain infarcts are found on MRI in about a quarter of patients, and the risk of a subsequent stroke is highest in the first week.<br> <b>Why the retina looks this way</b>: ischemic inner retinal layers swell and turn opaque white. The fovea has no inner layers and is nourished by the choroid, so the normal red choroid shows through — the <b>cherry-red spot</b>. The retina tolerates ischemia for only about 90 to 240 minutes.<br> <b>Therefore the priority is a stroke pathway</b>, not local eye maneuvers: urgent brain imaging, carotid and cardiac evaluation, and thrombolysis in selected patients within 4.5 hours. Traditional measures (ocular massage, lowering intraocular pressure) have not been shown to improve vision.<br> <b>Giant cell arteritis</b> must be excluded in older patients because it needs immediate steroids; no headache, jaw claudication, or elevated ESR makes it unlikely here.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:26%">Feature</th><th style="width:37%">Embolic CRAO (answer)</th><th>Arteritic CRAO — giant cell arteritis (closest rival)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Symptoms</td><td>Sudden painless loss, vascular risk factors</td><td>Headache, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, polymyalgia</td></tr> <tr><td>ESR/CRP</td><td><b>Normal (ESR 12 mm/h)</b></td><td>Markedly elevated</td></tr> <tr><td>Next step</td><td><b>Stroke center: MRI, vascular imaging, ± thrombolysis</b></td><td>Immediate high-dose steroids, then temporal artery biopsy</td></tr> </tbody></table> <b>The closest rival is IV methylprednisolone</b> for arteritic occlusion. The dividing line is <b>GCA symptoms and inflammatory markers</b>; without them, treat as an embolic stroke.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Carotid evaluation is necessary, but the risk of stroke is highest in the first days; waiting 2 weeks as an outpatient is too slow. It would be acceptable only for an old, incidental retinal embolus without symptoms.
  - (C) Ocular massage aims to dislodge the embolus but has not been shown to improve outcomes, and staying in the eye clinic delays the stroke evaluation. It is at most an adjunct performed while arranging emergent stroke-center care.
  - (D) Intravenous methylprednisolone is urgent when CRAO is caused by giant cell arteritis. It would be correct if he had jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, and a markedly elevated ESR or CRP.
  - (E) Lowering intraocular pressure with timolol and acetazolamide is appropriate for acute angle-closure glaucoma, which is painful with a red eye and mid-dilated pupil. For CRAO it has no proven benefit.
- **함정**: A cherry-red spot is an eye finding, but the emergency is a stroke — send the patient to the stroke pathway once arteritis is unlikely.
- **학습목표**: 중심망막동맥폐쇄를 알아보고 뇌졸중과 같은 응급으로 보아 즉시 뇌졸중센터 평가를 받게 한다

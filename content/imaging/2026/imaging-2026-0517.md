## 문제
A 19-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after he fainted while sprinting during a basketball game. He regained consciousness within a minute. His father died suddenly at age 40 years. Blood pressure is 124/76 mm Hg and pulse is 72/min. A grade 3/6 crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur is heard best at the left lower sternal border; it does not radiate to the carotid arteries. The murmur becomes louder when he performs the Valsalva maneuver and when he stands from a squatting position, and softer with sustained handgrip. No ejection click is heard. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

- A. Innocent flow murmur
- B. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
- C. Bicuspid aortic valve stenosis
- D. Mitral valve prolapse
- E. Ventricular septal defect

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Exertional syncope, a family history of sudden death at 40 years, and a systolic murmur at the left lower sternal border that gets louder with Valsalva and standing (less preload) and softer with handgrip (more afterload) point to dynamic left ventricular outflow obstruction from hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. A fixed aortic valve obstruction would get softer, not louder, when preload falls.
- **원리**: <b>Why preload changes the murmur</b>: In hypertrophic cardiomyopathy the thick septum and the anterior mitral leaflet (systolic anterior motion) narrow the outflow tract during systole. A <b>smaller</b> ventricle — less venous return with Valsalva or standing — brings the leaflet and septum closer, so the obstruction and the murmur increase. Squatting and handgrip enlarge the ventricle or raise afterload and widen the tract, so the murmur softens.<br> <b>Why aortic stenosis behaves the opposite way</b>: A stiff valve is a fixed opening. Less blood crossing it means less turbulence, so Valsalva softens the murmur.<br> <b>Why it matters</b>: Exertional syncope plus a family history of sudden death are risk markers for ventricular arrhythmia; the next steps are echocardiography, avoidance of competitive sport until evaluated, and screening of first-degree relatives.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Maneuver</th><th style="width:38%">Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (answer)</th><th>Aortic stenosis (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Valsalva / standing</td><td><b>Louder</b> (smaller LV, more obstruction)</td><td>Softer (less flow)</td></tr> <tr><td>Squatting / handgrip</td><td>Softer</td><td>Squatting louder; handgrip softer</td></tr> <tr><td>Location, radiation</td><td>Left lower sternal border, no carotid radiation</td><td>Right upper sternal border, to carotids; ejection click if bicuspid</td></tr> </tbody></table> Only two murmurs grow louder with Valsalva — HCM and mitral valve prolapse; the click and the apical location separate MVP.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) An innocent flow murmur would be correct in an asymptomatic person with a soft murmur that decreases on standing and no syncope or family history.
  - (C) Bicuspid aortic stenosis would be correct with an ejection click and a right upper sternal border murmur radiating to the carotids that softens with Valsalva.
  - (D) Mitral valve prolapse would be correct with a mid-systolic click and a late systolic apical murmur that starts earlier with Valsalva.
  - (E) A ventricular septal defect would be correct with a holosystolic murmur at the left lower sternal border that gets louder with handgrip.
- **함정**: Assuming every systolic ejection murmur in a fainting athlete is aortic stenosis — the Valsalva response decides.

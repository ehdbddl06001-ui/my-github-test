## 문제
A 56-year-old man comes to the physician because of swelling of both legs for 2 months. He has gained 6 kg during this period. He has no history of serious illness and takes no medications. He does not use nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Blood pressure is 128/78 mm Hg. There is 3+ pitting edema of the lower extremities. Serum albumin is 2.1 g/dL, cholesterol is 340 mg/dL, and creatinine is 0.9 mg/dL. Urinalysis shows 4+ protein and no blood; urine microscopy shows oval fat bodies and no red blood cells. A 24-hour urine collection contains 9.2 g of protein. Serum C3 and C4 are normal. Tests for hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis C antibody, HIV, and antinuclear antibodies are negative. Age-appropriate cancer screening is up to date and normal. Which of the following serum findings is most likely in this patient?

- A. Antistreptolysin O antibodies
- B. Antibodies to phospholipase A2 receptor
- C. Antibodies to the alpha-3 chain of type IV collagen
- D. Antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies with myeloperoxidase specificity
- E. Elevated serum free light chains with an abnormal kappa-to-lambda ratio

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: He has the nephrotic syndrome (proteinuria 9.2 g/day, albumin 2.1 g/dL, hyperlipidemia, edema) with a bland urine sediment, normal blood pressure, normal creatinine, and normal complement. In an adult without hepatitis B or C, HIV, lupus, drugs, or cancer, the most common cause is primary membranous nephropathy, in which about 70 to 80% of patients have circulating autoantibodies to the podocyte phospholipase A2 receptor (PLA2R).
- **원리**: <b>Why the antibody causes nephrotic, not nephritic, disease</b>: PLA2R is a protein on the foot processes of podocytes. Anti-PLA2R IgG (mainly IgG4) binds it in place, forming immune deposits on the outer (subepithelial) side of the glomerular basement membrane. Because the deposits face the urinary space, away from circulating leukocytes, complement damages the podocytes and the filtration barrier without recruiting inflammatory cells — so heavy proteinuria develops without red cell casts or hypocomplementemia.<br> <b>What the biopsy shows</b>: thickened capillary loops on light microscopy, spikes of new basement membrane between deposits on silver stain, granular IgG and C3 along the capillary walls, and subepithelial electron-dense deposits.<br> <b>Why the antibody matters clinically</b>: a positive anti-PLA2R in a patient with nephrotic syndrome and preserved renal function supports the diagnosis even without biopsy, and the titer tracks disease activity — falling before remission and rising before relapse — which guides immunosuppression (for example rituximab). Secondary causes (hepatitis B, lupus class V, solid tumors, NSAIDs, gold) must still be excluded, as was done here.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Primary membranous nephropathy (correct)</th><th>Anti-GBM disease (closest distractor by antibody)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Antigen</td><td><b>PLA2R</b> on podocytes</td><td><b>α3 chain of type IV collagen</b> in the GBM</td></tr> <tr><td>Deposit pattern</td><td>Granular, subepithelial</td><td>Linear along the GBM</td></tr> <tr><td>Syndrome</td><td>Nephrotic — bland sediment, normal creatinine early</td><td>Nephritic — red cell casts, rapidly rising creatinine, hemoptysis</td></tr> </tbody></table> Both are antibodies against a glomerular antigen; where the antigen sits decides the syndrome — the podocyte side causes protein leak, the basement membrane facing the blood causes inflammation.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Antistreptolysin O antibodies support poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis, presenting 1 to 3 weeks after pharyngitis with hematuria, hypertension, and low C3.
  - (C) Anti-type IV collagen (anti-GBM) antibodies are expected in rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis with red cell casts, rising creatinine, and sometimes pulmonary hemorrhage, not a bland nephrotic presentation.
  - (D) MPO-ANCA is found in microscopic polyangiitis with pauci-immune crescentic glomerulonephritis, hematuria, and rapidly declining renal function, often with systemic vasculitis.
  - (E) An abnormal free light chain ratio supports AL amyloidosis, which also causes nephrotic syndrome in older adults; it becomes the answer with macroglossia, cardiomyopathy, neuropathy, or a monoclonal protein.
- **함정**: Choosing the light chain test because older adults with nephrotic syndrome raise concern for amyloidosis — without systemic features and with normal cancer screening, primary membranous nephropathy is far more common.
- **학습목표**: 성인 신증후군에서 혈뇨·고혈압·보체 이상이 없고 이차 원인이 배제되면 원발성 막성 신병증을 의심하고, 포스포리파아제 A2 수용체 항체를 그 표지로 안다

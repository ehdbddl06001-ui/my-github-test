## 문제
A 74-year-old man is brought to the emergency department 40 minutes after he fainted while standing in his kitchen. He has had episodes of lightheadedness for 2 days. He takes amlodipine for hypertension and does not take any rate-slowing drugs. His pulse is 34/min and regular, blood pressure is 78/46 mm Hg, and respirations are 18/min. He is pale, diaphoretic, and drowsy but follows commands. Lungs are clear. An ECG shows P waves at a rate of 82/min that bear no relationship to wide QRS complexes occurring at 34/min. Serum potassium is 4.3 mEq/L. Atropine has been given intravenously to a total of 3 mg without any change in heart rate or blood pressure. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. An additional 1 mg of intravenous atropine
- B. Synchronized cardioversion
- C. Intravenous amiodarone
- D. Intravenous adenosine
- E. Transcutaneous pacing

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: Independent P waves and wide QRS complexes at 34/min indicate complete (third-degree) atrioventricular block with a ventricular escape rhythm. He is hypotensive and drowsy, so the bradycardia is unstable, and atropine has already reached its maximum dose without effect. Transcutaneous pacing is the next step, bridging to transvenous and then permanent pacing.
- **원리**: <b>Why atropine fails here</b>: atropine blocks vagal (muscarinic) tone, so it speeds the sinus node and improves conduction through the AV node. In complete heart block with a wide-complex escape, the block usually lies below the AV node in the His–Purkinje system, which has little vagal innervation; atropine can raise the atrial rate without letting any more impulses through.<br> <b>Why the escape rhythm is dangerous</b>: a ventricular escape focus fires slowly (20–40/min) and unreliably, so cardiac output depends on a rate that cannot rise with demand, and pauses can lead to asystole or torsades de pointes.<br> <b>The sequence for unstable bradycardia</b>: atropine first (1 mg every 3–5 minutes up to 3 mg); if it is ineffective, transcutaneous pacing or a chronotropic infusion (epinephrine or dopamine), then expert consultation for transvenous pacing. Reversible causes — hyperkalemia, ischemia, β-blocker or calcium-channel-blocker excess — are sought in parallel.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Transcutaneous pacing (answer)</th><th>More atropine (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Atropine already given</td><td><b>3 mg total — the maximum</b></td><td>Less than 3 mg</td></tr> <tr><td>Site of block</td><td>Infranodal (wide escape QRS)</td><td>AV nodal or sinus (narrow QRS, vagal)</td></tr> <tr><td>Expected response</td><td>Captures the ventricle directly</td><td>Faster sinus rate, better nodal conduction</td></tr> </tbody></table> A narrow-complex block after inferior MI may still respond to atropine; a wide-complex escape after maximal atropine needs pacing.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) More atropine would be reasonable only if less than the 3 mg maximum had been given, especially for sinus bradycardia or AV nodal block.
  - (B) Synchronized cardioversion is the answer for unstable tachyarrhythmias with a pulse, such as atrial flutter or ventricular tachycardia at fast rates.
  - (C) Amiodarone would be correct for stable monomorphic ventricular tachycardia; it slows conduction further and could worsen this block.
  - (D) Adenosine would be used for stable regular narrow-complex supraventricular tachycardia; it transiently blocks the AV node and would deepen bradycardia.
- **함정**: Repeating atropine after the 3-mg maximum — an infranodal block with a wide escape does not respond; pacing is next.
- **학습목표**: 아트로핀에 반응하지 않는 증상성 완전 방실차단에서 경피 심박조율을 고르고, 빈맥 치료나 아트로핀 추가와 구별한다

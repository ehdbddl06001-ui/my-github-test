## 문제
A 27-year-old woman comes to the office because she has been unable to conceive for 14 months despite regular unprotected intercourse. Her menstrual cycles occur every 45 to 90 days. Six months of diet and exercise lowered her weight by 4 kg. BMI is 32 kg/m². She has coarse hair on the upper lip and chin. Serum TSH and prolactin concentrations are within the reference ranges, and the 17-hydroxyprogesterone level is normal. Transvaginal ultrasonography shows more than 20 follicles in each ovary. Her partner's semen analysis is normal, and hysterosalpingography shows patent fallopian tubes. Which of the following is the most appropriate pharmacotherapy to achieve pregnancy?

- A. Spironolactone
- B. Letrozole
- C. Clomiphene citrate
- D. Metformin
- E. Combined oral contraceptive

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Oligomenorrhea, clinical hyperandrogenism, and polycystic ovarian morphology — with thyroid disease, hyperprolactinemia, and nonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia excluded — meet the Rotterdam criteria for polycystic ovary syndrome. With tubal and male factors normal, infertility is due to anovulation. Letrozole is the first-line ovulation induction agent because it produced more live births than clomiphene in women with PCOS.
- **원리**: <b>How letrozole induces ovulation</b>: Letrozole blocks aromatase, so less androgen is converted to estradiol. The hypothalamus senses low estrogen and raises GnRH pulses and pituitary FSH, which recruits a follicle. Because the drug is short-acting and does not occupy estrogen receptors, estrogen rises normally as the follicle grows, feedback is restored, and usually a single dominant follicle forms.<br> <b>Why clomiphene does less well</b>: Clomiphene blocks hypothalamic estrogen receptors for weeks; the long-lasting antiestrogen effect thins the endometrium and thickens cervical mucus, so ovulation does not always translate into pregnancy, and multiple follicles are more frequent.<br> <b>Where metformin fits</b>: It improves insulin resistance and may restore some cycles, but alone it gives fewer live births than either ovulation-inducing drug.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Letrozole (answer)</th><th>Clomiphene citrate (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Mechanism</td><td>Aromatase inhibitor — lowers estradiol briefly</td><td>Estrogen receptor antagonist — long half-life</td></tr> <tr><td>Live birth in PCOS</td><td><b>Higher</b></td><td>Lower</td></tr> <tr><td>Endometrium</td><td>Not thinned</td><td>Can be thinned by antiestrogen effect</td></tr> </tbody></table> Both trigger FSH release by making the hypothalamus "see" less estrogen — the difference is how long that signal lasts.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Spironolactone is correct for hirsutism in a woman using reliable contraception; it is avoided when trying to conceive because it can feminize a male fetus.
  - (C) Clomiphene citrate would be correct if letrozole were unavailable or not tolerated; it still induces ovulation but with fewer live births in PCOS.
  - (D) Metformin would be correct for glucose intolerance or as an adjunct to ovulation induction, not as the main drug when pregnancy is the goal.
  - (E) A combined oral contraceptive is correct for cycle control and hirsutism when she does not want pregnancy — it prevents ovulation.
- **함정**: Treating the hirsutism instead of the stated goal — the question asks for pregnancy, so ovulation induction comes first.

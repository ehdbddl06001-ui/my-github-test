## 문제
A 71-year-old man with chronic kidney disease and heart failure is brought to the emergency department because of generalized weakness for 2 days. His medications include lisinopril and spironolactone, which was added 3 weeks ago. Blood pressure is 128/70 mm Hg, pulse is 54/min, and respirations are 16/min. Serum potassium is 7.4 mEq/L, creatinine is 2.9 mg/dL, and glucose is 132 mg/dL. A repeat sample confirms the potassium level. An ECG shows peaked T waves and widened QRS complexes. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial step in management?

- A. Nebulized albuterol
- B. Oral sodium zirconium cyclosilicate
- C. Intravenous sodium bicarbonate
- D. Intravenous calcium gluconate
- E. Intravenous regular insulin with dextrose

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: D

- **정답 핵심**: Severe hyperkalemia (7.4 mEq/L, confirmed on repeat) caused by an ACE inhibitor plus spironolactone in chronic kidney disease has produced ECG changes — peaked T waves, QRS widening, and bradycardia. ECG changes mean an imminent risk of arrhythmia, so the first step is intravenous calcium, which stabilizes the cardiac membrane within minutes. It does not lower potassium; insulin with dextrose, beta-agonists, and potassium removal follow immediately.
- **원리**: <b>Why calcium works without lowering potassium</b>: the resting membrane potential depends on the potassium gradient. Hyperkalemia depolarizes the resting membrane, which inactivates fast sodium channels — conduction slows (wide QRS), and the heart drifts toward bradycardia, sine-wave rhythm, and asystole. Calcium raises the threshold potential, restoring the gap between resting and threshold potentials, so excitability normalizes within 1–3 minutes even though potassium is unchanged. The effect lasts only 30–60 minutes.<br> <b>Why the order is protect → shift → remove</b>: membrane stabilization buys time; insulin (with dextrose to prevent hypoglycemia) and beta-2 agonists drive potassium into cells within 15–30 minutes; binders, diuretics, or dialysis then remove potassium from the body over hours. Doing the slower steps first leaves the patient exposed to a fatal arrhythmia while waiting.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">IV calcium (answer)</th><th>Insulin + dextrose (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Action</td><td>Membrane stabilization</td><td>Shifts K⁺ into cells (Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase)</td></tr> <tr><td>Onset</td><td><b>1–3 min</b></td><td>15–30 min</td></tr> <tr><td>Lowers K⁺?</td><td>No</td><td>Yes, by about 0.5–1.2 mEq/L</td></tr> <tr><td>First when</td><td><b>ECG changes</b> present</td><td>No ECG changes, or right after calcium</td></tr> </tbody></table> If the ECG is abnormal, protect the heart first; if it is normal, start by shifting potassium.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Nebulized albuterol shifts potassium into cells and can be added to insulin, but its onset is slower and some patients do not respond, so it is adjunctive.
  - (B) Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate removes potassium through the gut over hours; it would suit mild, stable hyperkalemia without ECG changes.
  - (C) Sodium bicarbonate helps shift potassium mainly when there is significant metabolic acidosis, and its effect on potassium alone is small and unreliable.
  - (E) Insulin with dextrose would be the first step if hyperkalemia had no ECG changes, and it is given immediately after calcium here to shift potassium into cells.
- **함정**: Choosing the drug that lowers potassium fastest — with ECG changes, the first priority is protecting the myocardium, not the number.
- **학습목표**: Hyperkalemia with ECG changes is treated first with intravenous calcium to stabilize the myocardial membrane, before therapies that shift or remove potassium

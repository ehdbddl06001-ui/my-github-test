## 문제
A 68-year-old woman comes to the physician because of easy bruising and bleeding gums for 3 days. She has atrial fibrillation treated with warfarin, and her INR has been stable between 2.0 and 3.0 for the past year. Eight days ago, she began a 10-day course of trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for acute cystitis. She has not changed her diet or other medications. Pulse is 84/min and irregularly irregular, and blood pressure is 132/80 mm Hg. Several ecchymoses are present on the forearms. INR is 6.8, hemoglobin concentration is 12.4 g/dL, platelet count is 230,000/mm3, and serum creatinine concentration is 0.9 mg/dL. Which of the following is the most likely mechanism of the change in her INR?

- A. Inhibition of hepatic CYP2C9
- B. Displacement of warfarin from albumin binding sites
- C. Induction of hepatic CYP3A4
- D. Decreased renal excretion of warfarin
- E. Inhibition of platelet cyclooxygenase

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: Sulfamethoxazole inhibits CYP2C9, the enzyme that metabolizes S-warfarin, the more potent enantiomer. Its clearance falls, the free S-warfarin concentration rises, and the INR climbs within several days of starting the antibiotic. Normal platelet count and renal function and the unchanged diet point to a pharmacokinetic interaction rather than another cause of bleeding.
- **원리**: <b>Why CYP2C9 matters most</b>: warfarin is a racemic mixture. S-warfarin is 3-5 times more potent as a vitamin K epoxide reductase inhibitor and is cleared almost entirely by CYP2C9; R-warfarin is cleared by CYP3A4 and CYP1A2. A drug that inhibits CYP2C9 (sulfamethoxazole, metronidazole, fluconazole, amiodarone) therefore raises the INR much more than one that affects only R-warfarin.<br> <b>Why protein-binding displacement is not the main answer</b>: displacement transiently raises the free fraction, but free drug is then cleared faster, so the free concentration returns to baseline within a few half-lives. Sustained INR rise over a week requires reduced clearance.<br> <b>Time course</b>: the INR reflects depletion of clotting factors (factor VII first, prothrombin last), so it rises over 3-5 days after the interacting drug is started — matching this case.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">CYP2C9 inhibition (answer)</th><th>Albumin displacement (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Effect on clearance</td><td><b>Decreased</b> — S-warfarin accumulates</td><td>Unchanged or increased</td></tr> <tr><td>Free drug level</td><td>Stays high while the inhibitor is given</td><td>Transient rise, returns to baseline</td></tr> <tr><td>Clinical importance</td><td>Major, predictable — reduce warfarin dose</td><td>Minor, short-lived</td></tr> </tbody></table> A sustained INR rise over days means reduced metabolism, not displacement.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Displacement from albumin contributes briefly, but it would be the main explanation only if the INR rose transiently and fell back despite continuing both drugs.
  - (C) Induction of CYP3A4 would be correct for a falling INR after starting rifampin or carbamazepine, which speed warfarin clearance.
  - (D) Decreased renal excretion is not relevant because warfarin is cleared by hepatic metabolism; it would matter for dabigatran in kidney failure.
  - (E) Platelet cyclooxygenase inhibition explains bleeding with aspirin or NSAIDs, but it does not prolong the INR.
- **함정**: Recalling sulfonamides as albumin-displacing drugs — displacement is transient; the sustained INR rise comes from CYP2C9 inhibition.
- **학습목표**: 와파린 복용 중 설파메톡사졸 추가 뒤 INR 이 오른 주된 기전이 S-와파린을 대사하는 CYP2C9 억제임을 안다

## 문제
A 64-year-old man is evaluated on postoperative day 7 after a total knee arthroplasty because of new pain and swelling of the left calf. Since surgery he has received subcutaneous unfractionated heparin 5000 units every 8 hours for thromboprophylaxis. He has no bleeding. His temperature is 37.0°C, pulse is 88/min, and blood pressure is 136/82 mm Hg. The left calf is swollen and tender. Platelet count was 260,000/mm3 before surgery and is 98,000/mm3 today. Hemoglobin is 12.1 g/dL. Prothrombin time and fibrinogen are within the reference ranges. Duplex ultrasonography shows a thrombus in the left popliteal vein. An immunoassay for platelet factor 4–heparin antibodies has been sent. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Continue heparin at treatment doses
- B. Transfuse platelets and continue heparin
- C. Start warfarin and stop heparin
- D. Stop heparin and start argatroban
- E. Switch to enoxaparin at treatment doses

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: D

- **정답 핵심**: A 62% fall in platelet count beginning 5–10 days after heparin exposure, with a new venous thrombosis and no other cause, gives a high 4T score for heparin-induced thrombocytopenia. Because HIT is prothrombotic, all heparin is stopped immediately and a non-heparin anticoagulant such as argatroban is started without waiting for the antibody result. Low-molecular-weight heparin cross-reacts with the antibodies, warfarin alone can cause venous limb gangrene, and platelet transfusion adds fuel to thrombosis.
- **원리**: <b>Why a low platelet count causes clots</b>: Heparin binds platelet factor 4 (PF4) released from platelets, exposing new epitopes. Some patients form IgG against the PF4–heparin complex. The immune complexes cross-link FcγRIIa receptors on platelets, activating them — platelets are consumed (thrombocytopenia) and release procoagulant microparticles, and monocytes and endothelium generate thrombin. The result is a <b>hypercoagulable state despite thrombocytopenia</b>, with venous more than arterial thrombosis.<br> <b>Why the timing is 5–10 days</b>: It takes that long to make IgG on first exposure. A fall within 24 hours occurs only if heparin was given in the previous 100 days and antibodies are still present.<br> <b>Why argatroban and not warfarin</b>: Direct thrombin inhibitors (argatroban, bivalirudin) or fondaparinux block thrombin without needing heparin. Warfarin started alone first depletes protein C (short half-life) before prothrombin, tipping toward thrombosis and causing venous limb gangrene; it is started only after platelets recover to ≥ 150,000/mm3, overlapping with the parenteral agent.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Argatroban (answer)</th><th>Enoxaparin (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Mechanism</td><td><b>Direct thrombin inhibitor</b> — no heparin structure</td><td>Low-molecular-weight heparin — still binds PF4</td></tr> <tr><td>Reaction with HIT antibodies</td><td>None</td><td>Cross-reacts in most patients</td></tr> <tr><td>Clearance</td><td>Hepatic — usable in renal failure</td><td>Renal</td></tr> <tr><td>Use in suspected HIT</td><td>Start immediately when the 4T score is intermediate or high</td><td>Contraindicated</td></tr> </tbody></table> In suspected HIT the rule is "no heparin of any kind, but still anticoagulate".
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Continuing heparin at treatment doses would be appropriate if the thrombocytopenia had another clear cause and HIT were unlikely; here it drives thrombosis.
  - (B) Platelet transfusion would be considered for severe bleeding or before an invasive procedure, but this patient is not bleeding and transfusion can worsen thrombosis.
  - (C) Warfarin is started only after the platelet count recovers, overlapping with a non-heparin anticoagulant; starting it alone now risks venous limb gangrene.
  - (E) Enoxaparin at treatment doses would be correct for a postoperative deep venous thrombosis with a stable platelet count, but it cross-reacts with HIT antibodies.
- **함정**: Treating thrombocytopenia as a bleeding risk — in HIT the danger is thrombosis, so anticoagulation continues with a non-heparin agent.
- **학습목표**: Recognize heparin-induced thrombocytopenia from a >50% platelet fall 5–10 days after heparin with new thrombosis, and stop all heparin and start a non-heparin anticoagulant before test results

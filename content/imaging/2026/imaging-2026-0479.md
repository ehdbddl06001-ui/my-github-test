## 문제
A 34-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of headache and fever for 2 weeks. During the past 3 days, he has also had nausea and intermittent blurred vision. He was diagnosed with HIV infection 1 month ago but has not started antiretroviral therapy. Temperature is 38.1°C (100.6°F), pulse is 92/min, and blood pressure is 124/78 mm Hg. He is alert and oriented. There is mild neck stiffness; there are no focal neurologic deficits. CD4+ T-lymphocyte count is 42/mm3. CT of the head shows no abnormalities. Lumbar puncture shows an opening pressure of 32 cm H2O; cerebrospinal fluid has a leukocyte count of 20/mm3 (90% lymphocytes), protein of 70 mg/dL, and glucose of 35 mg/dL (serum glucose 95 mg/dL). India ink stain shows encapsulated yeast forms, and cryptococcal antigen is positive. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial pharmacotherapy?

- A. Dexamethasone and fluconazole
- B. Liposomal amphotericin B and flucytosine
- C. Fluconazole alone
- D. Fluconazole and antiretroviral therapy
- E. Ceftriaxone and vancomycin

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Subacute headache and fever, CD4 count below 100, encapsulated yeast on India ink, and a positive cryptococcal antigen establish cryptococcal meningitis. Induction therapy is amphotericin B (liposomal preferred) plus flucytosine, followed by fluconazole consolidation and maintenance. Antiretroviral therapy is deferred 4–6 weeks, and the high opening pressure is lowered with repeated lumbar punctures.
- **원리**: <b>Why two drugs at induction</b>: amphotericin B binds ergosterol and punches pores in the fungal membrane — it kills quickly. Flucytosine is converted inside the fungus to 5-fluorouracil and blocks DNA/RNA synthesis; the combination clears the CSF faster and lowers mortality. Fluconazole only inhibits ergosterol synthesis (fungistatic), so it is too slow to start with but suits long consolidation and maintenance.<br> <b>Why delay antiretroviral therapy</b>: restoring immunity early lets T cells attack the large antigen burden in the closed space of the meninges — <b>immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome</b> — which raised mortality when ART was started within 1–2 weeks in trials (COAT).<br> <b>Why the pressure matters</b>: the polysaccharide capsule clogs arachnoid villi, so CSF outflow falls and pressure rises — causing headache, vision loss, and cranial nerve palsies. Serial lumbar punctures to bring it under 20 cm H2O are part of treatment; glucocorticoids do not help and worsen outcome.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">AmB + flucytosine now (answer)</th><th>Fluconazole + ART now (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Antifungal action</td><td><b>Fungicidal, rapid CSF clearance</b></td><td>Fungistatic, slow clearance</td></tr> <tr><td>ART timing</td><td>Deferred 4–6 weeks</td><td>Immediate — risk of IRIS</td></tr> <tr><td>Role of fluconazole</td><td>Consolidation, then maintenance</td><td>Used alone as induction</td></tr> </tbody></table> Fluconazole is the right drug for the later phases and for antigen-positive patients without meningitis, not for induction in established meningitis.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Dexamethasone is correct adjunct therapy for pneumococcal meningitis or tuberculous meningitis, but it increases harm in cryptococcal meningitis.
  - (C) Fluconazole alone is correct for asymptomatic cryptococcal antigenemia without meningitis, or later as consolidation after induction therapy.
  - (D) Starting ART is correct for most HIV opportunistic infections within 2 weeks, but in cryptococcal meningitis it is delayed 4–6 weeks to avoid IRIS.
  - (E) Ceftriaxone and vancomycin are correct empiric therapy for acute bacterial meningitis with neutrophilic CSF before an organism is identified.
- **함정**: Starting ART right away because the CD4 count is very low — in cryptococcal meningitis, early ART increases death from immune reconstitution.
- **학습목표**: CD4 100 미만 HIV 환자의 크립토코쿠스 수막염을 진단하고 유도 치료로 암포테리신 B 와 플루시토신을 쓰며, 항레트로바이러스 치료는 4~6주 미룬다

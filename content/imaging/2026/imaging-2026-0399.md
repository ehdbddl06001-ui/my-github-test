## 문제
A 34-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department 1 hour after she was found confused next to empty bottles of her prescribed amitriptyline. She has a history of depression. Her temperature is 37.9°C (100.2°F), pulse is 132/min, respirations are 18/min, and blood pressure is 86/50 mm Hg. She is drowsy but protects her airway. Pupils are 7 mm and sluggishly reactive, the skin is dry and flushed, and bowel sounds are decreased. Serum glucose and electrolyte concentrations are within the reference ranges. An ECG shows sinus tachycardia with a QRS duration of 148 msec and a tall terminal R wave in lead aVR. Two liters of normal saline are being infused. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Intravenous procainamide
- B. Intravenous metoprolol
- C. Intravenous sodium bicarbonate
- D. Intravenous physostigmine
- E. Intravenous flumazenil

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: Anticholinergic signs (dilated pupils, dry flushed skin, decreased bowel sounds, tachycardia, fever) with a widened QRS and a terminal R wave in aVR are tricyclic antidepressant toxicity. Blockade of fast sodium channels widens the QRS and causes hypotension and ventricular arrhythmias; hypertonic sodium bicarbonate is given for a QRS of 100 msec or more.
- **원리**: <b>Why the QRS widens</b>: tricyclic antidepressants block the fast sodium channels of the His-Purkinje system and ventricular myocardium in the same way as class IA/IC antiarrhythmics. Phase 0 depolarization slows, so ventricular conduction is delayed — the QRS widens, and the right bundle, which conducts last, produces the terminal rightward deflection seen as a tall R in aVR.<br> <b>Why bicarbonate works</b>: it acts in two ways. The extra sodium load increases the gradient across the partially blocked channels, and alkalinization (target pH 7.45–7.55) lowers the fraction of the drug that is ionized and bound to the channel. The QRS narrows and blood pressure rises within minutes.<br> <b>Other toxicities</b>: anticholinergic effects, alpha-1 blockade (hypotension), and seizures from GABA-A antagonism. A QRS above 100 msec predicts seizures and above 160 msec predicts ventricular arrhythmias.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Sodium bicarbonate (answer)</th><th>Physostigmine (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Target</td><td><b>Sodium-channel blockade</b> (QRS widening, hypotension)</td><td>Peripheral and central anticholinergic signs</td></tr> <tr><td>Fits when</td><td>QRS ≥ 100 msec, arrhythmia, hypotension</td><td>Pure anticholinergic poisoning with a narrow QRS</td></tr> <tr><td>In TCA overdose</td><td>First-line</td><td><b>Contraindicated</b> — bradycardia, asystole, seizures</td></tr> </tbody></table> Both answer the anticholinergic picture, but only the ECG decides: a wide QRS means a sodium-channel problem, and cholinesterase inhibition then becomes dangerous.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Procainamide would be correct for stable ventricular tachycardia from other causes; as a class IA sodium-channel blocker it worsens tricyclic toxicity.
  - (B) Metoprolol would be correct for symptomatic sinus tachycardia from thyrotoxicosis; here the tachycardia is compensatory and beta-blockade worsens hypotension.
  - (D) Physostigmine would be correct for a pure anticholinergic toxidrome with a normal QRS; in tricyclic overdose it can precipitate bradycardia and asystole.
  - (E) Flumazenil would be correct for isolated benzodiazepine sedation; with tricyclic coingestion it lowers the seizure threshold and is contraindicated.
- **함정**: Treating the anticholinergic signs with physostigmine — a wide QRS points to sodium-channel blockade, for which physostigmine is dangerous.
- **학습목표**: 삼환계 항우울제 중독에서 QRS 폭 100 ms 이상·저혈압·심실 부정맥이 있으면 고장성 탄산수소나트륨으로 나트륨 통로 차단을 거스른다

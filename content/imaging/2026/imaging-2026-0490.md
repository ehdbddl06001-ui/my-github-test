## 문제
A 34-year-old man comes to the emergency department because he noticed that the right side of his face was drooping when he woke up this morning. He also has mild pain behind the right ear since yesterday and finds loud sounds uncomfortable in the right ear. He has no weakness or numbness of the limbs and no rash. Vital signs are within normal limits. He cannot wrinkle the right side of his forehead or fully close the right eye, and the right corner of his mouth droops. The ear canal and tympanic membrane appear normal. The remainder of the neurologic examination is normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Oral valacyclovir alone
- B. Lumbar puncture
- C. Oral prednisone
- D. Noncontrast CT of the head
- E. Intravenous alteplase

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: Sudden weakness of the whole right half of the face, including the forehead, with retroauricular pain and hyperacusis and an otherwise normal neurologic examination is a peripheral facial nerve palsy — Bell palsy. Starting oral glucocorticoids within 72 hours improves the chance of complete recovery; eye protection is added because he cannot close the eye.
- **원리**: <b>Why the forehead decides central versus peripheral</b>: the part of the facial motor nucleus that supplies the forehead receives corticobulbar fibers from both hemispheres, while the part for the lower face receives mainly crossed fibers. A stroke above the nucleus therefore spares the forehead, whereas a lesion of the nerve itself paralyzes the whole half of the face.<br> <b>Why hyperacusis and ear pain</b>: inflammation and swelling of the nerve inside the narrow facial canal (often linked to herpes simplex reactivation) involve the branch to the stapedius — sounds become too loud — and irritate nearby sensory fibers behind the ear.<br> <b>Why steroids work early</b>: they reduce edema of the nerve in its bony canal before compression causes axonal degeneration; benefit is shown when started within 72 hours. Antivirals alone add no proven benefit.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Bell palsy → prednisone (answer)</th><th>Stroke → CT of the head (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Forehead</td><td><b>Paralyzed</b> — lower motor neuron</td><td>Spared — bilateral cortical input</td></tr> <tr><td>Other deficits</td><td>None besides facial nerve functions (hyperacusis, taste)</td><td>Arm weakness, dysarthria, aphasia</td></tr> <tr><td>Imaging</td><td>Not needed in a typical case</td><td>Urgent noncontrast CT</td></tr> </tbody></table> Check the forehead first: whole-face weakness with a normal remaining examination is a nerve problem, not a brain problem.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Valacyclovir alone would not be appropriate; an antiviral is reserved as an optional addition to steroids, or for Ramsay Hunt syndrome with a vesicular ear rash.
  - (B) Lumbar puncture would be correct with bilateral facial palsy, fever, or meningeal signs suggesting Lyme disease, Guillain–Barré syndrome, or meningitis.
  - (D) Noncontrast CT of the head would be correct if the forehead were spared or if limb weakness or speech difficulty suggested an acute stroke.
  - (E) Intravenous alteplase would be correct for an acute ischemic stroke with a disabling deficit within 4.5 hours after hemorrhage is excluded by imaging.
- **함정**: Ordering a head CT for every facial droop — forehead involvement with an otherwise normal examination identifies a peripheral palsy.
- **학습목표**: 이마를 포함한 한쪽 얼굴 전체의 급성 마비를 말초성 얼굴신경 마비(벨마비)로 판단하고 72시간 안에 경구 글루코코르티코이드를 시작한다

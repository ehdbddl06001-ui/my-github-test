## 문제
A 3-year-old boy is brought to the physician because of fever for 5 days. His temperature has reached 39.8°C daily despite acetaminophen. His parents report red eyes without discharge, cracked red lips, and swelling of his hands and feet. Temperature is 39.5°C. Examination shows bilateral bulbar conjunctival injection sparing the limbus, a red tongue with prominent papillae, erythema and induration of the palms and soles, a polymorphous rash on the trunk, and a 2-cm nontender right cervical lymph node. A rapid streptococcal antigen test is negative. Leukocyte count is 15,800/mm3, platelet count is 450,000/mm3, and C-reactive protein is 9.6 mg/dL. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Oral amoxicillin
- B. Oral prednisolone alone
- C. Supportive care with antipyretics
- D. Intramuscular benzathine penicillin
- E. Intravenous immunoglobulin and aspirin

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: Fever for 5 days with all five principal features — bilateral nonexudative limbus-sparing conjunctivitis, oral changes (cracked lips, strawberry tongue), extremity changes, polymorphous rash, and a cervical node of 1.5 cm or more — meets the criteria for Kawasaki disease. Intravenous immunoglobulin (2 g/kg) with aspirin given within the first 10 days of illness reduces the risk of coronary artery aneurysms from about 25% to under 5%; echocardiography is obtained at diagnosis.
- **원리**: <b>Why the coronary arteries</b>: Kawasaki disease is a medium-vessel vasculitis of young children. Inflammatory cells infiltrate the arterial wall, destroy the internal elastic lamina, and weaken it; the coronary arteries are the most affected medium-sized vessels, and aneurysms form in about one-quarter of untreated children, leading to thrombosis, myocardial infarction, or sudden death years later.<br> <b>Why IVIG and aspirin</b>: High-dose IVIG neutralizes the inflammatory response through mechanisms that include Fc-receptor modulation and blocking of cytokines; it shortens fever and prevents aneurysms when given early (ideally within 10 days). Aspirin is anti-inflammatory at higher doses during the febrile phase and antiplatelet at low doses afterward to prevent coronary thrombosis.<br> <b>Why not antibiotics</b>: The disease mimics infection (fever, rash, high CRP), but cultures and streptococcal tests are negative and the fever does not respond to antibiotics.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Oral amoxicillin would be correct for streptococcal pharyngitis or scarlet fever with exudative tonsillitis and a positive rapid strep test.
  - (B) Prednisolone alone is not standard initial therapy; steroids are added to IVIG for high-risk patients or for disease that persists after IVIG.
  - (C) Supportive care alone would be correct for a self-limited viral illness without the criteria for Kawasaki disease.
  - (D) Benzathine penicillin would be correct for streptococcal pharyngitis when adherence to oral therapy is a concern or for rheumatic fever prophylaxis.
- **함정**: Treating fever with a strawberry tongue and rash as streptococcal infection — bilateral conjunctivitis, extremity edema, and a negative strep test point to Kawasaki disease.

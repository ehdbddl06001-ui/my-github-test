## 문제
A 74-year-old woman is admitted to the intensive care unit because of abdominal distension, decreased stool output, and confusion for 1 day. Ten days ago, she completed a course of clindamycin for a dental infection, after which she had watery diarrhea. Temperature is 38.9°C (102°F), pulse is 122/min, and blood pressure is 82/50 mm Hg despite 3 L of intravenous crystalloid; norepinephrine is started. The abdomen is distended and tender with hypoactive bowel sounds and no peritoneal signs. The leukocyte count is 32,000/mm3, and serum creatinine is 2.3 mg/dL (baseline 0.8). Stool testing is positive for Clostridioides difficile toxin. An abdominal x-ray shows dilated colon without free air. Which of the following is the most appropriate pharmacotherapy?

- A. Oral fidaxomicin alone
- B. Oral loperamide plus oral vancomycin
- C. Oral vancomycin plus intravenous metronidazole
- D. Oral metronidazole alone
- E. Intravenous vancomycin alone

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: C. difficile toxin–positive colitis after clindamycin with <b>hypotension requiring vasopressors</b>, <b>ileus</b> (distension, decreased stool), leukocytosis above 15,000, and acute kidney injury is <b>fulminant</b> disease. Guidelines recommend <b>high-dose oral (or rectal, in ileus) vancomycin plus intravenous metronidazole</b>, with early surgical consultation.
- **원리**: <b>Why oral vancomycin</b>: C. difficile lives in the colon lumen. Oral vancomycin is not absorbed, so it reaches very high colonic concentrations; intravenous vancomycin is not excreted into the colon and does nothing for the infection.<br> <b>Why add intravenous metronidazole in fulminant disease</b>: With ileus, oral drug may not travel down to the colon. Intravenous metronidazole is excreted into bile and through the inflamed colon wall, so it reaches the lumen even when motility stops. Rectal vancomycin enemas are added for the same reason.<br> <b>Why fulminant is a different category</b>: Toxins A and B disrupt the colonic epithelium; severe inflammation can cause toxic megacolon and perforation. Shock, ileus, or megacolon mark fulminant disease, which carries high mortality — colectomy or diverting loop ileostomy is considered if the patient does not improve or lactate rises.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Fulminant (answer)</th><th>Non-severe/severe — fidaxomicin (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Defining signs</td><td><b>Hypotension/shock, ileus, megacolon</b></td><td>WBC ≥ 15,000 or creatinine > 1.5 without shock</td></tr> <tr><td>Therapy</td><td>Oral/rectal vancomycin + IV metronidazole</td><td>Fidaxomicin (preferred) or oral vancomycin</td></tr> <tr><td>Surgery</td><td>Early consultation</td><td>Not needed</td></tr> </tbody></table> Classify the episode first: without shock or ileus, fidaxomicin; with shock or ileus, combine routes.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Oral fidaxomicin alone would be preferred for a first non-severe or severe episode without shock, ileus, or megacolon.
  - (B) Loperamide would be avoided here because antimotility drugs can precipitate toxic megacolon in C. difficile colitis.
  - (D) Oral metronidazole alone is an alternative only for a non-severe first episode when fidaxomicin and vancomycin are unavailable.
  - (E) Intravenous vancomycin alone treats MRSA bacteremia but does not reach the colon lumen and has no role in C. difficile infection.
- **함정**: Choosing fidaxomicin because it is the current preferred drug — that applies to episodes without shock or ileus.
- **학습목표**: Treat fulminant Clostridioides difficile infection (hypotension, ileus) with high-dose oral vancomycin plus intravenous metronidazole and distinguish it from non-severe infection

## 문제
A 32-year-old man comes to the physician for a routine health maintenance examination. He feels well. His father had a myocardial infarction at the age of 46 years. He does not smoke and exercises regularly. He takes no medications. Blood pressure is 118/74 mm Hg, and BMI is 23 kg/m2. Firm yellow nodules are palpable along both Achilles tendons. Fasting serum studies show total cholesterol 298 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 224 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 48 mg/dL, triglycerides 112 mg/dL, glucose 88 mg/dL, and thyroid-stimulating hormone 1.8 μU/mL. Urinalysis shows no protein. His estimated 10-year risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease is less than 5%. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Recommend lifestyle changes and repeat the lipid panel in 1 year
- B. Begin ezetimibe monotherapy
- C. Begin fenofibrate therapy
- D. Begin PCSK9 inhibitor monotherapy
- E. Begin high-intensity statin therapy

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: LDL cholesterol of 224 mg/dL with tendon xanthomas and premature coronary disease in his father indicates heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia. An LDL cholesterol of 190 mg/dL or higher is a statin benefit group on its own: high-intensity statin therapy (atorvastatin 40-80 mg or rosuvastatin 20-40 mg) is started without using a 10-year risk calculator, after secondary causes are excluded (normal TSH, no proteinuria).
- **원리**: <b>Why the risk calculator does not apply</b>: 10-year risk equations were built from population cohorts and weight age heavily, so a 32-year-old scores low even with lifelong very high LDL. Atherosclerosis depends on cumulative LDL exposure ("LDL-years"); in familial hypercholesterolemia the LDL receptor (or apoB/PCSK9) defect raises LDL from birth, and untreated men often have coronary events in their 40s. Treating early prevents that cumulative burden.<br> <b>Why high-intensity statin first</b>: statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, lowering hepatocyte cholesterol and upregulating the remaining functional LDL receptors. High intensity lowers LDL by about 50% or more. If LDL stays at or above about 100 mg/dL, ezetimibe and then a PCSK9 inhibitor are added.<br> <b>Why exclude secondary causes</b>: hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, cholestasis, and drugs can raise LDL; treating the cause may correct it. Here TSH and the urinalysis are normal, and cascade screening of first-degree relatives is recommended.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Lifestyle change with repeat testing fits a young adult with mildly raised LDL and low calculated risk; it would be wrong here because an LDL of 190 or higher needs drug therapy regardless of the score.
  - (B) Ezetimibe monotherapy lowers LDL by only about 20% and is used as an add-on or when statins are not tolerated; it would be chosen if he had documented statin intolerance.
  - (C) Fenofibrate mainly lowers triglycerides and is used for severe hypertriglyceridemia to prevent pancreatitis; his triglycerides are normal, and fibrates do little for LDL.
  - (D) A PCSK9 inhibitor is added when LDL remains high despite maximally tolerated statin plus ezetimibe; it would be appropriate after first-line therapy fails, not as initial monotherapy.
- **함정**: Trusting the low 10-year risk estimate in a young patient — an LDL of 190 mg/dL or higher bypasses the calculator.
- **학습목표**: LDL 콜레스테롤 190 mg/dL 이상은 10년 위험 계산과 관계없이 고강도 스타틴의 적응증임을 알고, 이차 원인(갑상샘저하증·신증후군)을 먼저 배제한다

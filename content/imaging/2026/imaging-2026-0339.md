## 문제
A 26-year-old woman at 20 weeks' gestation comes to the physician 1 day after her roommate was hospitalized with meningitis. The roommate's cerebrospinal fluid culture grew gram-negative diplococci identified as Neisseria meningitidis. The patient has shared an apartment and meals with her roommate during the past week. She feels well. She has no history of serious illness and takes only a prenatal vitamin. She has no known drug allergies. Temperature is 36.8°C. Physical examination shows no abnormalities; there is no rash or neck stiffness. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Single oral dose of ciprofloxacin
- B. Oral rifampin twice daily for 2 days
- C. Meningococcal vaccination without antibiotics
- D. Observation with return if fever develops
- E. Single intramuscular dose of ceftriaxone

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: Living in the same household and sharing meals during the week before the index patient's illness makes her a close contact, so she needs chemoprophylaxis now, regardless of vaccination status. Ciprofloxacin and rifampin are standard for nonpregnant adults, but in pregnancy a single intramuscular dose of ceftriaxone is the recommended agent.
- **원리**: <b>Why close contacts need antibiotics immediately</b>: Neisseria meningitidis spreads by respiratory droplets and oral secretions. Household contacts have a risk of invasive disease several hundred times that of the general population, mostly within the first days to weeks. Prophylaxis eradicates nasopharyngeal carriage, so it should be given as soon as possible (ideally within 24 hours of identifying the index case); waiting for symptoms defeats its purpose because meningococcemia can kill within hours.<br> <b>Why the agent depends on pregnancy</b>: three drugs eradicate carriage — rifampin, ciprofloxacin, and ceftriaxone. Rifampin is a potent CYP450 inducer and is not recommended in pregnancy; fluoroquinolones are generally avoided in pregnancy when an alternative exists. A single 250-mg intramuscular dose of ceftriaxone is effective and safe in pregnancy, so it is the agent of choice here.<br> <b>Vaccination</b>: vaccine may be added in an outbreak of a vaccine-preventable serogroup, but it takes 7–14 days to produce protection and does not replace chemoprophylaxis.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) A single oral dose of ciprofloxacin would be correct for a nonpregnant adult close contact in an area without ciprofloxacin-resistant meningococci.
  - (B) Rifampin for 2 days would be correct for a child or a nonpregnant adult contact; it is avoided in pregnancy and interacts with hormonal contraceptives.
  - (C) Vaccination alone would not protect her in time; vaccine is added to chemoprophylaxis only during an outbreak caused by a vaccine-covered serogroup.
  - (D) Observation would be appropriate only for a casual contact without exposure to oral secretions, such as a coworker in the same building.
- **함정**: Choosing ciprofloxacin because a single oral dose is convenient — in pregnancy ceftriaxone is the recommended prophylactic agent.
- **학습목표**: 수막알균 환자의 밀접 접촉자에게 화학적 예방이 필요함을 판단하고, 임신부에게는 시프로플록사신·리팜핀 대신 세프트리악손 근육주사를 고른다

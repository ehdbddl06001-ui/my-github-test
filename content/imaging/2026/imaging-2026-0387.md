## 문제
A 68-year-old man is brought to the emergency department because of a 3-day history of increasing shortness of breath and cough productive of purulent sputum. He has chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and a 50-pack-year smoking history. His temperature is 37.9°C (100.2°F), pulse is 112/min, respirations are 30/min, and blood pressure is 146/88 mm Hg. He is using accessory muscles but is alert, follows commands, and can clear his secretions. There are diffuse expiratory wheezes. Arterial blood gas analysis while receiving oxygen by nasal cannula at 2 L/min shows pH 7.28, PaCO2 68 mm Hg, and PaO2 62 mm Hg. A chest x-ray shows hyperinflation without consolidation. Nebulized albuterol and ipratropium, intravenous methylprednisolone, and oral doxycycline are given. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Noninvasive positive-pressure ventilation
- B. Endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation
- C. Oxygen by nonrebreather mask to keep oxygen saturation above 98%
- D. Intravenous aminophylline infusion
- E. Intravenous sodium bicarbonate infusion

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: He has an exacerbation of COPD with acute respiratory acidosis (pH 7.28, PaCO2 68 mm Hg) despite bronchodilators and corticosteroids. Because he is alert, cooperative, hemodynamically stable, and able to clear secretions, noninvasive positive-pressure ventilation is indicated; it lowers the need for intubation and mortality in this setting.
- **원리**: <b>Why the CO2 rises</b>: in an exacerbation, airway inflammation and mucus raise resistance, so expiration is not finished before the next breath (dynamic hyperinflation and intrinsic PEEP). The flattened diaphragm works at a mechanical disadvantage, breaths become rapid and shallow, dead-space fraction increases, and alveolar ventilation falls — PaCO2 rises faster than the kidneys can compensate, so pH drops.<br> <b>How NIV helps</b>: inspiratory pressure support increases tidal volume and unloads the fatigued diaphragm, while expiratory pressure offsets intrinsic PEEP so each breath needs less effort to trigger. Alveolar ventilation rises and PaCO2 falls, usually within 1–2 hours.<br> <b>Who can use it</b>: NIV needs a patient who protects the airway — awake, cooperative, able to clear secretions, and hemodynamically stable. Recheck arterial blood gases at 1–2 hours; if pH and PaCO2 do not improve or mental status worsens, intubate.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">NIV (answer)</th><th>Intubation (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Fits when</td><td>pH &lt; 7.35 and PaCO2 &gt; 45 mm Hg, <b>alert and cooperative</b></td><td>Coma or deteriorating mental status, apnea, shock</td></tr> <tr><td>Airway</td><td>Patient protects it and clears secretions</td><td>Cannot protect it, copious secretions, vomiting</td></tr> <tr><td>After NIV fails</td><td>—</td><td>pH and PaCO2 worse at 1–2 h despite NIV</td></tr> </tbody></table> The blood gas alone does not decide; mental status and airway protection do. The same pH 7.28 in a somnolent patient who cannot clear secretions would call for intubation.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Intubation would be correct if he were obtunded, unable to clear secretions, hemodynamically unstable, or worsening after a trial of noninvasive ventilation.
  - (C) High-flow oxygen would be correct in hypoxemia without CO2 retention; here, oxygen saturation above 98% worsens hypercapnia, so the target is 88% to 92%.
  - (D) Aminophylline would be considered only for refractory bronchospasm, as it adds little to inhaled bronchodilators and causes arrhythmias and seizures.
  - (E) Sodium bicarbonate would be correct for severe metabolic acidosis; this acidosis is respiratory and is corrected by increasing ventilation.
- **함정**: Equating a pH of 7.28 with the need to intubate — an alert patient who protects his airway gets a trial of noninvasive ventilation first.
- **학습목표**: COPD 급성 악화에서 pH 7.35 미만·PaCO2 상승의 고탄산혈증 산증이 있고 의식·협조가 유지되면 기관삽관 전에 비침습적 양압환기를 선택한다

## 문제
A 34-year-old man is brought to the emergency department 40 minutes after his roommate found him difficult to arouse. Two empty bottles of oxycodone are found next to him. He has no history of lung disease and does not smoke. His respirations are 6/min and shallow, pulse is 64/min, and blood pressure is 112/70 mm Hg. He responds only to painful stimuli, and the pupils are pinpoint. Breath sounds are clear bilaterally with no crackles. Arterial blood gas analysis while breathing room air at sea level shows a pH of 7.18, Paco2 of 72 mm Hg, Pao2 of 52 mm Hg, and HCO3- of 26 mEq/L. A chest x-ray shows no abnormalities. Which of the following is the most likely primary mechanism of this patient's hypoxemia?

- A. Ventilation-perfusion mismatch
- B. Right-to-left shunt
- C. Diffusion impairment
- D. Low inspired oxygen tension
- E. Alveolar hypoventilation

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: Alveolar Po2 = 0.21 × (760 − 47) − Paco2/0.8 ≈ 150 − 72/0.8 = 60 mm Hg. The A–a gradient is 60 − 52 = 8 mm Hg, which is normal (expected about age/4 + 4 ≈ 12 or less). Hypoxemia with a normal A–a gradient and a high Paco2 at sea level means the lungs themselves exchange gas normally; the opioid-depressed respiratory drive has lowered alveolar ventilation, so CO2 rises and displaces oxygen in the alveoli.
- **원리**: <b>The alveolar gas equation</b>: PAo2 = Fio2 × (Patm − PH2O) − Paco2/R. At sea level on room air, the first term is about 150 mm Hg and R ≈ 0.8. Every 1 mm Hg rise in Paco2 lowers alveolar Po2 by about 1.25 mm Hg — so a Paco2 of 72 alone pulls alveolar Po2 down to about 60.<br> <b>Reading the A–a gradient</b>: a normal gradient (≤ 10–15 mm Hg in young adults) means oxygen moves from alveolus to blood normally; the problem is how much fresh gas reaches the alveoli (hypoventilation) or how much oxygen is in the inspired air (altitude). A widened gradient points into the lung — V/Q mismatch, shunt, or diffusion limitation.<br> <b>Telling the two normal-gradient causes apart</b>: hypoventilation raises Paco2; high altitude lowers Paco2 because the hypoxic drive makes the person hyperventilate. Supplemental oxygen corrects both, but the real fix here is naloxone or assisted ventilation.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Mechanism</th><th style="width:39%">Hypoventilation (answer)</th><th>V/Q mismatch (closest distractor — e.g., aspiration)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>A–a gradient</td><td><b>Normal — here 60 − 52 = 8 mm Hg</b></td><td>Increased</td></tr> <tr><td>Paco2</td><td>High (72 mm Hg)</td><td>Normal or low (unless very severe)</td></tr> <tr><td>Chest x-ray / auscultation</td><td>Clear</td><td>Infiltrate, crackles, wheeze</td></tr> </tbody></table> If this patient later aspirated, the gradient would widen — the same calculation would then show a second, lung-based mechanism.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Ventilation-perfusion mismatch is the answer when the A–a gradient is widened with an abnormal lung exam or x-ray, such as aspiration pneumonia or COPD.
  - (B) Right-to-left shunt is the answer when the A–a gradient is wide and hypoxemia barely improves with 100% oxygen, as in ARDS or an intracardiac shunt.
  - (C) Diffusion impairment is the answer in interstitial lung disease, where the A–a gradient widens and desaturation worsens with exercise.
  - (D) Low inspired oxygen tension is the answer at high altitude, where the A–a gradient is normal but Paco2 is low from hyperventilation, not high.
- **함정**: Assuming that any overdose with hypoxemia must have aspiration (V/Q mismatch) — the calculated A–a gradient of 8 mm Hg is normal, so hypoventilation alone explains it.
- **학습목표**: 실내공기 동맥혈가스로 폐포 기체 방정식을 계산해 정상 폐포-동맥 산소분압차를 확인하고, 오피오이드 과량의 저산소혈증을 폐포 저환기로 설명한다

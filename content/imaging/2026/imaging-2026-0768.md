## 문제
A 52-year-old woman asks her physician what a positive result on a new blood-based screening test for colorectal cancer would mean for her. She has no symptoms and no family history of colorectal cancer. In the population in which the test was evaluated, the prevalence of colorectal neoplasia that would be detected is 2%. The test has a sensitivity of 90% and a specificity of 95%. Her physician explains the result in terms of 1,000 similar persons screened. If her result is positive, which of the following is closest to the probability that she has the disease?

- A. 27%
- B. 2%
- C. 90%
- D. 95%
- E. 99.8%

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: In 1,000 persons, 20 have disease. The test detects 90% of them: <b>18 true positives</b>. Of the 980 without disease, 5% test positive: <b>49 false positives</b>. Positive predictive value = 18 / (18 + 49) = 18/67 ≈ <b>27%</b>.
- **원리**: <b>Why PPV depends on prevalence</b>: Sensitivity and specificity describe the test within groups already known to have or not have the disease, so they do not change with prevalence. A patient, however, starts from a positive result and asks which group she belongs to. The answer depends on how large each group is. When the disease is rare, even a small false-positive rate applied to the large disease-free group produces more false positives than the true positives from the small diseased group.<br> <b>Bayes in practice</b>: The 2% prevalence is her pretest probability; the positive result multiplies the odds by the likelihood ratio (sensitivity / [1 − specificity] = 0.9 / 0.05 = 18). Pretest odds 2:98 × 18 ≈ 0.37, which corresponds to a probability of about 27%.<br> <b>Screening implication</b>: A positive screen is a reason for a confirmatory test (colonoscopy), not a diagnosis.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:26%">Measure</th><th style="width:37%">PPV (answer, 27%)</th><th>Sensitivity (closest distractor, 90%)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Question it answers</td><td>Positive result — how likely is disease?</td><td>Disease present — how likely is a positive result?</td></tr> <tr><td>Denominator</td><td><b>All positive results</b> (18 + 49)</td><td>All diseased persons (20)</td></tr> <tr><td>Changes with prevalence?</td><td>Yes — falls as prevalence falls</td><td>No</td></tr> </tbody></table> Read the question's starting point: if it starts from a test result, the answer is a predictive value; if it starts from disease status, it is sensitivity or specificity.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) 2% is the prevalence, the probability before testing; it would be correct if the question asked about her risk before the test.
  - (C) 90% is the sensitivity; it would be correct if the question asked the probability of a positive test in a person who has the disease.
  - (D) 95% is the specificity; it would be correct if the question asked the probability of a negative test in a person without the disease.
  - (E) 99.8% is the negative predictive value (931/933); it would be correct if her result had been negative.
- **함정**: Equating the sensitivity of the test with the chance that a positive result is a true positive.
- **학습목표**: Calculate the positive predictive value of a screening test from sensitivity, specificity, and prevalence and recognize that low prevalence lowers it

## 문제
A 5-week-old boy is brought to the emergency department because of forceful, nonbilious vomiting after every feeding for 4 days. He is hungry immediately after vomiting. He was born at term and is formula-fed. He has had fewer wet diapers. He appears thin; the anterior fontanelle is slightly sunken, and capillary refill time is 3 seconds. A firm, mobile, olive-shaped mass is palpated in the epigastrium. Serum studies show sodium 133 mEq/L, potassium 3.0 mEq/L, chloride 86 mEq/L, bicarbonate 34 mEq/L, and glucose 82 mg/dL. Abdominal ultrasonography shows a pyloric muscle thickness of 5 mm. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Immediate pyloromyotomy
- B. Intravenous sodium bicarbonate
- C. Oral rehydration solution feeds every 2 hours
- D. Intravenous atropine
- E. Intravenous 0.9% saline bolus followed by dextrose-saline with potassium chloride

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: Projectile nonbilious vomiting in a 5-week-old boy, a palpable olive, and a pyloric muscle thickness of 5 mm (3 mm or more is diagnostic) establish hypertrophic pyloric stenosis. Loss of gastric hydrochloric acid has produced hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis with dehydration. Pyloromyotomy is not an emergency; the alkalosis must first be corrected with isotonic saline and then dextrose-containing maintenance fluid with potassium chloride, because an alkalotic infant is at risk of postoperative apnea from anesthesia.
- **원리**: <b>Why the alkalosis develops</b>: Vomited gastric juice contains hydrochloric acid. Each lost hydrogen ion leaves a bicarbonate ion in the blood, and loss of chloride removes the anion the kidney needs to excrete bicarbonate. Volume depletion activates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, so the kidney reabsorbs sodium in exchange for potassium and hydrogen — worsening hypokalemia and producing a paradoxical aciduria that sustains the alkalosis.<br> <b>Why saline corrects it</b>: Restoring volume removes the stimulus for sodium avidity, and supplying chloride allows the kidney to excrete bicarbonate (chloride-responsive alkalosis). Potassium is added once urine output is established. Surgery is delayed until chloride and bicarbonate approach normal (for example, chloride above 100 mEq/L and bicarbonate below 30 mEq/L).<br> <b>Why not operate immediately</b>: Metabolic alkalosis causes compensatory hypoventilation, and anesthetic agents further depress the respiratory drive, so infants operated on while alkalotic have a higher risk of postoperative apnea.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Fluid and electrolyte correction (answer)</th><th>Immediate pyloromyotomy (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Timing</td><td><b>First</b>, over 24–48 hours</td><td>After chloride and bicarbonate normalize</td></tr> <tr><td>Rationale</td><td>Chloride-responsive alkalosis and dehydration</td><td>Definitive treatment of the obstruction</td></tr> <tr><td>Risk if skipped</td><td>Postoperative apnea, arrhythmia from hypokalemia</td><td>None — the obstruction is not an emergency</td></tr> </tbody></table> Both are part of treatment; the sequence matters — the metabolic derangement, not the pyloric muscle, is the urgent problem.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Immediate pyloromyotomy would be correct once electrolytes are normal (chloride above 100 mEq/L, bicarbonate below 30 mEq/L) and the infant is rehydrated.
  - (B) Intravenous sodium bicarbonate would be correct for severe metabolic acidosis, as in sepsis with shock, not for an infant who is already alkalotic.
  - (C) Oral rehydration solution would be correct for mild dehydration from viral gastroenteritis, not for gastric outlet obstruction with persistent vomiting.
  - (D) Intravenous atropine would be correct for symptomatic bradycardia or as a historical nonsurgical option in some regions, not as the first step here.
- **함정**: Rushing to pyloromyotomy because the diagnosis is confirmed — the hypochloremic alkalosis is corrected with saline and potassium first.

## 문제
A 68-year-old man with severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease is brought to the emergency department because of worsening shortness of breath and increased purulent sputum for 3 days. He uses a tiotropium-olodaterol inhaler and has no home oxygen. He is alert and oriented but uses accessory muscles. His temperature is 37.8°C (100°F), pulse is 108/min, respirations are 28/min, and blood pressure is 142/84 mm Hg. Diffuse expiratory wheezes are heard. He receives nebulized albuterol and ipratropium, intravenous methylprednisolone, oral doxycycline, and oxygen by Venturi mask titrated to an oxygen saturation of 90%. One hour later, arterial blood gas analysis shows pH 7.28, PaCO2 68 mm Hg, PaO2 62 mm Hg, and HCO3- 31 mEq/L. A chest x-ray shows hyperinflation without consolidation. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation
- B. Increase the inspired oxygen to maintain saturation above 96%
- C. High-flow nasal cannula oxygen
- D. Intravenous aminophylline
- E. Noninvasive positive pressure ventilation

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: Despite bronchodilators, steroids, antibiotics, and controlled oxygen, he has acute-on-chronic hypercapnic respiratory acidosis (pH 7.28, PaCO2 68 mm Hg; HCO3- 31 shows chronic compensation) with increased work of breathing. He is alert, protecting his airway, and hemodynamically stable. Noninvasive positive pressure ventilation (bilevel) is indicated for pH 7.35 or lower with PaCO2 above 45 mm Hg; it reduces intubation and mortality.
- **원리**: <b>Why CO2 rises in a COPD exacerbation</b>: airway inflammation and bronchospasm increase resistance, and air trapping (dynamic hyperinflation) flattens the diaphragm and creates <b>intrinsic PEEP</b> that the muscles must overcome before any inspiratory flow. The patient breathes fast and shallow, so dead-space ventilation rises and alveolar ventilation falls — PaCO2 climbs until the pH drops.<br> <b>How bilevel NIV helps</b>: expiratory pressure (EPAP) offsets intrinsic PEEP, so each breath starts with less effort; inspiratory pressure (IPAP) augments tidal volume, increasing alveolar ventilation and washing out CO2. It rests fatigued muscles without an endotracheal tube, avoiding sedation and ventilator-associated pneumonia.<br> <b>Selection</b>: NIV is for pH 7.25 to 7.35 (can be tried below 7.25 in an ICU) in a patient who is <b>awake, cooperative, able to clear secretions, and hemodynamically stable</b>. Reassess the blood gas in 1 to 2 hours; failure to improve, deteriorating consciousness, or inability to protect the airway calls for intubation.<br> <b>Oxygen should be controlled</b> to 88 to 92%: excess oxygen worsens hypercapnia by releasing hypoxic vasoconstriction (V/Q mismatch) and the Haldane effect.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Intubation is indicated when NIV fails or is contraindicated — decreased consciousness, inability to clear secretions, hemodynamic instability, or respiratory arrest. It would be correct if he were somnolent or the pH fell after a 1- to 2-hour NIV trial.
  - (B) Raising the saturation above 96% worsens hypercapnia in COPD by reversing hypoxic vasoconstriction and through the Haldane effect. The target is 88 to 92%; more oxygen is appropriate only if saturation fell below that range.
  - (C) High-flow nasal cannula provides some dead-space washout but is less established than NIV for hypercapnic COPD exacerbations. It would be considered if he could not tolerate the NIV mask or for hypoxemic failure without acidosis.
  - (D) Aminophylline adds little bronchodilation beyond inhaled agents and causes arrhythmias and seizures; guidelines do not recommend it. It would be considered only in refractory cases already on maximal inhaled therapy.
- **함정**: Hypercapnia is not itself a reason to intubate — an alert patient with pH 7.25 to 7.35 gets NIV first.
- **학습목표**: 만성폐쇄폐질환 급성 악화에서 의식이 있는 급성 고탄산 호흡산증(pH 7.25~7.35)에 비침습 양압환기를 시작한다

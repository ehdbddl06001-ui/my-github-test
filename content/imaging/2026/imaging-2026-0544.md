## 문제
A 68-year-old man comes to the physician because of low back pain and fatigue for 3 months. He has lost 4 kg. He takes no medications other than occasional acetaminophen. Blood pressure is 128/76 mm Hg. There is tenderness over the lumbar spine. Hemoglobin is 9.1 g/dL, serum calcium is 11.6 mg/dL, total protein is 9.4 g/dL, albumin is 3.4 g/dL, and creatinine is 3.2 mg/dL (it was 0.9 mg/dL 1 year ago). Urine dipstick is negative for protein, but a 24-hour urine collection contains 3.8 g of protein. Urine sediment shows no red blood cells or casts. Renal ultrasonography shows normal-sized kidneys without hydronephrosis. Which of the following is the most likely cause of this patient's kidney injury?

- A. IgA nephropathy
- B. Obstructive uropathy
- C. Light chain cast nephropathy
- D. AL amyloidosis
- E. Analgesic nephropathy

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: Anemia, hypercalcemia, bone pain, a high total protein with a large protein-albumin gap, and kidney injury point to multiple myeloma. The urine dipstick detects mainly albumin, so a negative dipstick with 3.8 g/day of protein means the urine protein is not albumin — it is free monoclonal light chains (Bence Jones protein). Filtered light chains bind Tamm-Horsfall protein and form obstructing casts in the distal tubules: light chain cast nephropathy (myeloma kidney).
- **원리**: <b>Why the dipstick misses it</b>: The urine dipstick color reaction is sensitive to albumin and much less to other proteins. In glomerular diseases such as amyloidosis, albumin leaks through the damaged filter and the dipstick is strongly positive. In myeloma, the glomerulus is intact but small free light chains (about 22-45 kDa) are filtered in amounts that overwhelm proximal tubular reabsorption, so the urine is full of non-albumin protein — a quantitative test or urine protein electrophoresis detects it, the dipstick does not.<br> <b>How the casts form</b>: In the distal tubule, light chains bind uromodulin (Tamm-Horsfall protein) and precipitate as dense casts surrounded by giant cells. Dehydration, hypercalcemia, NSAIDs, and contrast promote precipitation. Hypercalcemia also causes afferent vasoconstriction and nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, worsening volume depletion.<br> <b>Why it matters</b>: Rapid lowering of the light chain load with anti-myeloma therapy, together with hydration and correction of calcium, is what recovers kidney function.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Light chain cast nephropathy (answer)</th><th>AL amyloidosis (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Site of injury</td><td>Distal tubules (casts)</td><td><b>Glomeruli</b> (amyloid deposits)</td></tr> <tr><td>Urine protein</td><td>Mostly light chains — dipstick negative</td><td>Mostly albumin — dipstick strongly positive, nephrotic syndrome</td></tr> <tr><td>Usual setting</td><td>High tumor burden, hypercalcemia, bone lesions</td><td>Low plasma cell burden, edema, heart or nerve involvement</td></tr> </tbody></table> Comparing the dipstick with the quantitative urine protein tells whether the leaking protein is albumin or light chains.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) IgA nephropathy would be correct with episodes of gross or microscopic hematuria after upper respiratory infections and red cell casts.
  - (B) Obstructive uropathy would be correct if renal ultrasonography showed hydronephrosis from an obstructing lesion such as prostatic enlargement.
  - (D) AL amyloidosis would be correct with nephrotic-range albuminuria and a strongly positive dipstick, edema, and often heart or nerve involvement.
  - (E) Analgesic nephropathy would be correct after years of heavy combination analgesic use with papillary necrosis and small, scarred kidneys.
- **함정**: Assuming a negative urine dipstick means no proteinuria — the dipstick misses light chains.

## 문제
A 44-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of severe epigastric pain that began 10 hours ago after a night of heavy drinking. The pain is constant, radiates to the back, and is accompanied by nausea and vomiting. He drinks about 8 beers daily. Temperature is 37.6°C, pulse is 108/min, respirations are 20/min, and blood pressure is 128/80 mm Hg. The epigastrium is tender without rebound. Serum lipase is 1,450 U/L (N < 60). Serum total bilirubin is 0.9 mg/dL, alkaline phosphatase is 88 U/L, and triglycerides are 180 mg/dL. Abdominal ultrasonography shows no gallstones and a normal common bile duct. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Total parenteral nutrition
- B. Intravenous lactated Ringer's solution and analgesia
- C. Contrast-enhanced CT of the abdomen
- D. Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography
- E. Intravenous imipenem

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Typical pain radiating to the back plus <b>lipase more than 3 times the upper limit of normal</b> establishes acute pancreatitis; imaging is not needed for diagnosis. Alcohol is the likely cause because there are no gallstones, the bile duct is normal, and triglycerides are not high. Initial care is <b>moderate, goal-directed intravenous lactated Ringer's solution</b>, analgesia, and early oral feeding as tolerated.
- **원리**: <b>Why fluids come first</b>: Activated enzymes injure acinar cells and capillaries, causing fluid to leak into the retroperitoneum and the pancreatic microcirculation to slow. Hypovolemia can turn edematous pancreatitis into necrosis and cause prerenal kidney injury. Fluids keep the gland and kidneys perfused. Lactated Ringer's is preferred over normal saline because it causes less hyperchloremic acidosis, and acidosis may increase enzyme activation.<br> <b>Why not aggressive volume</b>: A 2022 trial (WATERFALL) found that very aggressive fluid loading caused more volume overload without better outcomes, so resuscitation is now moderate and adjusted to heart rate, urine output, and BUN.<br> <b>Why CT can wait</b>: Necrosis takes 48 to 72 hours to declare itself; an early CT underestimates it and adds contrast risk. CT is reserved for diagnostic doubt or for patients who fail to improve.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Fluids and analgesia (answer)</th><th>Early contrast CT (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>When indicated</td><td>Every patient at diagnosis</td><td><b>Diagnosis uncertain</b>, or no improvement after 48–72 h</td></tr> <tr><td>Effect</td><td>Preserves pancreatic and renal perfusion</td><td>Grades necrosis and finds complications</td></tr> <tr><td>Risk</td><td>Volume overload if excessive</td><td>Underestimates early necrosis; contrast load</td></tr> </tbody></table> When pain and lipase already make the diagnosis, treatment comes before imaging; imaging answers a later question about complications.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Parenteral nutrition would be reserved for patients who cannot tolerate enteral feeding for several days; early oral or enteral feeding is preferred.
  - (C) Contrast-enhanced CT would be appropriate if the diagnosis were uncertain or if he worsened after 48 to 72 hours, suggesting necrosis.
  - (D) ERCP would be indicated for gallstone pancreatitis with cholangitis or persistent biliary obstruction, which this normal duct and bilirubin argue against.
  - (E) Imipenem would be indicated for infected necrosis suggested by gas in a collection or a positive aspirate, not as prophylaxis at presentation.
- **함정**: Ordering a CT first because the pain is severe, although the diagnosis is already established by clinical criteria.
- **학습목표**: Diagnose acute pancreatitis from typical pain and lipase elevation without imaging, and start moderate goal-directed lactated Ringer's resuscitation rather than early CT, ERCP, or prophylactic antibiotics

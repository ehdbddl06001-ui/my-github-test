## 문제
A 74-year-old woman is evaluated in the hospital because of 8 watery stools daily for 2 days. She was admitted 9 days ago for pyelonephritis and has been treated with intravenous ceftriaxone. Her temperature is 38.1°C (100.6°F), pulse is 98/min, and blood pressure is 128/74 mm Hg. The abdomen is soft and mildly tender in the lower quadrants without distention or rebound tenderness, and bowel sounds are active. Leukocyte count is 18,600/mm3, and serum creatinine concentration is 1.0 mg/dL. Serum lactate concentration is normal. A stool PCR test for Clostridioides difficile toxin genes is positive, and a toxin enzyme immunoassay is also positive. An abdominal x-ray shows no colonic dilation. Which of the following is the most appropriate pharmacotherapy?

- A. Bezlotoxumab alone
- B. Oral vancomycin
- C. Oral metronidazole
- D. Intravenous vancomycin
- E. Intravenous metronidazole plus rectal vancomycin

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Hospital-onset diarrhea after a cephalosporin with positive toxin testing is Clostridioides difficile infection. A leukocyte count of at least 15,000/mm3 makes it severe, but there is no hypotension, ileus, or megacolon, so it is not fulminant. An initial severe episode is treated with oral vancomycin (or fidaxomicin) for 10 days.
- **원리**: <b>Why oral, and why vancomycin</b>: C. difficile lives in the colonic lumen and causes disease through toxins A and B that glucosylate Rho GTPases, disrupting the epithelial cytoskeleton. The drug must reach the lumen. Oral vancomycin is hardly absorbed, so stool concentrations are hundreds of times above the MIC; intravenous vancomycin is not excreted into the colon and does nothing.<br> <b>Why metronidazole fell out</b>: metronidazole is well absorbed in the small intestine, and its colonic concentration falls as the inflamed mucosa heals. Trials showed lower cure rates than vancomycin, especially in severe disease, and repeated courses cause neuropathy.<br> <b>How severity is graded</b>: nonsevere — WBC &lt; 15,000 and creatinine &lt; 1.5; severe — WBC ≥ 15,000 or creatinine ≥ 1.5 mg/dL; fulminant — hypotension or shock, ileus, or megacolon, treated with high-dose oral (plus rectal if ileus) vancomycin and intravenous metronidazole, with surgical consultation. Stop the inciting antibiotic when possible.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Oral vancomycin (answer)</th><th>IV metronidazole + rectal vancomycin (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Severity</td><td>Initial episode, nonsevere or <b>severe</b></td><td><b>Fulminant</b> — shock, ileus, megacolon</td></tr> <tr><td>Why that route</td><td>Drug reaches the colonic lumen directly</td><td>Ileus prevents oral drug from reaching the colon</td></tr> <tr><td>Here</td><td>WBC 18,600, active bowel sounds, no dilation, normal lactate</td><td>Not present</td></tr> </tbody></table> A high WBC moves the case from nonsevere to severe, but not to fulminant; the route changes only when the colon cannot be reached orally.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Bezlotoxumab would be added to standard antibiotic therapy to reduce recurrence in high-risk patients; it is never given alone for active infection.
  - (C) Oral metronidazole would be acceptable only for a nonsevere initial episode when vancomycin and fidaxomicin are unavailable.
  - (D) Intravenous vancomycin would be correct for systemic MRSA infection; it does not reach the colonic lumen and does not treat C. difficile.
  - (E) IV metronidazole plus rectal vancomycin would be correct for fulminant disease with ileus, hypotension, or toxic megacolon.
- **함정**: Escalating to the fulminant regimen because the leukocyte count is high — without shock, ileus, or megacolon this is severe, not fulminant, and oral vancomycin suffices.
- **학습목표**: 첫 C. difficile 감염에서 백혈구 15,000/mm3 이상 또는 크레아티닌 1.5 mg/dL 이상이면 비전격성 중증으로 보고 경구 vancomycin 또는 fidaxomicin 을 쓰며, metronidazole 은 1차가 아니다

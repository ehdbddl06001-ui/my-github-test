## 문제
A 24-year-old man with type 1 diabetes mellitus is brought to the emergency department because of vomiting and abdominal pain for 2 days. He ran out of insulin 4 days ago. His pulse is 118/min, respirations are 30/min and deep, and blood pressure is 102/64 mm Hg. His mucous membranes are dry. Laboratory studies show glucose 486 mg/dL, sodium 131 mEq/L, potassium 3.1 mEq/L, chloride 94 mEq/L, bicarbonate 8 mEq/L, and β-hydroxybutyrate 7.2 mmol/L. Arterial pH is 7.08. Urine output is 60 mL/h. An intravenous infusion of 0.9% saline is started at 1 L/h. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Intravenous potassium chloride before starting insulin
- B. Intravenous regular insulin infusion at 0.1 U/kg/h
- C. Intravenous sodium bicarbonate
- D. Switch the infusion to 5% dextrose in 0.45% saline
- E. Subcutaneous insulin glargine

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: Glucose 486 mg/dL, pH 7.08, bicarbonate 8 mEq/L, an anion gap of 29, and high β-hydroxybutyrate establish severe diabetic ketoacidosis. Total-body potassium is depleted, and the serum level of 3.1 mEq/L is low despite acidosis. Insulin drives potassium into cells and would cause dangerous hypokalemia, so insulin is withheld until potassium is replaced to at least 3.5 mEq/L; fluids continue meanwhile. Urine output confirms that potassium can be given safely.
- **원리**: <b>Why the serum potassium hides a large deficit</b>: Osmotic diuresis and vomiting waste several hundred milliequivalents of potassium, and secondary hyperaldosteronism adds more urinary loss. Yet insulin deficiency and hyperosmolality keep potassium outside cells, so most patients present with a normal or high serum potassium. A <b>low</b> potassium at presentation therefore signals a profound total-body deficit.<br> <b>Why insulin is the dangerous step</b>: Insulin activates Na⁺-K⁺-ATPase and moves potassium into cells within minutes; correcting acidosis adds to the shift. Starting insulin at 3.1 mEq/L can drop potassium below 2.5 mEq/L, causing respiratory muscle weakness and ventricular arrhythmias.<br> <b>The rule by potassium level</b>: < 3.5 — hold insulin and give 10–20 mEq/h of potassium; 3.5–5.0 — give insulin with 20–30 mEq of potassium per liter; > 5.0 — give insulin and recheck every 2 hours without potassium.<br> <b>Why not bicarbonate</b>: Bicarbonate is reserved for pH below about 7.0; it also shifts potassium into cells.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Potassium first (answer)</th><th>Insulin infusion now (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Serum K⁺</td><td><b>&lt; 3.5 mEq/L</b></td><td>≥ 3.5 mEq/L</td></tr> <tr><td>Effect on K⁺</td><td>Rebuilds the extracellular level before the shift</td><td>Moves K⁺ into cells within minutes</td></tr> <tr><td>Why the threshold</td><td>Below 3.5, the insulin shift risks arrhythmia and respiratory weakness</td><td>Above 3.5, insulin with K⁺ in the fluids is safe</td></tr> <tr><td>This patient</td><td>K⁺ 3.1, urine output 60 mL/h — indicated</td><td>Not safe yet</td></tr> </tbody></table> Insulin is the cure for ketoacidosis, but potassium decides when it can start.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) An insulin infusion at 0.1 U/kg/h would be correct once potassium is at least 3.5 mEq/L, together with potassium in the fluids.
  - (C) Sodium bicarbonate would be considered only if arterial pH were below about 7.0, and it would still worsen hypokalemia.
  - (D) Dextrose in 0.45% saline would be correct once glucose falls to about 200–250 mg/dL during insulin therapy, to avoid hypoglycemia while ketosis clears.
  - (E) Subcutaneous glargine would be correct after the anion gap closes and the patient can eat, overlapping intravenous insulin by 1–2 hours.
- **함정**: Starting insulin immediately because it treats ketoacidosis — at a potassium of 3.1 mEq/L insulin is the step that can kill.
- **학습목표**: In diabetic ketoacidosis with an initial potassium below 3.5 mEq/L, hold insulin and replace potassium first, because insulin shifts potassium into cells and can precipitate fatal arrhythmia

## 문제
A 58-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department 6 hours after the onset of vomiting, diffuse abdominal pain, and lightheadedness. She has rheumatoid arthritis treated with prednisone 15 mg daily for the past 3 years; she stopped taking it 5 days ago when she ran out of tablets while traveling. Her temperature is 37.9°C (100.2°F), pulse is 118/min, and blood pressure is 76/44 mm Hg. She is drowsy but oriented. The abdomen is diffusely tender without guarding. Serum studies show sodium 129 mEq/L, potassium 4.6 mEq/L, glucose 62 mg/dL, and creatinine 1.3 mg/dL. Blood cultures are obtained. After 1 L of intravenous 0.9% saline, her blood pressure is 82/50 mm Hg. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Resume oral prednisone 15 mg daily
- B. Intravenous hydrocortisone 100 mg
- C. Cosyntropin stimulation test before any steroid
- D. Norepinephrine infusion without corticosteroids
- E. Oral fludrocortisone 0.1 mg daily

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Three years of prednisone suppressed the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis; stopping it abruptly left her without cortisol. Hypotension unresponsive to fluids, vomiting, abdominal pain, hypoglycemia, and hyponatremia indicate adrenal crisis. Intravenous hydrocortisone 100 mg must be given immediately, with continued saline and dextrose; diagnostic testing must not delay treatment.
- **원리**: <b>Why stopping prednisone causes crisis</b>: exogenous glucocorticoid suppresses CRH and ACTH. After weeks to months, the adrenal zona fasciculata atrophies and cannot produce cortisol on demand. When the drug stops, there is neither drug nor endogenous cortisol.<br> <b>Why blood pressure falls</b>: cortisol is required for the vascular response to catecholamines (it maintains adrenergic receptor expression and angiotensin-sensitive tone). Without it, vessels dilate and do not respond well to fluids or vasopressors.<br> <b>Why potassium is normal</b>: this is secondary (central) adrenal insufficiency. Aldosterone is controlled mainly by renin-angiotensin and potassium, not ACTH, so mineralocorticoid function is preserved — hyperkalemia is absent and fludrocortisone is not needed. Hyponatremia arises from cortisol deficiency increasing ADH release.<br> <b>Why hydrocortisone at 100 mg</b>: at this dose hydrocortisone also has mineralocorticoid activity, it acts within hours, and it treats every form of adrenal insufficiency. If testing is desired, dexamethasone could be used because it does not cross-react with cortisol assays, but treatment comes first.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Oral prednisone would be appropriate for a mild missed dose in a patient who can take oral medication; vomiting and shock require parenteral therapy.
  - (C) A cosyntropin test would be correct in a hemodynamically stable patient with suspected adrenal insufficiency, such as one with fatigue and weight loss.
  - (D) Norepinephrine without steroids would be inappropriate; vasopressors are added to steroids only if hypotension persists after hydrocortisone and fluids.
  - (E) Fludrocortisone would be correct as maintenance therapy for primary adrenal insufficiency with hyperkalemia; it acts too slowly and is unnecessary in secondary deficiency.
- **함정**: Wanting to confirm the diagnosis with a cosyntropin test before giving steroids — in adrenal crisis, testing delays life-saving therapy.
- **학습목표**: 장기 글루코코르티코이드를 갑자기 끊은 뒤 수액에 반응하지 않는 저혈압·저혈당·저나트륨이 있으면 부신 위기로 보고 검사를 기다리지 않고 정맥 하이드로코르티손을 준다

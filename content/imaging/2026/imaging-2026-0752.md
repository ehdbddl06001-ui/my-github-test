## 문제
A 34-year-old man comes to the clinic for a preemployment health examination at a hospital. He feels well and has no history of jaundice. He does not know whether he received hepatitis B vaccination as a child. He drinks alcohol occasionally and takes no medications. Physical examination shows no abnormalities. Serum ALT activity is 22 U/L. Serologic studies show: HBsAg negative; anti-HBs positive (180 mIU/mL); IgM anti-HBc negative; IgG anti-HBc positive; HBV DNA undetectable. Which of the following best explains these findings?

- A. Occult hepatitis B infection with ongoing replication
- B. Resolved past hepatitis B infection with immunity
- C. Immunity from hepatitis B vaccination alone
- D. Chronic hepatitis B infection in an inactive phase
- E. Acute hepatitis B infection in the window period

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: HBsAg is negative and HBV DNA is undetectable, so there is no current infection. <b>Anti-HBc (IgG) is positive</b>, which can arise only from exposure to the virus itself because the vaccine contains HBsAg alone. <b>Anti-HBs is positive</b>, showing protective immunity. Positive anti-HBc plus positive anti-HBs with negative HBsAg is resolved natural infection.
- **원리**: <b>Why anti-HBc separates infection from vaccination</b>: Hepatitis B vaccines are recombinant HBsAg. A vaccinated person therefore makes only anti-HBs. The core antigen is made only inside infected hepatocytes during viral replication, so any anti-HBc — IgM early, IgG for life — is proof that the virus once replicated in the liver.<br> <b>Reading the panel in order</b>: HBsAg tells whether infection is present now; anti-HBc tells whether infection ever occurred; anti-HBs tells whether there is immunity. IgM anti-HBc marks recent infection and is the only marker during the window period, when HBsAg has cleared but anti-HBs has not yet appeared.<br> <b>Why it still matters</b>: Even after recovery, covalently closed circular DNA persists in hepatocytes. Strong immunosuppression such as rituximab can reactivate HBV in an anti-HBc-positive person, so this result must be remembered before such therapy.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Marker</th><th style="width:38%">Resolved infection (answer)</th><th>Vaccination (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>HBsAg</td><td>Negative</td><td>Negative</td></tr> <tr><td>Anti-HBs</td><td>Positive</td><td>Positive</td></tr> <tr><td>Anti-HBc (total/IgG)</td><td><b>Positive</b></td><td><b>Negative</b></td></tr> <tr><td>Reactivation risk with rituximab</td><td>Present</td><td>None</td></tr> </tbody></table> Only anti-HBc differs; when the two rows look alike, look for the core antibody.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Occult infection would be correct if HBV DNA were detectable in serum or liver despite negative HBsAg, usually with anti-HBc.
  - (C) Vaccine-only immunity would be correct if anti-HBs were positive but anti-HBc were negative, because the vaccine contains no core antigen.
  - (D) Inactive chronic hepatitis B would be correct if HBsAg remained positive for more than 6 months with low HBV DNA and normal ALT.
  - (E) The window period would be correct if both HBsAg and anti-HBs were negative and IgM anti-HBc were positive after recent acute hepatitis.
- **함정**: Assuming that a positive anti-HBs always means vaccination, ignoring the anti-HBc result.
- **학습목표**: Interpret a hepatitis B serologic panel with negative HBsAg, positive anti-HBs, and positive IgG anti-HBc as resolved past infection with immunity, and distinguish it from vaccine-induced immunity

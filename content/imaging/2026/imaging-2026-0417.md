## 문제
A 46-year-old man with bipolar I disorder is brought to the emergency department by his wife because of 2 days of worsening confusion, slurred speech, and diarrhea. He has taken lithium carbonate 900 mg daily for 6 years, with stable serum lithium concentrations of 0.7 to 0.9 mEq/L. Three weeks ago, hydrochlorothiazide was started for hypertension, and 5 days ago he began taking ibuprofen for back pain. His temperature is 37.1°C (98.8°F), pulse is 64/min, and blood pressure is 128/80 mm Hg. He is disoriented to time and place. There is a coarse tremor of both hands and multifocal myoclonus. Serum sodium is 136 mEq/L, creatinine is 2.4 mg/dL (baseline 1.0 mg/dL), and lithium is 4.1 mEq/L. An ECG shows sinus rhythm with flattened T waves. Lithium, hydrochlorothiazide, and ibuprofen are discontinued, and intravenous 0.9% saline is started. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Saline alone with a repeat lithium level in 6 hours
- B. Hemodialysis
- C. Activated charcoal by nasogastric tube
- D. Whole-bowel irrigation with polyethylene glycol
- E. Oral sodium polystyrene sulfonate

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Hydrochlorothiazide and ibuprofen lowered renal lithium clearance and precipitated acute kidney injury, producing chronic lithium toxicity with a level of 4.1 mEq/L, confusion, and myoclonus. Because lithium is a small, unbound, water-soluble ion, it is efficiently removed by hemodialysis, which is recommended when the level exceeds 4.0 mEq/L with impaired kidney function or when there is a decreased level of consciousness.
- **원리**: <b>How the kidney handles lithium</b>: lithium is filtered freely and about 80% is reabsorbed in the proximal tubule along with sodium. Anything that makes the proximal tubule avidly reclaim sodium — volume depletion, thiazides (which cause mild volume contraction), or reduced GFR — increases lithium reabsorption. NSAIDs reduce renal prostaglandins and GFR, and ACE inhibitors act similarly.<br> <b>Why chronic toxicity is worse at a given level</b>: in chronic poisoning, lithium has already distributed into the brain, so neurologic toxicity (confusion, myoclonus, seizures) appears at lower serum levels and persists longer than in an acute overdose, where the serum level is high but the brain is not yet loaded.<br> <b>Why dialysis works</b>: lithium has a low molecular weight, no protein binding, and a small volume of distribution, so dialysis clears it several times faster than even normal kidneys. The EXTRIP workgroup recommends it for a level above 4.0 mEq/L with impaired kidney function, or for decreased consciousness, seizures, or dysrhythmias regardless of level.<br> <b>Why charcoal does not help</b>: lithium, like other metals and alcohols, does not bind activated charcoal.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Hemodialysis (answer)</th><th>Saline alone (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Lithium level</td><td><b>&gt; 4.0 mEq/L with impaired kidneys</b></td><td>Mildly elevated (e.g., 1.5–2.5 mEq/L)</td></tr> <tr><td>Kidney function</td><td>Creatinine 2.4 (acute kidney injury)</td><td>Normal or rapidly recovering</td></tr> <tr><td>Neurologic state</td><td>Confusion, myoclonus</td><td>Tremor only, alert</td></tr> </tbody></table> Saline restores sodium and GFR so the kidney can excrete lithium, but with creatinine more than doubled and the brain already affected, clearance is too slow; dialysis removes it directly.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Saline alone would be correct for mild chronic toxicity with a lower level, preserved kidney function, and only a tremor, with frequent repeat levels.
  - (C) Activated charcoal would be appropriate for a recent co-ingestion of a drug that it binds, such as a tricyclic antidepressant; lithium is not adsorbed.
  - (D) Whole-bowel irrigation would be correct after an acute ingestion of sustained-release lithium tablets; in chronic toxicity there is no drug left in the gut.
  - (E) Sodium polystyrene sulfonate binds lithium modestly in experiments but causes hypokalemia and bowel injury and is not a recommended therapy.
- **함정**: Choosing saline alone because the level might fall with hydration — a level above 4.0 mEq/L with acute kidney injury and encephalopathy needs dialysis.
- **학습목표**: 티아지드·NSAID 추가로 리튬 제거가 줄어 생긴 만성 리튬 중독에서 신기능 저하와 신경 증상이 있고 농도가 4.0 mEq/L 을 넘으면 혈액투석을 고른다

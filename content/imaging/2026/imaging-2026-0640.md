## 문제
A 58-year-old woman comes to the physician because of 3 episodes of vaginal spotting during the past month. Her last menstrual period was 7 years ago, and she has not taken hormone therapy. She has type 2 diabetes mellitus and hypertension. Her body mass index is 34 kg/m². Her blood pressure is 136/84 mm Hg. Pelvic examination shows a normal-appearing cervix and a small amount of blood in the vaginal vault; the uterus is normal in size, and there are no adnexal masses. A cervical cytology test 1 year ago was negative for intraepithelial lesion and malignancy, with negative human papillomavirus testing. Transvaginal ultrasonography shows a homogeneous endometrial stripe of 9 mm and normal ovaries. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Endometrial biopsy
- B. Repeat transvaginal ultrasonography in 3 months
- C. Total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy
- D. Cyclic progestin therapy
- E. Repeat cervical cytology

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: Any bleeding after menopause must be evaluated for endometrial carcinoma. On transvaginal ultrasonography, an endometrial thickness of 4 mm or less has a very high negative predictive value, but her stripe is 9 mm, and she has risk factors for unopposed estrogen (obesity, diabetes). Tissue sampling is therefore required, and office endometrial biopsy is the next step; hysteroscopy with dilation and curettage follows if the biopsy is nondiagnostic or bleeding persists.
- **원리**: <b>Why the 4-mm threshold</b>: After menopause, the endometrium is no longer stimulated by cyclic estrogen and becomes thin and atrophic. In women with postmenopausal bleeding, an endometrial thickness ≤ 4 mm on transvaginal ultrasonography makes carcinoma very unlikely (negative predictive value > 99 %), so atrophy is the presumed cause. A thicker stripe means hyperplasia, polyp, or carcinoma cannot be excluded and tissue is needed.<br> <b>Why this patient is at risk</b>: Adipose tissue converts androstenedione to estrone by aromatase, producing estrogen unopposed by progesterone. Obesity, diabetes, nulliparity, late menopause, and tamoxifen raise the risk of endometrial hyperplasia and type I (endometrioid) carcinoma.<br> <b>Sampling sequence</b>: office endometrial biopsy (pipelle) first; if nondiagnostic, insufficient, or bleeding recurs despite a benign result, hysteroscopy with directed biopsy or dilation and curettage. Surgery is planned only after a tissue diagnosis.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Repeat transvaginal ultrasonography in 3 months would be reasonable only if the endometrial stripe were 4 mm or less and bleeding did not recur.
  - (C) Total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy would be appropriate after biopsy confirmed endometrial carcinoma or atypical hyperplasia.
  - (D) Cyclic progestin therapy would be used for anovulatory bleeding in a premenopausal woman or for hyperplasia without atypia after tissue diagnosis.
  - (E) Repeat cervical cytology would be indicated if the cervix appeared abnormal or cervical screening were overdue, which is not the case here.
- **함정**: Attributing postmenopausal bleeding to atrophy without measuring the endometrium — a stripe over 4 mm requires tissue sampling.
- **학습목표**: Evaluate postmenopausal bleeding with an endometrial thickness above 4 mm by endometrial sampling, and separate it from expectant management appropriate for a thin endometrium

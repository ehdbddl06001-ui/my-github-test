## 문제
A 38-year-old man comes to the physician because of a painful ulcer on his left shin that began 3 weeks ago as a small pustule at the site of a minor scrape and has enlarged rapidly despite 10 days of oral cephalexin. He has ulcerative colitis treated with mesalamine and has had increased stool frequency with blood for the past month. Temperature is 37.4°C (99.3°F), pulse is 86/min, and blood pressure is 122/76 mm Hg. Examination shows a 6-cm ulcer with a purulent base and an undermined, violaceous border. Pedal pulses are normal. Bacterial culture of the ulcer base grows only normal skin flora. A skin biopsy specimen taken from the ulcer edge shows a dense neutrophilic infiltrate without vasculitis or organisms. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Hyperbaric oxygen therapy
- B. Oral prednisone
- C. Surgical debridement of the ulcer
- D. Intravenous vancomycin
- E. Compression bandaging

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: A rapidly enlarging, painful ulcer with an undermined violaceous border, starting at a minor injury (pathergy), in a patient with active ulcerative colitis, with sterile cultures and a neutrophilic infiltrate without vasculitis or infection, is pyoderma gangrenosum. Treatment is immunosuppression — systemic glucocorticoids (or cyclosporine) for a large or rapidly progressing lesion — with control of the bowel disease.
- **원리**: <b>Why it is a neutrophilic, not infectious, ulcer</b>: pyoderma gangrenosum is a neutrophilic dermatosis — dysregulated innate immunity (IL-1, IL-8, TNF-α, IL-17) draws neutrophils into the dermis, which destroy tissue and form a sterile ulcer. About half of patients have a systemic disease, most often inflammatory bowel disease, rheumatoid arthritis, or a hematologic malignancy, and activity often parallels the bowel disease.<br> <b>Why surgery makes it worse</b>: the hallmark is pathergy — minor trauma, including debridement, needle sticks, or skin grafting, triggers new lesions or enlargement. That is why a lesion that started at a scrape keeps growing, and why aggressive debridement is avoided.<br> <b>How to treat</b>: small lesions may respond to potent topical steroids or tacrolimus, but a large, rapidly enlarging ulcer needs systemic therapy — prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day or cyclosporine — and in IBD-associated disease anti-TNF therapy (infliximab) treats both skin and bowel. Wound care is gentle and moist.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Hyperbaric oxygen is an adjunct for refractory diabetic foot ulcers or radiation injury, not for a neutrophilic dermatosis.
  - (C) Surgical debridement would be correct for a necrotizing soft-tissue infection with crepitus and systemic toxicity; in pyoderma gangrenosum it provokes pathergy.
  - (D) Intravenous vancomycin would be correct if cultures grew MRSA with spreading cellulitis and fever, which is not the case here.
  - (E) Compression bandaging is the treatment of a venous ulcer over the medial malleolus with edema and hemosiderin staining.
- **함정**: Reading the purulent base as infection and escalating antibiotics or debriding — the culture is sterile and debridement worsens pyoderma gangrenosum.
- **학습목표**: 궤양성대장염 환자의 작은 상처에서 시작해 빠르게 커지는 보라색 경계의 통증성 궤양을 괴저농피증으로 진단하고, 수술적 변연절제를 피하며 전신 글루코코르티코이드로 치료한다

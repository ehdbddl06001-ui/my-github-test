## 문제
A 44-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of a 2-day history of pain and swelling of the right knee. He has had two similar episodes in the left knee during the past year that resolved within 10 days. He also reports fatigue and decreased libido for 1 year. He drinks alcohol occasionally. Temperature is 37.6°C. The right knee is warm and swollen with a large effusion. There is bony enlargement of the second and third metacarpophalangeal joints of both hands. Synovial fluid analysis shows a leukocyte count of 18,000/mm3 (85% neutrophils) and rhomboid-shaped crystals that are weakly positively birefringent under polarized light; Gram stain is negative. X-rays of both knees show linear calcification of the menisci. Serum alanine aminotransferase is 68 U/L, and the fasting glucose is 128 mg/dL. Which of the following serum studies is most likely to identify the underlying cause of this condition?

- A. Antinuclear antibody titer
- B. Transferrin saturation and ferritin
- C. Serum uric acid concentration
- D. Rheumatoid factor and anti-CCP
- E. HLA-B27 genotyping

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: Rhomboid, weakly positively birefringent crystals with meniscal chondrocalcinosis indicate calcium pyrophosphate deposition (pseudogout). CPPD before age 55 calls for a search for a metabolic cause; second and third MCP arthropathy, fatigue, low libido (hypogonadism), elevated ALT, and hyperglycemia point to hereditary hemochromatosis, screened with transferrin saturation and ferritin.
- **원리**: <b>Why young CPPD is a clue</b>: pyrophosphate crystals usually form in aging cartilage; when they appear before about 55 years, cartilage chemistry has been altered by a metabolic disorder — hemochromatosis, hyperparathyroidism, hypomagnesemia, or hypophosphatasia.<br> <b>How iron causes it</b>: iron deposited in synovium and cartilage inhibits pyrophosphatase, raising local inorganic pyrophosphate that combines with calcium to form crystals. Iron also damages chondrocytes directly, producing a characteristic degenerative arthropathy of the 2nd and 3rd MCP joints with hook-like osteophytes.<br> <b>The multisystem picture</b>: iron in the liver (elevated aminotransferases, cirrhosis), pancreas (diabetes), pituitary (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism — low libido), heart, and skin. Transferrin saturation above 45 % is the most sensitive first test; ferritin reflects stores; HFE genotyping follows.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Antinuclear antibodies are correct for suspected lupus with non-erosive arthritis, rash, or cytopenias, none of which are present here.
  - (C) Uric acid is correct when needle-shaped, strongly negatively birefringent crystals indicate gout; it does not explain pyrophosphate deposition.
  - (D) Rheumatoid factor and anti-CCP are correct for symmetric small-joint synovitis with erosions; MCP hook osteophytes with crystals point elsewhere.
  - (E) HLA-B27 is correct for inflammatory back pain or reactive arthritis with enthesitis, not for crystal-proven monoarthritis with chondrocalcinosis.
- **함정**: Stopping at 'pseudogout' and treating — in a 44-year-old the crystal is a sign; MCP 2–3 changes, hypogonadism, and liver enzymes point to iron overload.
- **학습목표**: 55세 미만에서 생긴 CPPD 관절염은 혈색소증·부갑상샘기능항진증·저마그네슘혈증 같은 대사 원인을 찾아야 하며, 그중 혈색소증 선별에 철 검사를 고른다

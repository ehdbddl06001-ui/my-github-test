## 문제
A 29-year-old woman, gravida 1, para 0, comes to the emergency department because of light vaginal bleeding and mild left lower abdominal pain for 2 days. Her last menstrual period was 6 weeks ago. Temperature is 36.8°C, pulse is 78/min, and blood pressure is 118/74 mm Hg. The abdomen is soft with mild left lower quadrant tenderness and no rebound. Serum β-hCG concentration is 2,400 mIU/mL; it was 2,100 mIU/mL 48 hours ago. Transvaginal ultrasonography shows no intrauterine gestational sac and a 2.5-cm left adnexal mass containing a yolk sac without embryonic cardiac activity; there is no free fluid. Hemoglobin, serum creatinine, and aminotransferase levels are normal. She wishes to preserve fertility and can return for follow-up visits. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Expectant management with weekly β-hCG
- B. Intramuscular methotrexate
- C. Laparoscopic salpingectomy
- D. Dilation and curettage
- E. Repeat β-hCG measurement in 48 hours

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답 핵심**: A <b>yolk sac inside an adnexal mass with an empty uterus</b> confirms a tubal ectopic pregnancy, and the β-hCG plateau (14% rise in 48 hours) fits an abnormal pregnancy. She is <b>hemodynamically stable with no free fluid</b>, the β-hCG is <b>below 5,000 mIU/mL</b>, the mass is small with no cardiac activity, and renal and liver function are normal with reliable follow-up — all criteria for medical treatment with intramuscular methotrexate.
- **원리**: <b>Why methotrexate works</b>: Methotrexate inhibits dihydrofolate reductase and so blocks DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing cells. Trophoblast is among the fastest-dividing tissues, so a small, early ectopic pregnancy can be resolved without surgery and the tube preserved.<br> <b>Why the selection criteria matter</b>: Success falls as trophoblast mass rises. A β-hCG above about 5,000 mIU/mL, embryonic cardiac activity, or a mass larger than about 3.5–4 cm predicts failure and later rupture. Rupture is a surgical emergency, so methotrexate is reserved for stable patients without signs of rupture who can return for serial β-hCG on days 4 and 7 until the level is undetectable.<br> <b>Why the diagnosis is secure without more waiting</b>: An extrauterine yolk sac or embryo is diagnostic. Without it, an adnexal mass and an abnormal β-hCG trend would only make ectopic pregnancy likely, and repeating β-hCG to exclude an early intrauterine pregnancy would be reasonable before giving a teratogen.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Expectant management would be correct if the β-hCG were low (for example below 200 mIU/mL) and already falling.
  - (C) Laparoscopic salpingectomy would be correct with signs of rupture, embryonic cardiac activity, a very high β-hCG, or inability to follow up.
  - (D) Dilation and curettage would be correct to look for chorionic villi when the location is unknown and the pregnancy is clearly nonviable.
  - (E) Repeating β-hCG would be correct if ultrasound showed no extrauterine yolk sac or embryo and the location were still uncertain.
- **함정**: Choosing surgery for any confirmed ectopic pregnancy without applying the criteria for medical treatment.
- **학습목표**: Select intramuscular methotrexate for a hemodynamically stable, unruptured tubal ectopic pregnancy with a low β-hCG, a small mass without cardiac activity, and reliable follow-up

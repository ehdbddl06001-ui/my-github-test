## 문제
A 52-year-old woman who works as a public health officer reviews registry data for a chronic autoimmune disease in her country. For the past decade, the incidence has remained stable at 20 new cases per 100,000 persons per year. Before a new disease-modifying drug became available, patients lived an average of 4 years after diagnosis. The drug does not cure the disease, but it extends average survival after diagnosis to 10 years, and it is rapidly adopted nationwide. Risk factors for the disease and diagnostic criteria do not change, and the population size remains constant. Which of the following best describes the expected incidence and prevalence once a new steady state is reached?

- A. Incidence falls; prevalence stays at about 80 per 100,000
- B. Incidence rises to 50 per 100,000 per year; prevalence stays at about 80 per 100,000
- C. Incidence and prevalence both unchanged because the disease is not cured
- D. Incidence unchanged; prevalence rises from about 80 to about 200 per 100,000
- E. Incidence unchanged; prevalence falls from about 200 to about 80 per 100,000

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: D

- **정답 핵심**: At steady state, prevalence ≈ incidence × average duration of disease (when prevalence is low). Before the drug: 20 per 100,000 per year × 4 years = 80 per 100,000. After the drug: 20 × 10 = 200 per 100,000. Incidence counts only new cases, which depend on risk factors and diagnostic criteria — neither changed — so it stays at 20 per 100,000 per year. A treatment that prolongs life without cure keeps people in the prevalent pool longer, so prevalence rises.
- **원리**: <b>Why prevalence behaves like water in a bathtub</b>: new cases flow in at the incidence rate, and patients leave the pool by cure or death. At steady state, inflow equals outflow, so the number in the tub equals the inflow rate multiplied by how long each person stays: P ≈ I × D (strictly, P/(1 − P) = I × D, which is nearly identical when prevalence is small).<br> <b>What moves each measure</b>: incidence changes only when the rate of new disease changes — new exposures, prevention, screening that detects more cases, or new diagnostic criteria. Prevalence changes with incidence <b>or</b> duration. A cure or rapid death shortens duration and lowers prevalence; a life-prolonging but non-curative therapy lengthens duration and raises prevalence.<br> <b>Why it matters in practice</b>: a rising prevalence after a successful treatment is not evidence that the disease is becoming more common, and it raises the number of patients needing long-term care and drug supply — a planning issue for health services (as with HIV after antiretroviral therapy).
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Change</th><th style="width:39%">Life-prolonging, non-curative drug (this case)</th><th>Curative drug (distractor pattern)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Incidence</td><td>Unchanged (20/100,000/year)</td><td>Unchanged</td></tr> <tr><td>Duration</td><td>Longer (4 → 10 years)</td><td>Shorter</td></tr> <tr><td>Prevalence</td><td><b>Rises</b> (80 → 200/100,000)</td><td><b>Falls</b></td></tr> </tbody></table> Ask two questions in order: did the rate of new cases change (incidence), and did the time spent sick change (duration)? Prevalence follows their product.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Incidence falls only when fewer new cases arise — after primary prevention or removal of a risk factor; this drug acts after diagnosis and risk factors are unchanged, so incidence is unaffected.
  - (B) Incidence would rise with a new exposure, a screening program that detects more cases, or broader diagnostic criteria; none occurred, and longer survival alone raises prevalence, not incidence.
  - (C) Prevalence stays the same only when both incidence and average duration stay the same; because the drug extends duration without cure, patients remain in the prevalent pool longer and prevalence rises.
  - (E) A fall in prevalence from 200 to 80 would follow a treatment that shortens disease duration, such as a cure or a rise in early deaths; here the drug lengthens survival from 4 to 10 years.
- **함정**: Assuming that a successful drug must lower prevalence — without cure, longer survival enlarges the pool of people living with the disease.
- **학습목표**: 정상 상태에서 유병률 ≈ 발생률 × 평균 유병 기간임을 알고, 완치 없이 생존만 늘리는 치료가 발생률은 그대로 두고 유병률을 높임을 계산으로 보인다

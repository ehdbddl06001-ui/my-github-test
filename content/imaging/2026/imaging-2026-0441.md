## 문제
A 66-year-old man asks his physician whether he should join a regional screening program for pancreatic cancer that uses annual imaging. The program brochure reports that, among participants whose cancer was detected by screening, mean survival after diagnosis is 3.4 years, compared with 1.2 years among people in a neighboring region without screening whose cancer was diagnosed after symptoms appeared. The two regions have similar age, sex, and smoking distributions. In both regions, the mean age at death from pancreatic cancer is 71 years, and pancreatic cancer mortality per 100,000 person-years has not changed since the program began. Screen-detected and symptom-detected tumors have a similar proportion of slow-growing, low-grade histology, and almost all screen-detected cancers progressed to symptomatic disease during follow-up. Which of the following best explains the longer survival after diagnosis in the screened group?

- A. Lead-time bias
- B. Length-time bias
- C. Overdiagnosis bias
- D. Volunteer (selection) bias
- E. Confounding by age

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: Survival after diagnosis is longer in the screened group, but people die at the same age and disease-specific mortality is unchanged. Screening has only moved the date of diagnosis earlier, adding the interval between screen detection and the time symptoms would have appeared — lead-time bias. Similar tumor grades exclude length-time bias, progression of nearly all screen-detected cancers excludes overdiagnosis, and unchanged population mortality with similar demographics argues against volunteer bias or confounding.
- **원리**: <b>Why survival from diagnosis is a misleading yardstick</b>: survival is measured from the date of diagnosis to death. Screening detects disease in its preclinical phase — the sojourn time before symptoms. If earlier treatment does not change the course, death occurs on the same date, but the clock started earlier, so survival appears longer by exactly the lead time.<br> <b>How the three screening biases differ</b>: lead-time bias moves the starting point; <b>length-time bias</b> arises because periodic screening preferentially catches slow-growing tumors with long preclinical phases, which have better prognosis anyway; <b>overdiagnosis</b> is the extreme form, detecting tumors that would never have caused symptoms or death. Each inflates survival and case counts without benefit.<br> <b>The unbiased outcome</b>: disease-specific mortality in the whole population (deaths per person-years), ideally from a randomized trial of screening versus no screening. Only a fall in mortality shows that screening changes outcomes.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Lead-time bias (answer)</th><th>Length-time bias (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Cause</td><td><b>Earlier diagnosis date, same death date</b></td><td>Screening preferentially finds slow-growing tumors</td></tr> <tr><td>Tumor biology in screened group</td><td>Same as symptomatic tumors</td><td>More indolent, lower grade</td></tr> <tr><td>Age at death / mortality</td><td>Unchanged</td><td>Unchanged</td></tr> </tbody></table> Both leave mortality unchanged; the tell is tumor biology — similar grades point to lead time, an excess of indolent tumors points to length time.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Length-time bias would explain the result if screen-detected tumors were disproportionately slow-growing and low-grade; here the grade distribution is similar.
  - (C) Overdiagnosis would be likely if many screen-detected cancers never progressed or caused symptoms; almost all of these progressed during follow-up.
  - (D) Volunteer bias would apply if healthier, health-conscious people joined the program, which would tend to lower their mortality; mortality was unchanged.
  - (E) Confounding by age would require the regions to differ in age; their age, sex, and smoking distributions are similar.
- **함정**: Choosing length-time bias because survival is longer with screening — it needs indolent tumors; with similar biology and unchanged death age, only the start of the clock moved.
- **학습목표**: 선별검사 군의 진단 후 생존이 길어졌지만 사망 나이와 질병 사망률이 그대로면 리드타임 비뚤림으로 설명하고, 길이 비뚤림·과잉진단과 구별한다

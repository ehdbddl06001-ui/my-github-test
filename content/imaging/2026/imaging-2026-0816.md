## 문제
A 29-year-old woman, gravida 0, comes to the office because she has been unable to conceive despite 2 years of regular unprotected intercourse. She has had progressively severe menstrual pain that begins 2 days before menses, deep pain during intercourse, and painful defecation during menses. Her menstrual cycles are regular every 28 days with normal flow. Her partner's semen analysis is normal. Pelvic examination shows a normal-sized, fixed, retroverted uterus and tender nodularity along the uterosacral ligaments. Transvaginal ultrasonography shows a 4-cm left ovarian cyst with diffuse homogeneous low-level internal echoes and no solid component. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

- A. Endometriosis
- B. Adenomyosis
- C. Chronic pelvic inflammatory disease
- D. Uterine leiomyoma
- E. Mature cystic teratoma

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: Cyclic pain that starts <b>before menses</b>, <b>deep dyspareunia</b>, and <b>dyschezia during menses</b> point to implants in the posterior cul-de-sac and uterosacral ligaments. The exam shows <b>tender uterosacral nodularity</b> and a <b>fixed retroverted uterus</b> (adhesions) of <b>normal size</b>, and the ultrasound shows an ovarian cyst with <b>homogeneous ground-glass echoes</b> — an endometrioma. With infertility in a nulligravid 29-year-old, this is <b>endometriosis</b>.
- **원리**: <b>Ectopic endometrium behaves like endometrium</b>: Implants outside the uterus (most often on the ovaries, cul-de-sac, and uterosacral ligaments) proliferate and bleed with each cycle. The bleeding triggers inflammation, fibrosis, and adhesions — hence pain that starts before menses and deep pain when the posterior cul-de-sac is stretched (intercourse, defecation).<br> <b>Why the ovarian cyst looks like ground glass</b>: Repeated bleeding into an ovarian implant forms a cyst filled with old, thick blood ("chocolate cyst"). The uniform hemosiderin-rich fluid produces diffuse low-level echoes on ultrasound.<br> <b>Why infertility occurs</b>: Adhesions distort tubo-ovarian anatomy and impair oocyte pickup, and the inflammatory peritoneal fluid is toxic to gametes and embryos. Definitive diagnosis is by laparoscopy, which also allows excision; first-line pain treatment is NSAIDs plus hormonal suppression, but suppression does not help conception.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Endometriosis (answer)</th><th>Adenomyosis (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Typical patient</td><td><b>Young, nulligravid, infertile</b></td><td>Multiparous, 40s</td></tr> <tr><td>Uterus</td><td><b>Normal size</b>, often fixed retroverted</td><td>Diffusely enlarged, boggy, tender</td></tr> <tr><td>Other findings</td><td>Uterosacral nodules, endometrioma</td><td>Heavy menstrual bleeding</td></tr> </tbody></table> Both cause dysmenorrhea. A normal-sized uterus with cul-de-sac nodularity and an ovarian chocolate cyst points to endometriosis; a globally enlarged uterus with heavy bleeding in an older multiparous woman points to adenomyosis.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Adenomyosis would be the answer if a multiparous woman in her 40s had heavy menses and a diffusely enlarged, boggy, tender uterus.
  - (C) Chronic pelvic inflammatory disease would be favored by prior sexually transmitted infection, noncyclic pain, and a tubo-ovarian complex mass.
  - (D) A uterine leiomyoma would present with an enlarged, irregular, firm uterus and heavy menstrual bleeding, not uterosacral nodules.
  - (E) A mature cystic teratoma would show an echogenic fat nodule, hair lines, or calcification on ultrasound and is usually painless.
- **함정**: Choosing adenomyosis because both cause worsening dysmenorrhea — the normal-sized uterus, uterosacral nodules, and ovarian cyst point outside the uterus.
- **학습목표**: Diagnose endometriosis from cyclic dysmenorrhea, deep dyspareunia, dyschezia, uterosacral nodularity, and an ovarian cyst with homogeneous low-level echoes in a nulligravid woman with infertility, and distinguish it from adenomyosis

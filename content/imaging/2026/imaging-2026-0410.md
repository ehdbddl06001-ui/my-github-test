## 문제
A 61-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of a 4-day history of left lower quadrant abdominal pain and fever. He has hypertension treated with amlodipine. His temperature is 38.6°C (101.5°F), pulse is 102/min, and blood pressure is 132/80 mm Hg. Abdominal examination shows tenderness and vague fullness in the left lower quadrant without rigidity or rebound tenderness. Bowel sounds are normal. Leukocyte count is 16,800/mm3, and serum lactate is 1.4 mmol/L. CT of the abdomen with contrast shows sigmoid wall thickening with surrounding fat stranding and a 6-cm rim-enhancing fluid collection adjacent to the sigmoid colon; there is no free intraperitoneal air. Intravenous antibiotics are begun. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. CT-guided percutaneous drainage of the collection
- B. Emergency sigmoid resection with end colostomy
- C. Colonoscopy within the next 48 hours
- D. Oral antibiotics and outpatient follow-up
- E. Laparoscopic peritoneal lavage

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: He has sigmoid diverticulitis complicated by a 6-cm pericolic abscess (Hinchey II) but no generalized peritonitis, free air, or sepsis with hypoperfusion. Abscesses of about 4 cm or larger respond poorly to antibiotics alone, so the next step is CT-guided percutaneous drainage together with intravenous antibiotics; this usually controls sepsis and allows a later elective one-stage resection if needed.
- **원리**: <b>Why size matters</b>: antibiotics reach an abscess cavity poorly because it has no blood supply and its acidic pus inactivates some drugs. Small abscesses (under about 3–4 cm) usually resolve with antibiotics; larger ones need the pus removed.<br> <b>Why drainage rather than surgery</b>: the infection is contained by omentum and adjacent bowel. Draining it through the skin under CT guidance controls the source without a laparotomy, and the inflamed colon can then cool down. If surgery is needed later, it can be done electively as a single resection with primary anastomosis instead of an emergency Hartmann procedure with a stoma.<br> <b>Hinchey stages</b>: I pericolic abscess, II distant (pelvic) abscess, III purulent peritonitis, IV feculent peritonitis. Stages III–IV, free air with peritonitis, or failure of drainage call for operation.<br> <b>Colonoscopy</b> is done about 6–8 weeks after recovery to exclude carcinoma, never in the acute phase because insufflation can perforate the inflamed wall.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Percutaneous drainage (answer)</th><th>Emergency Hartmann procedure (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Abdominal examination</td><td><b>Localized tenderness, no rigidity</b></td><td>Generalized peritonitis, rigidity</td></tr> <tr><td>CT</td><td>Contained abscess ≥ 4 cm, no free air</td><td>Free air or free fluid throughout the abdomen (Hinchey III–IV)</td></tr> <tr><td>Physiology</td><td>Stable, lactate normal</td><td>Septic shock or failure of drainage</td></tr> </tbody></table> The same disease moves from radiology to the operating room when the contamination is no longer walled off.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Emergency sigmoid resection would be correct if he had generalized peritonitis, free intraperitoneal air, or septic shock, or if drainage failed.
  - (C) Colonoscopy would be correct 6 to 8 weeks after the episode has resolved, to exclude cancer; in acute diverticulitis it risks perforation.
  - (D) Oral outpatient antibiotics would be correct for uncomplicated diverticulitis without abscess in a patient who tolerates oral intake and has no sepsis.
  - (E) Laparoscopic lavage was proposed for purulent (Hinchey III) peritonitis; it does not drain a walled-off abscess and has higher reintervention rates.
- **함정**: Jumping to emergency resection because there is an abscess — a contained abscess without peritonitis is drained percutaneously first.
- **학습목표**: 복막염·유리 공기가 없는 게실염에 4 cm 이상 고름집이 있으면 항생제와 함께 CT 유도 경피 배액을 하고, 범발성 복막염의 응급 수술과 구별한다

## 문제
A 34-year-old man comes to the physician because of a rash on his right thigh for 5 days. Ten days ago he went hiking in the woods of Connecticut in June. He has mild fatigue and myalgia but no joint swelling, facial weakness, or palpitations. His temperature is 37.8°C (100°F), pulse is 76/min, and blood pressure is 122/78 mm Hg. Examination shows a 9-cm, warm, nontender, annular erythematous patch with central clearing on the right thigh that has enlarged over the past 3 days. There is no vesicle or necrosis. Neurologic and cardiac examinations show no abnormalities. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Intravenous ceftriaxone for 14 days
- B. Topical clotrimazole for 2 weeks
- C. Oral doxycycline for 10 days
- D. Serologic testing for Borrelia burgdorferi before treatment
- E. Oral cephalexin for 7 days

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: An expanding annular erythema larger than 5 cm with central clearing, appearing days after outdoor exposure in a Lyme-endemic area in summer, is erythema migrans. This is clinically diagnostic of early localized Lyme disease; serology is often negative in the first weeks and is not needed. Without neurologic or cardiac involvement, oral doxycycline for 10 days is the treatment.
- **원리**: <b>Why the rash is the diagnosis</b>: After an infected Ixodes tick feeds for more than about 36 hours, Borrelia burgdorferi multiplies in the skin and spreads outward from the bite. The inflammatory front moves with the spirochetes, so the erythema expands day by day, often clearing in the center as the organisms move outward. A lesion this typical in an endemic area has a much higher predictive value than any test.<br> <b>Why serology is not helpful now</b>: IgM antibodies take 2 to 4 weeks to appear, so in the first days of erythema migrans the two-tier test is negative in most patients. Waiting for or relying on serology delays treatment, and a negative result would not change management.<br> <b>Why oral doxycycline</b>: Doxycycline reaches skin and blood well and also covers Anaplasma, which the same tick transmits. Intravenous ceftriaxone is reserved for disease that has reached the central nervous system with meningitis or radiculopathy, or for high-degree heart block.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Erythema migrans (answer — treat)</th><th>Need for serology (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Typical setting</td><td><b>Expanding rash at least 5 cm, days to 1 month after exposure</b></td><td>Later manifestations: arthritis, carditis, neuropathy without a rash</td></tr> <tr><td>Sensitivity of serology</td><td>Low in the first 2 to 4 weeks</td><td>High once disseminated (IgG positive)</td></tr> <tr><td>Basis of diagnosis</td><td>Clinical</td><td>Clinical syndrome plus positive two-tier test</td></tr> </tbody></table> Test when the picture is late or atypical; treat directly when the classic rash is present.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Intravenous ceftriaxone would be correct for Lyme meningitis or radiculopathy, or carditis with high-degree atrioventricular block.
  - (B) Topical clotrimazole would be correct for tinea corporis, which has a scaly, advancing border and enlarges slowly over weeks rather than days.
  - (D) Serologic testing would be correct for suspected later Lyme disease, such as monoarthritis of the knee or facial palsy weeks to months after exposure without a rash.
  - (E) Oral cephalexin would be correct for cellulitis, which is tender, spreads with ill-defined margins, and lacks central clearing after a skin break.
- **함정**: Ordering Lyme serology before treating a classic erythema migrans — antibodies are usually absent in the first weeks, and the rash itself is diagnostic.

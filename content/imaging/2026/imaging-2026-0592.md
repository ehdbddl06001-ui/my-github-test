## 문제
A 58-year-old man comes to the physician for a routine health maintenance examination. He feels well and has no symptoms. He asks about a new blood test for a cancer that his community plans to offer as a screening test to asymptomatic adults aged 50 to 75 years. In a validation study, the test has a sensitivity of 90% and a specificity of 95%. The prevalence of the cancer in the target population is estimated to be 2%. He wants to know how likely he is to have the cancer if his test result is positive. Which of the following is closest to the positive predictive value of the test in this population?

- A. 27%
- B. 18%
- C. 47%
- D. 90%
- E. 95%

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: In 10,000 people, 200 have the cancer and 9,800 do not. The test detects 90% of the 200 (180 true positives) and falsely labels 5% of the 9,800 (490 false positives). Positive predictive value is true positives divided by all positives: 180 ÷ (180 + 490) = 180 ÷ 670 ≈ 27%. Even with high sensitivity and specificity, most positive results are false when the disease is rare.
- **원리**: <b>Why the predictive value depends on prevalence</b>: Sensitivity and specificity are properties of the test measured within groups that do or do not have the disease. Predictive values describe the people who received a given result, so they depend on how many diseased and nondiseased people are tested. When prevalence is low, the nondiseased group is huge, and even a small false-positive rate (1 − specificity) generates more false positives than there are true positives.<br> <b>Why specificity matters most for screening rare diseases</b>: With 2% prevalence, raising specificity from 95% to 99% would cut false positives from 490 to 98 and raise PPV to about 65%; raising sensitivity to 100% would barely change PPV (200 ÷ 690 ≈ 29%). That is why screening programs target higher-risk groups (raising prevalence) or use a highly specific confirmatory test after a positive screen.<br> <b>Bayes in one line</b>: PPV = (Se × P) ÷ [Se × P + (1 − Sp) × (1 − P)].
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:26%">Measure</th><th style="width:37%">Positive predictive value (asked)</th><th>Sensitivity (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Question it answers</td><td><b>"I tested positive — do I have it?"</b></td><td>"I have it — will the test be positive?"</td></tr> <tr><td>Denominator</td><td>All positive results (TP + FP = 670)</td><td>All diseased people (TP + FN = 200)</td></tr> <tr><td>Depends on prevalence</td><td>Yes</td><td>No</td></tr> <tr><td>Value here</td><td>180 ÷ 670 ≈ 27%</td><td>180 ÷ 200 = 90%</td></tr> </tbody></table> The numerator is the same 180 true positives; choosing the right denominator is the whole question.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) 18% would be correct if the specificity were about 92% at the same prevalence, or if prevalence were about 1.2% with the same test characteristics.
  - (C) 47% would be correct if prevalence were about 5% with the same sensitivity and specificity (450 ÷ 925 true among positives in 10,000).
  - (D) 90% is the sensitivity; it would be correct if the question asked what proportion of people with the cancer test positive.
  - (E) 95% is the specificity; it would be correct if the question asked what proportion of people without the cancer test negative.
- **함정**: Reading the sensitivity as the chance that a positive person has the disease — at 2% prevalence, false positives outnumber true positives.
- **학습목표**: 민감도·특이도·유병률로 양성예측도를 계산하고 유병률이 낮을 때 양성예측도가 낮아지는 이유를 설명한다

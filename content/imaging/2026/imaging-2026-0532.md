## 문제
A 24-year-old woman, gravida 1, para 0, at 18 weeks' gestation comes to the physician because of a nonpruritic rash on her trunk, palms, and soles for 1 week. She has had mild malaise. Two months ago she noticed a painless genital sore that healed on its own. She reports developing generalized hives and wheezing after taking amoxicillin as a teenager. Temperature is 37.5°C. Examination shows scattered copper-colored macules and papules, including on the palms and soles, and generalized nontender lymphadenopathy. Rapid plasma reagin titer is 1:64, and a treponemal antibody test is positive. HIV testing is negative. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Oral doxycycline for 14 days
- B. Intramuscular ceftriaxone for 10 days
- C. Oral azithromycin as a single dose
- D. Repeat the rapid plasma reagin titer in 4 weeks
- E. Penicillin desensitization followed by benzathine penicillin G

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: A healed painless chancre followed by a copper-colored rash on the palms and soles, lymphadenopathy, and a high RPR titer with a positive treponemal test is secondary syphilis. In pregnancy, only penicillin reliably treats the mother and prevents congenital syphilis, so a pregnant woman with an IgE-mediated penicillin allergy (hives and wheezing) should be desensitized and then given benzathine penicillin G.
- **원리**: <b>Why penicillin is irreplaceable in pregnancy</b>: Treponema pallidum crosses the placenta at any stage of pregnancy; untreated secondary syphilis — the stage with the most circulating spirochetes — transmits to the fetus most often, causing stillbirth or congenital syphilis. Penicillin is the only drug proven to treat the fetus as well as the mother. Doxycycline is avoided in pregnancy (fetal tooth and bone effects), macrolides cross the placenta poorly and resistance is common, and ceftriaxone has too little evidence for preventing congenital infection.<br> <b>Why desensitization works</b>: Giving gradually increasing doses over hours lets mast cells bind small amounts of drug-specific IgE without massive degranulation, creating a temporary tolerant state. Treatment must begin while tolerance lasts. Hives and wheezing indicate an IgE-mediated reaction, so a direct challenge is unsafe.<br> <b>After treatment</b>: Expect a Jarisch–Herxheimer reaction (fever, which in pregnancy may trigger contractions), and follow RPR titers for a fourfold decline.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Patient</th><th style="width:38%">Pregnant, penicillin allergy (answer)</th><th>Not pregnant, penicillin allergy (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Early syphilis therapy</td><td><b>Desensitize → benzathine penicillin G</b></td><td>Doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 14 days</td></tr> <tr><td>Reason</td><td>Only penicillin prevents congenital syphilis; doxycycline is contraindicated</td><td>Doxycycline is an acceptable alternative with close follow-up</td></tr> <tr><td>Same rule applies to</td><td>Neurosyphilis (desensitize if allergic)</td><td>—</td></tr> </tbody></table> The deciding fact is the pregnancy — the same allergy in a nonpregnant adult would allow doxycycline.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Doxycycline for 14 days would be correct for early syphilis in a nonpregnant adult with penicillin allergy; it is avoided in pregnancy.
  - (B) Ceftriaxone is an alternative in nonpregnant adults without anaphylaxis, but it is not adequate to prevent congenital syphilis in pregnancy.
  - (C) Single-dose azithromycin is no longer recommended because of macrolide resistance; it would be right only for some other STIs, such as chlamydia.
  - (D) Repeating the titer would be right only after treatment to confirm a fourfold decline — waiting now risks fetal infection.
- **함정**: Choosing the nonpregnant alternative (doxycycline) for a penicillin-allergic pregnant woman — pregnancy requires desensitization and penicillin.

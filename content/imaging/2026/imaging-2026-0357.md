## 문제
A 29-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of sharp chest pain for 2 days. The pain is worse when he lies down or takes a deep breath and improves when he sits forward. He had a sore throat and runny nose 10 days ago. He takes no medications. Temperature is 37.9°C (100.2°F), pulse is 96/min, and blood pressure is 124/78 mm Hg. A high-pitched scratching sound is heard at the left lower sternal border with the patient leaning forward. Jugular venous pressure is normal, and there is no pulsus paradoxus. An ECG shows diffuse concave ST-segment elevation with PR-segment depression. Serum troponin I concentration is within the reference range. Echocardiography shows a trivial pericardial effusion and normal ventricular function. Which of the following is the most appropriate pharmacotherapy?

- A. Intravenous heparin infusion
- B. Intravenous ceftriaxone and vancomycin
- C. Metoprolol and sublingual nitroglycerin
- D. High-dose aspirin plus colchicine
- E. Oral prednisone

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: D

- **정답 핵심**: Positional pleuritic chest pain after a viral illness, a pericardial friction rub, and diffuse concave ST elevation with PR depression establish acute pericarditis. Normal troponin, normal ventricular function, normal jugular venous pressure, and no pulsus paradoxus exclude myocarditis and tamponade. First-line therapy is a high-dose NSAID or aspirin plus colchicine for 3 months, which shortens symptoms and halves the recurrence rate.
- **원리**: <b>Why the pain and ECG look this way</b>: inflamed visceral and parietal pericardium rub against each other and irritate the phrenic nerve territory, so the pain is sharp, worse supine or with inspiration, and relieved by leaning forward. Inflammation of the subepicardial myocardium produces ST elevation in many leads that is concave and does not follow one coronary territory; atrial injury produces PR depression.<br> <b>Why aspirin plus colchicine</b>: NSAIDs (or high-dose aspirin) treat the pain and inflammation. Colchicine inhibits microtubule polymerization in neutrophils and blocks NLRP3 inflammasome activation; added for 3 months it reduces incessant and recurrent pericarditis by about half.<br> <b>Why not glucocorticoids first</b>: steroids relieve symptoms quickly but increase recurrence, probably by allowing viral persistence and causing rebound on taper. They are reserved for contraindication to NSAIDs, autoimmune or uremic causes, or pregnancy.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Aspirin/NSAID + colchicine (answer)</th><th>Prednisone (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Use</td><td>First line for viral/idiopathic pericarditis</td><td>Second line — NSAID contraindication, autoimmune disease, pregnancy</td></tr> <tr><td>Recurrence</td><td><b>Reduced</b> (colchicine halves it)</td><td>Increased, especially at high dose</td></tr> <tr><td>Duration</td><td>NSAID 1-2 weeks with taper; colchicine 3 months</td><td>Slow taper over weeks to months</td></tr> </tbody></table> If the patient had lupus or could not take NSAIDs, low-dose prednisone plus colchicine would be chosen.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Heparin would be correct for an acute coronary syndrome with ischemic ST elevation in one territory and rising troponin; in pericarditis it risks hemopericardium.
  - (B) Ceftriaxone and vancomycin would be correct for purulent bacterial pericarditis with high fever, sepsis, and a large effusion showing pus on pericardiocentesis.
  - (C) Metoprolol and nitroglycerin would be correct for ischemic chest pain from coronary disease, which is pressure-like and does not change with position.
  - (E) Prednisone would be correct for pericarditis due to systemic lupus erythematosus or when NSAIDs are contraindicated, such as in severe renal disease or active peptic ulcer.
- **함정**: Seeing ST elevation and treating a myocardial infarction — the elevation is diffuse and concave with PR depression, and troponin is normal.
- **학습목표**: 자세에 따라 변하는 흉막성 흉통·마찰음·광범위 ST 상승과 PR 하강으로 급성 특발성 심낭염을 진단하고, 아스피린(NSAID)과 콜히친을 1차로 쓴다

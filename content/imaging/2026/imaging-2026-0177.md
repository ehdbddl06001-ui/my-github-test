## 문제
A 52-year-old man comes to the physician for evaluation of hypertension that is poorly controlled despite amlodipine, lisinopril, and chlorthalidone at maximum doses. He has had muscle cramps for 6 months. He wishes to pursue a surgical cure if possible. His pulse is 74/min and blood pressure is 164/98 mm Hg. Serum potassium concentration is 3.0 mEq/L. After spironolactone-free preparation and potassium repletion, the plasma aldosterone concentration is 28 ng/dL with suppressed plasma renin activity, and aldosterone remains 14 ng/dL after an intravenous saline infusion test. Adrenal-protocol CT shows a 1.2-cm low-attenuation nodule in the left adrenal gland and a normal right adrenal gland. Serum cortisol after a 1-mg overnight dexamethasone test is 1.2 μg/dL. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Repeat CT in 12 months
- B. Iodocholesterol (NP-59) scintigraphy
- C. Adrenal vein sampling
- D. Left laparoscopic adrenalectomy
- E. Spironolactone therapy without further testing

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: C

- **정답 핵심**: Hypokalemic resistant hypertension with a high aldosterone and suppressed renin that fails to suppress with saline confirms primary aldosteronism. He wants surgery, so lateralization must be proved. In patients older than about 35 years, small nonfunctioning adrenal incidentalomas are common and CT misclassifies the source in roughly a third of cases; bilateral hyperplasia or a contralateral microadenoma may be the true cause. Adrenal vein sampling is the recommended test before adrenalectomy.
- **원리**: <b>The diagnostic sequence has three steps</b>: screening (aldosterone-to-renin ratio), confirmation (saline infusion, oral salt loading, captopril, or fludrocortisone suppression — autonomous aldosterone does not fall), and <b>subtype</b> — unilateral aldosterone-producing adenoma, which surgery cures, versus bilateral adrenal hyperplasia, which is treated with a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist.<br> <b>Why CT is not enough</b>: CT shows anatomy, not function. Nonfunctioning adrenal nodules become common after middle age, and aldosterone-producing adenomas are often under 1 cm and invisible. So a visible left nodule may be an incidental bystander while the aldosterone comes from both glands or from the right. Operating on CT alone would remove the wrong gland or fail to cure in a substantial minority.<br> <b>How AVS works</b>: catheters sample blood from both adrenal veins and a peripheral vein. Cortisol confirms correct placement (selectivity index), and the <b>aldosterone/cortisol ratio</b> on one side more than about 4 times the other side indicates lateralization. Guidelines allow skipping AVS only in young patients (under 35) with marked hypokalemia, very high aldosterone, and a clear unilateral adenoma on CT.<br> The normal dexamethasone suppression test (cortisol ≤ 1.8 μg/dL) excludes cortisol co-secretion, which would otherwise complicate perioperative care.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Repeat imaging is the follow-up for a nonfunctioning incidentaloma with benign features. This patient has confirmed hormonal excess that needs subtype evaluation now, not surveillance.
  - (B) NP-59 scintigraphy has low sensitivity for small aldosterone-producing adenomas and is not widely available; it is not recommended for lateralization. Adrenal vein sampling is the reference standard, and scintigraphy would be considered only where AVS is impossible.
  - (D) Adrenalectomy on the basis of CT alone is acceptable only for patients younger than about 35 with florid primary aldosteronism and a clear unilateral adenoma. At 52 years, the nodule may be nonfunctioning, and surgery could fail or remove the wrong gland.
  - (E) A mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist is the treatment for bilateral hyperplasia or for patients who do not want or cannot undergo surgery. He wants a surgical cure, so lateralization testing comes first; this would be correct if he declined surgery.
- **함정**: A visible adrenal nodule tempts you to operate; after age 35, prove lateralization by adrenal vein sampling first.

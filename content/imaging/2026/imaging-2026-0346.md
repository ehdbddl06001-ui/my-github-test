## 문제
A 72-year-old woman comes to the emergency department because of a new right-sided headache for 3 weeks and two episodes of transient blurred vision in the right eye, each lasting about 5 minutes, during the past day. She has pain in her jaw when chewing that resolves with rest and has had morning stiffness of the shoulders and hips. She has hypertension treated with amlodipine. Temperature is 37.8°C (100°F), pulse is 84/min, and blood pressure is 142/84 mm Hg. The right temporal artery is tender and nodular with a decreased pulse. Visual acuity is 20/25 in both eyes, and funduscopic examination shows no abnormalities. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate is 92 mm/h, and serum C-reactive protein concentration is 6.8 mg/dL (N <0.5). Hemoglobin concentration is 10.9 g/dL, and platelet count is 486,000/mm3. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Perform temporal artery biopsy before starting treatment
- B. Begin low-dose prednisone, 15 mg daily
- C. Begin aspirin therapy alone
- D. Obtain MRI of the brain before starting treatment
- E. Begin high-dose glucocorticoid therapy immediately

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: E

- **정답 핵심**: New headache after age 50, jaw claudication, polymyalgia symptoms, a tender nodular temporal artery, ESR 92 mm/h, anemia, and thrombocytosis indicate giant cell arteritis. Transient monocular blurred vision signals ischemia of the posterior ciliary circulation and threatens permanent blindness. High-dose glucocorticoids (often IV methylprednisolone with visual symptoms) should start immediately; temporal artery biopsy is still done, but within about 2 weeks the result remains diagnostic despite treatment.
- **원리**: <b>Why vision is at risk</b>: giant cell arteritis is a granulomatous vasculitis of large and medium arteries with internal elastic lamina, especially branches of the external carotid and the ophthalmic artery. Inflammation thickens the intima and narrows the lumen. Occlusion of the posterior ciliary arteries causes arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, and once vision is lost it rarely returns — transient blurring or amaurosis fugax is the warning sign.<br> <b>Why treatment comes before the biopsy</b>: glucocorticoids rapidly suppress the inflammation and prevent new ischemic events, while the granulomatous changes in the artery wall persist for at least 2 weeks after starting treatment, so the biopsy remains informative. Waiting for the biopsy risks blindness in the other or the same eye.<br> <b>Why the dose matters</b>: polymyalgia rheumatica alone responds to 12.5–25 mg of prednisone, but vasculitis of cranial arteries needs 40–60 mg (or 1 mg/kg), and with visual symptoms many give IV methylprednisolone 500–1000 mg daily for 3 days.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Biopsy before treatment would be reasonable only if suspicion were low and there were no visual or ischemic symptoms, and even then treatment should not be delayed for long.
  - (B) Prednisone 15 mg daily is the dose for isolated polymyalgia rheumatica without headache, jaw claudication, or visual symptoms.
  - (C) Aspirin alone would be wrong here; low-dose aspirin may be added for cardiovascular risk, but it does not control the vasculitis.
  - (D) Brain MRI would be the next step if the transient visual loss were binocular or accompanied by focal neurologic deficits suggesting stroke rather than arteritis.
- **함정**: Delaying steroids to get a 'clean' biopsy — granulomatous inflammation persists for weeks, while vision loss can happen within days.
- **학습목표**: 거대세포동맥염에서 일과성 시력 저하가 있으면 측두동맥 생검을 기다리지 않고 즉시 고용량 글루코코르티코이드를 시작한다

## 문제
A 46-year-old woman comes to the physician for follow-up of hypertension diagnosed 2 years ago. Her blood pressure remains above 150/95 mm Hg despite taking amlodipine, losartan, and hydrochlorothiazide at maximal doses. She has no headache, palpitations, or sweating. Body mass index is 25 kg/m2. Blood pressure is 156/98 mm Hg. Examination shows no cushingoid features. Serum potassium is 3.6 mEq/L, and creatinine is 0.7 mg/dL. Morning plasma aldosterone concentration is 16 ng/dL, and plasma renin activity is 0.4 ng/mL/h (aldosterone-to-renin ratio, 40). The tests were repeated after correcting potassium with similar results. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in diagnosis?

- A. Saline infusion suppression test
- B. CT scan of the adrenal glands
- C. Adrenal venous sampling
- D. Overnight 1-mg dexamethasone suppression test
- E. 24-hour urine metanephrines

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: Resistant hypertension with a high aldosterone-to-renin ratio (40, with aldosterone 16 ng/dL and suppressed renin) is a positive screen for primary aldosteronism. Because she lacks the combination that makes confirmation unnecessary (spontaneous hypokalemia, undetectable renin, and aldosterone > 20 ng/dL), autonomous aldosterone secretion must first be confirmed with a suppression test such as saline infusion; imaging and venous sampling come only after confirmation.
- **원리**: <b>Why screening is not diagnosis</b>: the aldosterone-to-renin ratio rises whenever renin is low, even if aldosterone is only modestly elevated — and many drugs, age, and salt intake shift renin. A ratio is therefore sensitive but not specific.<br> <b>What confirmation proves</b>: normal adrenal glomerulosa is controlled by angiotensin II; volume loading (2 L saline over 4 hours) suppresses renin and should drop aldosterone below about 5–6 ng/dL. Aldosterone that stays above 10 ng/dL is autonomous — primary aldosteronism. Oral salt loading, fludrocortisone, and captopril tests use the same logic.<br> <b>Why the order matters</b>: adrenal incidentalomas are common in middle age, so a CT nodule does not prove the source; and bilateral hyperplasia, treated with mineralocorticoid antagonists, cannot be distinguished from a unilateral adenoma on CT. Hence confirm → CT (to exclude carcinoma and map anatomy) → adrenal venous sampling to lateralize before surgery.

- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Adrenal CT is correct after primary aldosteronism is confirmed (or confirmation is waived by hypokalemia, undetectable renin, and aldosterone > 20 ng/dL).
  - (C) Adrenal venous sampling is correct after confirmation and CT in a surgical candidate, to decide whether aldosterone excess is unilateral.
  - (D) The 1-mg dexamethasone suppression test is correct for suspected Cushing syndrome or for evaluating an adrenal incidentaloma for cortisol excess; she has no cushingoid features.
  - (E) Urine metanephrines are correct when episodic headache, palpitations, and sweating suggest pheochromocytoma; she has none of these.
- **함정**: Jumping to adrenal CT after a positive ratio — without confirmation, a nonfunctioning incidentaloma could send a patient with bilateral hyperplasia to surgery.
- **학습목표**: 알도스테론/레닌 비가 높은 고혈압 환자에서 자발 저칼륨혈증·알도스테론 > 20 ng/dL 같은 확정 조건을 갖추지 않았다면 확진 검사(식염수 부하)를 먼저 하고, 그 뒤 부신 CT·부신정맥채혈로 간다

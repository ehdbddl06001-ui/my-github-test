## 문제
A 66-year-old man is brought to the emergency department 90 minutes after the sudden onset of weakness of the right arm and difficulty speaking. He has hypertension and type 2 diabetes mellitus. He takes amlodipine and metformin and does not take anticoagulants. His blood pressure is 204/116 mm Hg on two measurements 10 minutes apart, and pulse is 84/min and regular. He has expressive aphasia, a right facial droop, and weakness of the right arm (2/5). The National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score is 9. Fingerstick glucose is 146 mg/dL, and platelet count is 240,000/mm3. A CT scan of the head without contrast shows no hemorrhage or early ischemic changes. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

- A. Aspirin and permissive hypertension up to 220/120 mm Hg
- B. Sublingual nifedipine to lower blood pressure below 140/90 mm Hg
- C. Intravenous heparin infusion and observation
- D. Intravenous labetalol, then alteplase once blood pressure is below 185/110 mm Hg
- E. Intravenous alteplase now without lowering blood pressure

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: D

- **정답 핵심**: He has an acute ischemic stroke within 4.5 hours with a disabling deficit and no hemorrhage on CT, so he is a candidate for intravenous thrombolysis. Blood pressure above 185/110 mm Hg raises the risk of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage and must be lowered first, with intravenous labetalol or nicardipine, before alteplase is given.
- **원리**: <b>Why the threshold exists</b>: after ischemia the blood-brain barrier in the infarct core is damaged. Alteplase converts plasminogen to plasmin systemically, and with high perfusion pressure, blood leaks through the injured vessels — symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation. The trials that proved benefit enrolled only patients whose pressure was below 185/110 mm Hg, so this is the limit for safe treatment.<br> <b>Why not lower it a lot</b>: the ischemic penumbra has lost autoregulation, and its flow depends passively on systemic pressure. Dropping pressure abruptly (e.g., sublingual nifedipine) shrinks penumbral perfusion and can enlarge the infarct. Titratable intravenous agents — labetalol, nicardipine, clevidipine — lower it just below 185/110.<br> <b>After treatment</b>: keep the pressure below 180/105 mm Hg for 24 hours. For patients who are not thrombolysed, pressure is left alone unless it exceeds 220/120 mm Hg (permissive hypertension).
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Lower BP, then alteplase (answer)</th><th>Alteplase now (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>BP before thrombolysis</td><td><b>Must be &lt; 185/110 mm Hg</b></td><td>Ignores the limit — 204/116 mm Hg</td></tr> <tr><td>Risk addressed</td><td>Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage</td><td>Delay to treatment</td></tr> <tr><td>Agents</td><td>IV labetalol, nicardipine, clevidipine</td><td>—</td></tr> </tbody></table> Both aim at early reperfusion; the difference is a few minutes of titration that keeps the hemorrhage risk within what the trials showed. If the pressure cannot be brought below 185/110 mm Hg, alteplase is not given.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Aspirin with permissive hypertension up to 220/120 mm Hg is the approach for patients who are not candidates for thrombolysis, such as those presenting after 4.5 hours.
  - (B) Sublingual nifedipine lowers pressure unpredictably and steeply; a target of 140/90 mm Hg would reduce penumbral perfusion and is not used acutely.
  - (C) Heparin infusion would be considered for selected conditions such as cerebral venous thrombosis; it has no role as initial therapy for acute arterial stroke.
  - (E) Alteplase without lowering blood pressure would be correct only if the pressure were already below 185/110 mm Hg on repeated measurements.
- **함정**: Giving alteplase immediately because 'time is brain' — blood pressure above 185/110 mm Hg must be lowered first.
- **학습목표**: 정맥 혈전용해 대상인 급성 허혈뇌졸중에서 혈압이 185/110 mm Hg 를 넘으면 투여 전에 labetalol·nicardipine 으로 그 아래로 낮추고, 투여 뒤 24시간은 180/105 미만으로 유지한다

## 문제
A 45-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of yellow eyes, fatigue, and abdominal swelling for 2 weeks. He has consumed about 10 cans of beer daily for 15 years, with his last drink 5 days ago. Temperature is 37.9°C (100.2°F), pulse is 102/min, and blood pressure is 118/70 mm Hg. He has jaundice, tender hepatomegaly, and mild ascites. Serum studies show total bilirubin 14.2 mg/dL, AST 210 U/L, ALT 82 U/L, and prothrombin time 9 seconds above control. The leukocyte count is 15,000/mm3. Diagnostic paracentesis shows 80 neutrophils/mm3, and blood and urine cultures are negative. There is no evidence of gastrointestinal bleeding. His Maddrey discriminant function is 58. In addition to abstinence and nutritional support, which of the following is the most appropriate pharmacotherapy?

- A. Prednisolone
- B. Pentoxifylline
- C. Ceftriaxone
- D. Lactulose
- E. Propranolol

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: A

- **정답 핵심**: Recent heavy drinking, jaundice with <b>bilirubin above 3 mg/dL</b>, <b>AST about twice ALT and below 400 U/L</b>, tender hepatomegaly, low-grade fever, and leukocytosis indicate <b>alcohol-associated hepatitis</b>. A <b>Maddrey discriminant function ≥ 32</b> marks severe disease with high short-term mortality. With infection excluded (ascitic neutrophils < 250/mm3, negative cultures) and no bleeding, <b>prednisolone</b> is indicated.
- **원리**: <b>Why a steroid</b>: In alcohol-associated hepatitis, injured hepatocytes and gut-derived endotoxin activate Kupffer cells and neutrophils, which release cytokines (TNF-α, IL-8) that cause further hepatocyte death and liver failure. Corticosteroids suppress this inflammatory amplification. In the STOPAH trial and meta-analyses, prednisolone reduced 28-day mortality in severe disease, though the benefit fades by 90 days.<br> <b>Why severity scoring first</b>: Mild disease recovers with abstinence alone, so steroid risks (infection, bleeding) are not worth it. The discriminant function [4.6 × (PT − control) + bilirubin] ≥ 32 or MELD > 20 identifies patients who benefit.<br> <b>Why exclude infection first</b>: Steroids worsen infection; SBP, bacteremia, and active bleeding must be ruled out. A Lille score at day 7 identifies nonresponders, in whom steroids are stopped.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:24%">Feature</th><th style="width:38%">Prednisolone (answer)</th><th>Pentoxifylline (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Mechanism</td><td>Broad suppression of inflammation</td><td>Phosphodiesterase inhibitor, reduces TNF-α</td></tr> <tr><td>Evidence</td><td><b>Reduces 28-day mortality</b> (DF ≥ 32)</td><td>No mortality benefit in STOPAH</td></tr> <tr><td>Current role</td><td>First-line in severe disease</td><td>Not recommended</td></tr> </tbody></table> Score severity first; treat severe disease with prednisolone once infection and bleeding are excluded.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (B) Pentoxifylline was used when steroids were contraindicated, but large trials showed no survival benefit, so it is no longer recommended.
  - (C) Ceftriaxone would be correct if paracentesis showed ≥ 250 neutrophils/mm3 (spontaneous bacterial peritonitis) or cultures were positive.
  - (D) Lactulose would be correct if he had hepatic encephalopathy with confusion or asterixis, which this patient does not have.
  - (E) Propranolol would be used for primary prophylaxis of variceal bleeding in cirrhosis with large varices, not for acute hepatitis.
- **함정**: Choosing antibiotics because of fever and leukocytosis — both are common in alcohol-associated hepatitis itself, and the workup here excludes infection.
- **학습목표**: Treat severe alcohol-associated hepatitis (Maddrey discriminant function ≥ 32) with prednisolone after excluding infection and gastrointestinal bleeding

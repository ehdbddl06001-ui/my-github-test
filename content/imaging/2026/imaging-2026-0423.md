## 문제
A 64-year-old man with end-stage kidney disease who receives hemodialysis through a tunneled right internal jugular catheter comes to the emergency department because of fever and shaking chills that began during dialysis yesterday. He has no allergies. His temperature is 39.1°C (102.4°F), pulse is 108/min, and blood pressure is 112/68 mm Hg. There is erythema and tenderness at the catheter exit site. Cardiac examination shows no murmurs. Blood cultures are drawn, and intravenous vancomycin is started. Two days later, both sets of blood cultures grow Staphylococcus aureus susceptible to oxacillin, cefazolin, and vancomycin (vancomycin MIC 1 µg/mL). The catheter has been removed, and repeat blood cultures have been sent. Which of the following is the most appropriate change in antibiotic therapy?

- A. Add gentamicin to vancomycin
- B. Switch vancomycin to daptomycin
- C. Switch vancomycin to oral linezolid
- D. Switch vancomycin to cefazolin
- E. Continue vancomycin at the current dose

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: D

- **정답 핵심**: The bloodstream isolate is methicillin-susceptible S. aureus. For MSSA bacteremia, a β-lactam — cefazolin or an antistaphylococcal penicillin such as nafcillin — kills faster and is associated with lower mortality and relapse than vancomycin, so vancomycin should be switched to cefazolin. Cefazolin is also convenient for hemodialysis patients because it can be given after each session.
- **원리**: <b>Why β-lactams beat vancomycin for MSSA</b>: β-lactams bind penicillin-binding proteins and are rapidly bactericidal against susceptible S. aureus. Vancomycin is a large glycopeptide that binds the D-Ala-D-Ala terminus; it penetrates tissue poorly and kills slowly, and observational studies and meta-analyses show higher failure, relapse, and death when MSSA bacteremia is treated with vancomycin alone.<br> <b>Why vancomycin is started first</b>: until susceptibility is known, MRSA must be covered, so vancomycin is the right empirical drug — the error is not switching once the isolate is reported susceptible.<br> <b>The rest of S. aureus bacteremia care</b>: remove the infected device, repeat blood cultures until negative, obtain echocardiography (TEE for most patients with risk factors such as a hemodialysis catheter), and treat for at least 14 days from the first negative culture if uncomplicated, or 4–6 weeks if complicated (endocarditis, persistent bacteremia, metastatic infection).<br> <b>Why gentamicin is no longer added</b>: synergy did not improve outcomes and caused kidney injury.
- **비교**: <table><thead><tr><th style="width:22%">Feature</th><th style="width:39%">Cefazolin (answer)</th><th>Continue vancomycin (closest distractor)</th></tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Isolate</td><td><b>Oxacillin-susceptible (MSSA)</b></td><td>Methicillin-resistant (MRSA)</td></tr> <tr><td>Killing</td><td>Rapid</td><td>Slow</td></tr> <tr><td>Outcomes in MSSA</td><td>Lower mortality and relapse</td><td>Higher failure</td></tr> <tr><td>Allergy</td><td>No serious β-lactam allergy</td><td>Severe β-lactam allergy</td></tr> </tbody></table> Empirical vancomycin is right on day 0; on the day susceptibility returns as MSSA, switch to a β-lactam.
- **오답 이유**:
  - (A) Adding gentamicin was once used for synergy but increases nephrotoxicity without improving survival; it is not recommended for bacteremia.
  - (B) Daptomycin is an alternative for MRSA bacteremia or vancomycin failure with a high MIC; it is not preferred over a β-lactam for MSSA.
  - (C) Oral linezolid is bacteriostatic against S. aureus and is not standard initial therapy for bacteremia; it may fit MRSA pneumonia or skin infection.
  - (E) Continuing vancomycin is correct if the isolate is MRSA or if the patient has a severe β-lactam allergy that precludes cefazolin.
- **함정**: Continuing the empirical vancomycin because it 'covers' the isolate — for MSSA, a β-lactam is more effective, so de-escalate.
- **학습목표**: 메티실린 감수성 황색포도알균 균혈증에서 경험적 반코마이신을 세파졸린(또는 항포도알균 페니실린)으로 바꾸고 감염원 도관을 제거한다

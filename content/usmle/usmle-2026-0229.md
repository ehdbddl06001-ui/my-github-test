## 문제
A 38-year-old man comes to the emergency department because he wants help to stop using heroin. He has injected heroin daily for 4 years and last injected 18 hours ago. He has tried to stop on his own 3 times but used again within days because of withdrawal symptoms and cravings. He has had 2 nonfatal overdoses in the past year. He drinks alcohol rarely and does not use benzodiazepines or other drugs. Two years ago, he was told after an ECG at a preemployment examination that he has a long QT interval of unknown cause; he has never fainted, and he takes no medications. He wants to begin treatment today. His vital signs are shown. He is restless and yawning, with rhinorrhea, lacrimation, piloerection and pupils 6 mm in diameter. There are injection marks on both forearms without erythema or fluctuance. His Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale score is 15, consistent with moderate withdrawal. Laboratory and ECG results are shown. Which of the following is the most appropriate pharmacotherapy to begin today?

- A. Methadone maintenance
- B. Buprenorphine-naloxone maintenance
- C. Extended-release naltrexone injection
- D. Clonidine and loperamide taper
- E. Oral naltrexone maintenance

## 정답 및 해설
> 정답: B

- **정답근거**: 3단계 추론. ① 매일 주사 + 반복 중단 실패·갈망 + 과다복용 2회 → **중등도~중증 아편유사제 사용장애 → 유지 약물치료**. ② QTc 518 msec(재검 동일) → **메타돈 시작 회피**(hERG 차단·torsades). ③ 마지막 사용 18시간 + COWS 15 중등도 금단 → **부프레노르핀-날록손을 지금 유도**(날트렉손은 7~10일 무사용 필요).
- **오답감별**:
  - (A) 메타돈은 1차 유지 약이지만 QT 를 용량 의존적으로 늘린다 — QTc 518 msec 에서는 시작하지 않는다.
  - (C) 장기 지속형 날트렉손은 7~10일 아편유사제 무사용 뒤에만 시작한다 — 18시간 전 사용한 환자에게 주면 침전 금단이 온다.
  - (D) 클로니딘·로페라마이드는 금단 증상만 줄인다 — 유지 치료 없이 금단만 관리하면 내성이 떨어진 채 재발해 과다복용 위험이 오히려 높아진다.
  - (E) 경구 날트렉손도 같은 이유로 오늘 시작할 수 없고, 복약 순응도가 낮아 사용장애 유지 치료로서 효과가 제한적이다.
- **임상핵심**: **아편유사제 사용장애는 유지 약물치료 — QTc > 500 msec 면 메타돈 대신 부프레노르핀, 중등도 금단이 된 뒤 유도하고, 날트렉손은 7~10일 무사용 뒤에.**
- **출처**: ASAM National Practice Guideline for the Treatment of Opioid Use Disorder: 2020 Focused Update; Martin JA, et al. QT interval screening in methadone maintenance treatment (SAMHSA expert panel, J Addict Dis 2011).
